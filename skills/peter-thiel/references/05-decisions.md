# 05-decisions.md — Peter Thiel's Key Decisions

---

## 1. Co-Founding The Stanford Review (1987)

**Context:** Stanford replaced its Western Culture program with a multiculturalism-focused CIV course. Thiel was outraged.

**The Decision:** Co-founded The Stanford Review, a conservative and libertarian newspaper.

**The Logic:**
- The university had abandoned its mission of teaching critical thinking
- Multiculturalism was enforcing conformity under the guise of diversity
- A student newspaper could fight back against the prevailing intellectual mood
- Got funding from Irving Kristol, the neoconservative intellectual

**The Result:**
- Thiel was first editor-in-chief (1987-1989)
- The Review is still publishing today
- Spawned a network of ~300 people who went into tech, politics, and media
- Established Thiel's contrarian voice before he ever started a company

---

## 2. Writing The Diversity Myth (1995)

**Context:** Thiel was a young lawyer at Sullivan & Cromwell, disillusioned with the legal profession.

**The Decision:** Co-authored The Diversity Myth with David Sacks — a critique of Stanford's multiculturalism.

**The Logic:**
- The book extended his Stanford Review activism into a full-length argument
- Wanted to document how intellectual conformity was being enforced on campus
- Established himself as a public intellectual before his tech career

**The Result:**
- Published by the Independent Institute
- Established Thiel's reputation as a contrarian thinker
- Laid the philosophical groundwork for his later views on education, competition, and innovation

---

## 3. Teaching CS183 at Stanford (2012)

**Context:** Thiel was already a successful investor. Stanford asked him to teach a class on startups.

**The Decision:** Agreed to teach CS183 — a 20-lecture course — and allowed Blake Masters to publish detailed notes online.

**The Logic:**
- Wanted to codify his thinking on startups and technology
- Teaching forces clarity — you can't teach what you don't understand
- The notes served as a test run for Zero to One

**The Result:**
- The CS183 notes went viral (millions of readers)
- Became the basis for Zero to One (2014), one of the most influential business books of the century
- The notes remain widely read and cited

## 4. Co-Founding PayPal (1998)

**Context:** Online payments were fragmented and insecure. Credit card companies dominated.

**The Decision:** Co-founded Confinity (later merged with Elon Musk's X.com to become PayPal) with Max Levchin and Luke Nosek.

**The Logic:**
- Saw that the internet needed a native payment system
- Focused on the specific pain point of sending money via Palm Pilot
- Shifted strategy rapidly when they realized email-based payments worked better

**The Result:**
- Sold to eBay in 2002 for $1.5 billion
- Thiel personally made ~$55 million
- The "PayPal Mafia" alumni went on to found Tesla, LinkedIn, YouTube, Yelp, Palantir, SpaceX

## 2. First Outside Investor in Facebook (2004)

**Context:** Facebook was a Harvard-only social network. Most investors thought it was a fad.

**The Decision:** Invested $500,000 for 10.2% of Facebook.

**The Logic:**
- Recognized the network effects and the "small market within a small market" strategy
- Facebook started at Harvard, expanded to other Ivy League schools, then all colleges
- The contrarian bet: most people thought social networks were already won by MySpace and Friendster
- "The perfect target market for a startup is a small group of particular people"

**The Result:**
- That $500,000 stake grew to be worth billions
- One of the most successful venture investments of all time
- Thiel sold most of his stake over time for billions

---

## 3. Founding Palantir (2003)

**Context:** Post-9/11, government intelligence agencies needed better data analysis tools.

**The Decision:** Founded Palantir Technologies with Alex Karp, Stephen Cohen, and others.

**The Logic:**
- Identified a "secret" that government data analysis was broken
- PayPal's fraud detection technology could be applied to intelligence problems
- Insisted on building for both government and commercial applications
- Focused on human-machine symbiosis rather than pure AI

**The Result:**
- Palantir went public in 2020 (direct listing)
- Current market cap: ~$50B+
- Thiel remains chairman
- The company is the most important data analytics firm for US government and allies

---

## 4. Founding Founders Fund (2005)

**Context:** After PayPal, Thiel saw a gap in venture capital for truly ambitious technology.

**The Decision:** Launched Founders Fund with Ken Howery and Luke Nosek.

**The Logic:**
- Conventional VC was investing in incremental improvements (1 to n)
- Founders Fund would bet on "zero to one" companies
- Backed founders who were thinking about the hard problems
- Concentrated bets on a few companies (power law approach)

**Key Investments:**
- SpaceX
- Airbnb
- Stripe
- Anduril Technologies
- DeepMind (pre-Google acquisition)
- Spotify

---

## 5. The Thiel Fellowship (2011)

**Context:** The cost of higher education was skyrocketing. Many talented young people were wasting time in classrooms.

**The Decision:** Launched the Thiel Fellowship — $100,000 to students under 20 to drop out and start companies.

**The Logic:**
- "College is the most expensive bubble in America"
- The best way to learn is by doing, not by studying
- Many great entrepreneurs don't need degrees (Zuckerberg, Gates, Jobs)
- Remove the safety net to force real ambition

**Controversy:**
- Heavily criticized by educators and parents
- Called "anti-intellectual" and dangerous
- But produced successful companies and founders

## 6. The Gawker Lawsuit (2012-2016)

**Context:** Gawker Media outed Thiel as gay in 2007. He waited years.

**The Decision:** Secretly funded Hulk Hogan's lawsuit against Gawker for publishing a sex tape.

**The Logic:**
- Thiel was offended by Gawker's pattern of destroying innocent people's lives
- Wanted to send a message that there are consequences for such behavior
- "It's less about revenge and more about specific deterrence"
- Funded the case secretly to avoid affecting the legal process

**The Result:**
- Hogan won $140 million judgment
- Gawker filed for bankruptcy and was sold
- Massive debate about press freedom vs. accountability
- Thiel acknowledged funding the lawsuit publicly (2016)

---

## 7. Political Activism (2016-2024)

**Context:** Thiel saw the Republican party and American politics as failing.

**The Decision:** Became the most prominent Silicon Valley supporter of Donald Trump.

**The Logic:**
- Saw Trump as a "builder" who could disrupt the political establishment
- Influencing politics at scale requires engaging with it
- The "regime question" — what kind of political system enables innovation?
- Supported JD Vance (Thiel's former employee at Mithril Capital) for Senate

**The Result:**
- Spoke at the 2016 RNC
- Influenced Trump administration tech positions
- Later became more critical of Trump
- JD Vance became a US Senator (and later VP pick)
- Paid legal settlement to support conservative causes

---

## 8. Investment in Anduril Industries (2017)

**Context:** The defense industry was dominated by old, slow contractors.

**The Decision:** Founders Fund led investment in Anduril (named after Aragorn's sword) — a defense tech startup.

**The Logic:**
- Defense technology needed a "zero to one" approach
- Silicon Valley had abandoned the defense sector
- Software-defined warfare would be the future
- "We wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters" — time to build again

**The Result:**
- Anduril grew to be a major defense contractor
- Valued at billions
- Spawned a new wave of defense tech startups
