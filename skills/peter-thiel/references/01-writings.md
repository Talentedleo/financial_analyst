## 1. Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future (2014)

**Authors:** Peter Thiel with Blake Masters
**Link:** [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Zero-One-Notes-Startups-Future/dp/0804139296)

The single most important source for understanding Thiel's thinking. Based on CS183 lecture notes at Stanford (Spring 2012).

### Core Thesis: 0 to 1 vs. 1 to n

> "Doing what we already know how to do takes the world from 1 to n, adding more of something familiar. But every time we create something new, we go from 0 to 1."

Going from **1 to n** = copying, horizontal progress, globalization
Going from **0 to 1** = creation, vertical progress, technology

### The Contrarian Question

> "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"

**Framework for answers:**
- Bad answer: "Our education system is broken" (everyone agrees)
- Good answer: "Most people believe in x, but the truth is the opposite of x"

### Monopoly Theory

> "All happy companies are different: each one earns a monopoly by solving a unique problem. All failed companies are the same: they failed to escape competition."

**Characteristics of durable monopolies:**
1. **Proprietary Technology** — at least 10x better than closest substitute
2. **Network Effects** — more valuable as more people use it
3. **Economies of Scale** — stronger as it grows
4. **Branding** — unique, defensible brand identity

### The Seven Questions Every Business Must Answer

1. **Engineering:** Can you create breakthrough technology?
2. **Timing:** Is now the right time to start?
3. **Monopoly:** Are you starting with a big share of a small market?
4. **People:** Do you have the right team?
5. **Distribution:** Can you deliver your product?
6. **Durability:** Will your position be defensible in 10-20 years?
7. **Secret:** Have you identified a unique opportunity others don't see?

### Last Mover Advantage

> "It's much better to be the last mover — that is, to make the last great development in a specific market and enjoy years or even decades of monopoly profits."

Thiel contrasts this with the conventional "first mover advantage" wisdom.

### Power Law in Venture Capital

> "Venture capitalists must find the handful of companies that will go from zero to one and then back them with every resource."

The returns in venture capital follow a power law: a tiny number of companies vastly outperform all others.

## 2. The Diversity Myth: Multiculturalism and the Politics of Intolerance at Stanford (1995)

**Authors:** Peter Thiel and David O. Sacks

Thiel's first book, written while at Stanford. Critiques multiculturalism and the replacement of Western Culture curriculum at Stanford.

**Core argument:** The "Culture, Ideas and Values" (CIV) program represented a politicization of education that undermined intellectual rigor and free inquiry.

## 3. CS183 Stanford Lecture Notes (2012)

**Source:** Blake Masters' notes from Thiel's Stanford CS183 class on startups
**Link:** [Blake Masters Archive](https://blakemasters.com/peter-thiels-cs183-startup)

These notes formed the basis for *Zero to One*. They contain raw, unfiltered versions of Thiel's ideas before editorial polish.

**Key additional insights:**
- The importance of **secrets** — knowledge that is important but not widely believed
- **Definite vs. indefinite optimism** — the future requires definite beliefs about what will happen
- **Founder paradox** — founders are both essential and strange, and the strangeness is the point

## 4. "The Straussian Moment" (2025)

**Source:** Co-authored essay, published in *First Things* magazine
**Link:** [First Things](https://firstthings.com)

Thiel's more recent philosophical writing draws on Leo Strauss and explores themes of:
- The tension between philosophy and politics
- The role of esoteric writing
- Technology and the future of liberal democracy
- The "regime question" in modern politics

## 5. Key Concepts Across All Writings

| Concept | Definition | Key Quote |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 0 to 1 | Creating something entirely new | "Every moment in business happens only once" |
| Monopoly | Dominating a unique market | "Competition is for losers" |
| Secrets | Important truths few believe | "Every great company is built on a secret" |
| Power Law | A few things vastly outperform | "Venture capital returns follow a power law" |
| Definite Optimism | Clear vision of the future | "A definite plan beats no plan" |
| Last Mover | Capturing long-term value | "First mover is a tactic, last mover is strategy" |
| 10x Better | Technology moat threshold | "Anything less is a marginal improvement" |
| Branding | The visible monopoly surface | "A monopoly on its own brand by definition" |

## 6. Thiel's Investment Philosophy

**Source:** Founders Fund and various interviews

- **Non-consensus:** Invest in things that most people think are bad ideas
- **High-conviction:** Concentrate bets on a few companies expected to be massive
- **Founder-centric:** Back exceptional founders, not just good business plans
- **Technology focus:** Invest in companies solving hard tech problems with proprietary advantages
- **Long time horizon:** Think in decades, not quarters

**Founders Fund motto:**
> "We wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters."

## 7. Stanford Review Editorials (1987-1989)

**Context:** As first editor-in-chief of The Stanford Review, Thiel wrote extensively on:
- The politicization of higher education
- The dangers of multiculturalism in academia
- Free speech and intellectual diversity
- The role of Western civilization in education

These editorials established Thiel's contrarian voice and attracted the attention of Irving Kristol (neoconservative intellectual) who funded the paper.

**Notable publications:**

## 9. Other Key Initiatives

### Thiel Fellowship (2011-present)
- Pays students under 20 to drop out and start companies ($100k each)
- "College is the most expensive bubble in America"
- Controversial: criticized as anti-intellectual, but produced successful companies

### Seasteading Institute (2008-2010s)
- Funded research into floating ocean city-states
- Political innovation through geographic experimentation
- "Seasteading" — creating new societies on the water
- Limited practical results but influenced thinking about governance competition

### Life Extension Research
- Significant funding through Thiel Foundation
- Views aging as an engineering problem, not an inevitability
- Supports SENS Research Foundation and other anti-aging research
- "Death is a problem to be solved"
