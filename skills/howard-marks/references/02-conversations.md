## Recent Major Interviews (2024-2026)

### 1. Howard Marks: AI, Debt vs Equity & The Next 40 Years of Investing
**Host:** Nikhil Kamath, "People by WTF"  
**Date:** April 1, 2026  
**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2HIJlD46Kig  
**Transcript:** https://ceointerviews.ai/transcript/883923/

**Conversation Overview:**
A wide-ranging 75-minute interview covering Marks's entire career arc. Nikhil Kamath, an Indian entrepreneur and investor, explores Marks's philosophy on debt vs equity, why he chose bonds for his career, his evolution of thinking on AI, and what the next 40 years might look like.

**Key Excerpts:**
> "The return on the S&P, which averages 10, is almost never between 8 and 12. Why is it that the norm is not the average? Greed and fear, excess and correction, excess of optimism correction excess of pessimism."

> "My answer is that indexation has taken over as it has not because it's so good, but because active management was so bad."

> "You look at bonds and say no upside some downside. I say predictable outcome which is achieved almost every time."

> "The most important one, the most relevant one is something called mujo, which literally means the turning of the wheel of the law. And what it means in everyday life is it means the inevitability of change. That change is inevitable, unpredictable and uncontrollable and that we have to accommodate to it and make the most of it."

**Notable Moments:**
- Discussed why he spent his entire career in bonds instead of equities
- Changed his mind on AI after a single conversation — showing intellectual flexibility
- Explained why the S&P 500's average return is 10% but almost never in the 8-12 range
- Talked about the inevitability of change (referencing mujo, a Japanese Buddhist concept)

**Host:** The Financial Economics  
**Date:** April 12, 2026  
**Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=brj1xQYqB4w

**Conversation Overview:**
A focused discussion on Marks's most profitable strategy — investing during market crises. He explains that the biggest opportunities come when fear and uncertainty peak.

**Key Excerpts:**
> "I've made more money by investing during market crises and downturns than by any other single strategy. When most investors sell in fear, that's exactly when smart investors step in."

- Quality assets at deeply discounted prices appear during crises

### 5. Howard Marks: The Most Powerful Interview of 2025 — Buffett Idol, Munger's System, AI Bubble
**Host:** William Green (author of *Richer, Wiser, Happier*)  
**Date:** December 13, 2025  
**Source:** https://new.qq.com/rain/a/20251215A0566M00

**Conversation Overview:**
A deep 75-minute interview. William Green, who has interviewed many top investors, prepared thoroughly by reviewing Marks's memos. The resulting conversation systematically walks through Marks's complete methodology.

**Key Excerpts (translated from Chinese media coverage):**

*On Buffett:*
> "My idol Warren Buffett often says he jumps out of bed every morning doing a tap dance to work. I truly feel the same way."

*On Munger's System:*
> "What I admire most about [Munger] is not just his talent, but that he turned his talent into a complete system. His brilliance is structured, with methods and frameworks... That's what lasts, and it profoundly influenced Buffett."

*On Partnership with Bruce Karsh:*
> "Bruce and I genuinely acknowledge that the other can do things we cannot. A truly healthy partnership is built on that recognition."

*On Emotional Stability:*
> "'Emotional stability' is one of the most critical qualities I have seen in great investors."

*On AI Bubble:*
> "If I have to find an analogy, the current AI situation most closely resembles the 1998-2000 internet bubble. Although different in degree, the logic is similar."

*On Gold:*
> "If you bought gold at the end of 2010, your annualized return to date would be about 7.7%. The S&P 500's annualized return over the same period was 12.7%. Gold is not a bad investment, but nowhere near good."

*On Index Investing for Most People:*
> "For most people, rather than investing directly, it's better to choose products managed by professional teams — funds, ETFs, etc."

*On Risk Positioning:*
> "The question is: are you trying to make as much money as possible when everything goes right, or lose as little as possible when everything goes wrong? You cannot maximize both."

## Key Themes Across Interviews

Across all his conversations (2024-2026), several themes recur with remarkable consistency:

### 1. AI as the Defining Unknown
The single most discussed topic in 2025-2026 interviews. Marks initially skeptical, evolved to acknowledge AI's transformative potential, but remains cautious about investing:
- "Changing the world does not equal investors making money" (echoing Buffett on the internet, 2000)
- Comparing AI to 1998-2000 internet bubble — same pattern of excitement, same uncertainty about winners
- "Most AI companies will end up worthless" (William Green interview, Dec 2025)

### 2. The Post-Zero-Rate World
Marks's "Sea Change" thesis remains central:
- The era of easy money is over
- Fixed income is investable again
- Investors must adjust return expectations downward
- Active management matters more in a higher-rate environment

### 3. Humility and "I Don't Know"
Consistent across every single interview:
- Rejects certainty in investing
- Emphasizes probabilistic thinking over binary predictions
- Acknowledges the limits of his own forecasting ability
- "The most dangerous investors are those who are certain they are right"

### 4. The Indexing Paradox
- Acknowledges that most people should index
- But argues that indexing has grown not because it is great, but because active management was terrible
- Active management can work — but only with genuine edge

### 5. Emotional Stability as a Superpower
- Identifies emotional stability as the most underrated investing quality
- Frequent advice: "Do nothing" is often the best action
- Patience, not activity, drives long-term results

### 6. China: Cautious Optimism
- Publicly stated "Don't bet China losing" (2025)
- Acknowledges challenges but maintains long-term presence
- Values understanding local market dynamics

### 7. The Buffett-Munger Frame
Every interviewer notes Marks's deep respect for Buffett and Munger:
- Buffett = idol, "tap dancing to work" lifestyle ideal
- Munger = system builder, "turned talent into a structured framework"
- Frequently uses Buffett and Munger stories to illustrate his own points
