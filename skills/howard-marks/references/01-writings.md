# 01 — Writings

Howard Marks's written body of work spans three books and over 30 years of investment memos distributed to Oaktree Capital clients. His writing is valued for its clarity, philosophical depth, and practical wisdom. Warren Buffett said: "When I see memos from Howard Marks in my mail, they're the first thing I open and read."

## Books

### The Most Important Thing: Uncommon Sense for the Thoughtful Investor (2011)

**Publisher:** Columbia University Press  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Most-Important-Thing-Thoughtful-Investor/dp/0231153684

Marks's first and most influential book. Distills his 40+ years of investment experience into 20 chapters, each centered on one "most important thing."

**Core Ideas:**
- Second-Level Thinking — going beyond surface-level consensus to understand deeper implications
- The relationship between price and value is the single most important thing in investing
- Risk is the probability of permanent capital loss, not volatility
- Market psychology swings like a pendulum between euphoria and despair
- The three stages of a bull/bear market cycle
- Contrarianism must be grounded in value, not merely being different
- Recognizing what you don't know is more important than knowing

**Key Quotes:**
> "You can't do the same things others do and expect to outperform."

> "The most important thing — above all — is the relationship between price and value."

> "The greatest risk doesn't come from low quality or high volatility. It comes from paying prices that are too high."

> "Investment success doesn't come from buying good things, but from buying things well."

> "There are few things as risky as the widespread belief that there's no risk."

> "Being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong."

> "Good times teach only bad lessons: that investing is easy, that you know its secrets, and that you needn't worry about risk."

### The Most Important Thing Illuminated (2013)

**Publisher:** Columbia University Press  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Most-Important-Thing-Illuminated-Uncommon/dp/0231153692

An annotated edition of the original book, with commentary from notable investors including Seth Klarman, Joel Greenblatt, and Christopher Davis.

**Key Additions:**
- Seth Klarman on silos and competitive advantage
- Joel Greenblatt on the math of negative compounding (a 40% loss requires 67% gain to recover)
- Christopher Davis on flawed incentives driving irrational behavior

### Mastering the Market Cycle: Getting the Odds on Your Side (2018)

**Publisher:** Houghton Mifflin Harcourt  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Mastering-Market-Cycle-Getting-Odds/dp/1328479257

Marks's deep dive into understanding and navigating market cycles. He argues that while cycles are inevitable and unpredictable in timing, investors can—and must—know "where we stand" to position portfolios appropriately.

**Core Ideas:**

The book identifies and examines multiple interrelated cycles:

1. **The Economic Cycle** — expansions and contractions in GDP, employment, and business activity
2. **The Profit Cycle** — corporate earnings follow economic cycles but with their own dynamics
3. **The Investor Psychology Cycle** — the pendulum swings between greed and fear, optimism and pessimism
4. **The Credit Cycle** — the most important cycle for investors; tightening and loosening of credit availability
5. **The Distressed Debt Cycle** — how the credit cycle drives opportunities in distressed assets
6. **The Real Estate Cycle** — property-specific cyclical dynamics
7. **The Market Cycle** — the aggregate of all cycles as reflected in asset prices

**The Credit Cycle Thesis:**
Marks emphasizes that the credit cycle is the most powerful force in markets:
- When the economy is strong, lenders are willing to lend → easy credit → excess → inevitable losses
- Losses make lenders cautious → credit tightens → companies can't refinance → defaults increase
- After enough defaults, fear subsides → lenders return → cycle begins again
- **"The worst loans are made at the best of times."**

**Key Quotes:**
> "The most important thing for investors to recognize is where we stand in the cycle."

> "Success carries the seeds of failure, and failure carries the seeds of success."

> "You can't predict the future, but you can know where we stand."

> "History doesn't repeat itself, but it does rhyme." (quoting Mark Twain)

**Course Correction Concept:**
Marks argues that cycles are not mechanical but are driven by human behavior. The best investors develop a sense of "cycle positioning" — a feel for whether markets are too optimistic or too pessimistic, and position accordingly with a margin of safety.

Marks began writing memos to Oaktree clients in 1990. As of 2026, he has published over 150 memos. They track his thinking through every major market event of the last 35+ years.

#### 2024: "The Folly of Certainty"

**Link:** https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-folly-of-certainty

**Key Takeaways:**
- Argues that certainty in investing is an illusion
- Markets and economies are not governed by fixed cause-and-effect relationships
- The most dangerous investors are those who are certain they are right
- Investors should be humble about what they know and acknowledge what they don't know
- "I don't know" is often the most intelligent answer in investing

#### 2025: "Revisiting the Rejection of Economic Laws"

**Source:** https://new.qq.com/rain/a/20250623A04WW100

**Key Takeaways:**
- Government intervention often has unintended consequences
- Rent control, tariffs, and insurance regulation case studies
- Economic laws cannot be repealed any more than physical laws
- Markets find ways to work around artificial constraints

#### 2025: "Don't Bet Against China" (July)

**Source:** Various financial news outlets

**Key Takeaways:**
- "Don't bet China losing" — echoing Buffett's "don't bet against America"
- Oaktree remains a long-term participant in Chinese markets
- Market timing is extremely difficult; long-term participation is the path to wealth creation

#### 2026: AI as "Autonomous Agent" Discussion (Feb)

**Source:** Chinese financial media coverage of Howard Marks's dialogue with AI

**Key Takeaways:**
- AI is replacing labor, not merely augmenting it — a structural shift
- Comparison to 1998-2000 internet bubble era
- "Changing the world" and "investors making money" are two different things
- Selective positioning and moderate position sizing recommended

## Oaktree Investment Philosophy (Official)

**Link:** https://www.oaktreecapital.com/about/investment-philosophy

Oaktree's formal investment philosophy, shaped by Marks:

1. **Risk Control First** — "If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves"
2. **A Contrarian Approach** — Buy when others are selling, sell when others are buying (but only when valuation supports it)
3. **An Emphasis on Value** — Price matters more than anything
4. **A Long-term Horizon** — Not trading, but investing with patience
5. **Specialization** — Focus on areas where Oaktree has deep expertise
6. **A Bottom-up Approach** — Company-by-company analysis, not top-down macro bets
7. **Avoiding Leverage** — Don't borrow to invest; survive to compound
8. **Asymmetric Return Profile** — Seek to participate in upside while defending against downside
