# 04 — External Views

Critical and external perspectives on Howard Marks's philosophy, methods, and track record. These are included to give the skill balanced awareness of its own limitations.

---

## Criticisms

### 1. Overly Cautious / Left Too Much on the Table
**Criticism:** Marks's emphasis on downside protection means Oaktree often misses major bull markets, especially in equities.

**Context:** Oaktree's core strategy is distressed debt and credit, which by design underperforms in strong bull markets. Marks acknowledges this: "If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves." The trade-off is intentional, but critics note that investors who followed Marks's cautious stance in strong bull markets underperformed simple index funds.

**Source:** Various market commentary comparing Oaktree returns to S&P 500 during bull runs.

### 2. Predictions That Didn't Pan Out
**Criticism:** Despite his emphasis on "nobody knows," Marks has made specific calls that proved wrong.

**Notable Examples:**
- **Gold (2010s):** Marks has been consistently skeptical of gold as an investment, arguing it has no intrinsic value. While his analytical point stands, gold performed well in certain periods.
- **Cryptocurrency (2017):** Marks warned strongly against crypto in his 2017 memo "I Beg to Differ." While many crypto investors lost money, Bitcoin returned over 800% in the years following that memo.
- **AI Skepticism (2023-2024):** Marks was initially dismissive of AI's investment potential, though he later revised his view in 2025-2026 interviews, demonstrating intellectual flexibility.

**Context:** No investor is right 100% of the time. Marks himself admits his success rate on market calls is roughly 5 good calls in 25 years.

### 3. Limited Applicability for Individual Investors
**Criticism:** Marks's philosophy is designed for institutional investors managing large pools of capital, especially in credit markets. Many of his principles—while philosophically sound—are hard for retail investors to implement.

**Specific Issues:**
- Distressed debt is not accessible to most individual investors
- His "don't trade" advice runs counter to his own ability to make tactical shifts
- Institutional resources (research teams, private market access) that inform his views are not available to individuals

### 4. The "Always Cautious" Bias
**Criticism:** Because Oaktree is primarily a credit manager, Marks is structurally biased toward caution and bearishness. This makes his framework less useful for equity-focused growth investors.

**Counterpoint:** Marks's framework of cycle positioning is asset-class agnostic. The same principles can guide when to be aggressive and when to be defensive.

### 5. Overreliance on Memoranda as Validation
**Criticism:** Some argue that Marks's fame is disproportionate to his investment returns. His memos have become more famous than his fund's performance, suggesting his writing skill may outshine his investing skill.

**Counterpoint:** Oaktree has delivered 19% annualized returns (net of fees) since inception—consistently above market benchmarks—and manages $224 billion.

## Key External Analyses

### Comparison to Value Investing Tradition
Marks's framework bridges the gap between:
- **Benjamin Graham** (value investing, margin of safety)
- **John Maynard Keynes** (animal spirits, market psychology)
- **Buffett/Munger** (quality, moats, circle of competence)

His unique contribution is synthesizing these into a practical framework for credit investors.

### Academic Perspective
Academics might critique Marks for:
- Lack of quantitative rigor in his cycle-timing approach
- The difficulty of defining "where we stand in the cycle" objectively
- Survivorship bias in his track record


### Credit vs. Equity: A Fundamental Difference
A critical distinction often missed by those applying Marks's framework to equity markets:

| Dimension | Marks (Credit/Distressed) | Mainstream Value (Equity) |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Return Profile** | Fixed upside (coupon), variable downside (default) | Variable upside (earnings growth), variable downside (loss) |
| **Asymmetry Goal** | Capture the coupon, avoid the default | Buy low, sell high |
| **Edge Source** | Credit analysis, legal/structural protection | Business analysis, competitive advantage |
| **Key Risk** | Default (does the company have cash to pay?) | Overpaying (is the growth priced in?) |
| **Cycle Sensitivity** | Highly sensitive to credit cycle | Moderately sensitive to business cycle |
| **Active Mgmt Value** | Very high (credit markets are less efficient) | Contested (indexing is more viable for equities) |

**Implication:** Many of Marks's principles generalize well (second-level thinking, cycle awareness), but his specific risk framework and tactical recommendations are optimized for credit, not equity. When using this skill, be aware of which context applies.

### Media Coverage
Financial media generally treats Marks as one of the few investors worth listening to, precisely because he doesn't make predictions. CNBC, Bloomberg, and the Financial Times regularly feature his views.
