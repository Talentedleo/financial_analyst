## 1. Hoover Institution — Capitalism and Freedom in the 21st Century (Oct 2025)

**Format:** Podcast interview with Jon Hartley
**Duration:** ~60 minutes
**Link:** [Hoover Institution](https://www.hoover.org/research/cliff-asness-factor-investing-and-history-financial-economics)

**Key Topics:**

### Early Life and Education
- Grew up in Queens, NY, moved to Long Island at age 4
- Not a standout student in high school — "mediocre grades, good boards"
- Father pushed him toward Penn's M&T program (dual engineering + business degree)
- Initially planned to go to law school until his father said "none of us can add — you can do math, do something with math"
- Switched from LSAT to GMAT, ended up at University of Chicago PhD program

### Chicago Years (1988-1994)
- Arrived at Chicago in fall 1988, right when Fama and French were developing their first cross-sectional returns paper
- "My entire career was in some sense a lucky break" — timing of being at Chicago during the birth of factor investing
- Fama and French co-chaired his dissertation
- John Cochrane was on his outside committee
- Merton Miller attended his dissertation proposal — "scary time to meet Merton when you're presenting"
- His dissertation focused on price momentum — initially nervous to tell Fama
- **Key quote:** "I was nervous going to tell Professor Fama that I wanna write a dissertation not only on momentum, but that I was including this result in a fairly prominent way"
- **Fama's response:** "If it's in the data, write the paper"

### From Academia to Goldman Sachs
- Summer job at Goldman Sachs Asset Management in 1991 through his friend John Byner
- Originally planned to be a professor but got "slowly seduced to what you might call the dark side"
- PIMCO read his early JPM paper ("Option-Adjusted Spreads and a Steep Yield Curve") and offered him a job
- He told Goldman about the offer; they said "we're looking to start a group like this"
- Fama was unhappy with his decision: "Why do you wanna stay and be a salesman?"

### On Momentum as a Factor
- Acknowledges Jegadeesh and Titman as the original discoverers of momentum (six months ahead of him)
- "I was probably only about six months behind them, which I will regret forever"
- But claims his version of momentum was "a better formulation"
- **Key insight on factor debates:** "For any factor, there are always two possible explanations — an efficient market story (risk-based) or a market inefficiency explanation (behavioral)"
- Risk-based explanations for momentum are "pretty hard to swallow"

## 2. Bloomberg — Money Stuff: The Podcast (Jun 2025, Rerun Feb 2026)

**Format:** Long-form podcast with Matt Levine and Katie Greifeld
**Link:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2025-06-20/money-stuff-the-podcast-cliff-asness)

**Key Topics:**

### Market Efficiency Debate
- Discusses his "Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" paper
- Argues markets have gotten dumber, not smarter, over the past decade
- The explosion of passive investing, ETFs, and zero-day options has created new inefficiencies
- Systematic active managers have more opportunities than ever

### AQR's Approach
- Emphasizes that AQR is not trying to beat the market on any given day
- The goal is consistent, repeatable alpha through disciplined factor exposure
- Combines multiple signals because no single factor works all the time

### On AI in Investing
- AQR uses machine learning but with skepticism
- "The danger of AI in finance is overfitting — finding patterns that look amazing in backtests and don't work in practice"
- Human judgment remains essential for model design and risk management

## 3. Bloomberg — Open Interest (Nov 2025)

**Format:** Television interview
**Link:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2025-11-10/aqr-s-cliff-asness-says-no-ai-bubble-video)

**Key Topics:**

### "Markets Give Me Some Nerves"
- Asness expressed concern about market concentration and risk-taking behavior
- Not calling a crash, but "the levels of leverage and speculation are concerning"

### No AI Bubble
- Contrasts the current AI enthusiasm with the dot-com bubble
- "AI is real — the technology is transformative. What's uncertain is who the winners will be and at what price."
- Unlike 1999, many AI companies have actual revenue and earnings

### AQR's Positioning
- Maintaining discipline: sticking to factor exposures, not chasing narratives
- Value factor is attractively positioned relative to growth
- Quality and defensive factors provide portfolio ballast

## 4. WSJ — Take On the Week (Sep 2025)

**Format:** Podcast
**Link:** [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/podcasts/take-on-the-week/billionaire-investor-cliff-asness-on-managing-market-risk-and-buffer-etfs/f574ff19-8b59-4037-860c-1cc23a7e818b)

**Key Topics:**

### Managing Market Risk
- Discusses buffer ETFs and structured protection strategies
- Risk management is about portfolio construction, not market timing
- "The best risk management is a diversified portfolio of uncorrelated return streams"

### Buffer ETFs
- Expresses cautious interest in options-based buffer strategies
- They serve a psychological purpose for investors who might otherwise panic-sell
- But warns: "There's no free lunch — protection costs something"

## 5. How I Invest with David Weisburd (May 2025)

**Format:** Podcast
**Link:** [How I Invest Podcast](https://howiinvestpodcast.com/episodes/T2u6Z5sawFt)

**Key Topic: "Why Most Investors Quit Before Winning"**
- The hardest part of systematic investing is sticking with it during drawdowns
- Factor investing requires faith in the process during periods of underperformance
- "If you can't handle a 30% drawdown in your strategy, you shouldn't be in it"
- Discipline is the only edge that persists over time

## 10. Key Conversation Themes Across All Interviews

1. **Intellectual Honesty:** The single most emphasized trait. Asness consistently acknowledges mistakes and the limits of his knowledge.
2. **Discipline Over Prediction:** Factor investing is not about predicting the future; it's about systematic exposure to rewarded risks.
3. **Respect for Data:** Following Fama's "if it's in the data, write the paper" philosophy, even when results are uncomfortable.
4. **Skepticism of Hype:** Whether it's crypto, AI, or factor timing — if the claims sound too good to be true, they probably are.
5. **Humility Despite Success:** Asness attributes much of his success to luck and timing (being at Chicago during the birth of factor investing).
6. **Passion for the Craft:** Often says he loves what he does and would do it even if not paid.

## 11. 2026 Updates and New Perspectives

### 11a. TD Wealth — Monthly Perspectives (Feb 2026)

**Format:** In-depth interview with TD Wealth
**Link:** [TD Wealth PDF](https://advisors.td.com/cmbwealthmanagement/mediahandler/media/783599/Monthly%20Perspectives%20Feb%202026%20EN.pdf)

**Key Topics:**

**On Market Bubbles and AI:**
- "I'm willing to call something a bubble. Rarely and occasionally. But my definition is a very stringent one."
- Distinguishes real bubbles from market concentration — the Mag 7 dominance is concentration, not necessarily a bubble
- During the tech bubble, quantitative investors faced an extremely difficult environment as valuations detached from historical norms

**On the Pain of Bubbles for Systematic Investors:**
- "We tend to really hate the last year of a bubble. We really love the period before, and we really love the period after, but we do not tend to enjoy the crescendo."
- This captures the asymmetric experience of factor investing during extreme market regimes

**On Diversification (60/40 Portfolio):**
- After 2022 (stocks and bonds fell together): "In a 60/40, the 40% is largely delevering stocks more than diversifying against stocks."
- Views the 2022 episode as unusual rather than structural — inflation shocks created a rare environment
- Over longer horizons, bonds have still served as a stabilizing force

**On Quantitative vs Discretionary Convergence:**
- Quant and discretionary approaches are gradually converging
- Both now rely on many of the same underlying concepts — valuation, profitability, risk, and momentum
- Machine learning improves forecasting but introduces new challenges when models become less intuitive
- "Embracing these tools required a shift in mindset"

### 11b. AQR Cliff's Perspectives (2026)

**"A Positive Stock-Bond Correlation Is a Terrible Reason to Add More Equity Risk to Your Portfolio"** (Apr 8, 2026)
- Argues against overreacting to the 2022 stock-bond correlation breakdown
- A single year of positive correlation is not a regime change
- Suggests investors should remain disciplined in their asset allocation

**"I Did Not Predict What Is Going on in Privates"** (Mar 25, 2026)
- Comments on private market valuations and the illusion of low volatility
- Private assets have smoothed returns that mask true risk
- Compares private equity NAV stability to the illusion of safety

**"An Interview with Cliff Asness: The Illusion of Safety in Private Assets"** (Journal of Private Markets Investing, Mar 31, 2026)
- Detailed interview with the editor of JPMI
- Reiterates views on flaws in private asset valuation methodology
- Private markets are riskier than they appear due to infrequent marking

**AQR 2026 Capital Market Assumptions** (Jan 14, 2026)
- Published 2026 capital market assumptions for major asset classes

### 11c. Infinite Loops Podcast (Jan 2026)

**Format:** Video interview
**Link:** [YouTube](https://youtu.be/FjGaAEh5GWI)

**Key Topic: "Do The Rational Strategy and Keep Repeating"**

**On Prospect Theory in Investing:**
- "People debate whether prospect theory is real… it's very real to me"
- Gains and losses are not symmetrical: "It feels about a third as good as the equivalent bad year feels bad"
- This psychological asymmetry is the biggest challenge for systematic investors

**On the Meme Stock Era:**
- Some users "can't distinguish FanDuel from Robinhood. It's just an app."
- The martingale doubling strategy was pitched as "you keep doubling and eventually you win" while ignoring "the risk of ruin"
- **Key warning:** "It isn't if you're going to die, it's when you're going to die"

**On Staying Disciplined:**
- During the 2008 crisis, many quants overrode their models; AQR "didn't do that"
- The ability to survive turbulence without abandoning strategy is a genuine edge
- **Key quote:** "Do the rational strategy and keep repeating"
