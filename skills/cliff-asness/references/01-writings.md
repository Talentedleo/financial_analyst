## 1. Key Academic Publications

### 1.1 The Interaction of Value and Momentum Strategies (1997)

**Source:** *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 1997

Asness's seminal paper combining value and momentum factors. This was the first systematic empirical evidence showing that value and momentum strategies, while individually profitable, are even more powerful when combined due to their negative correlation.

**Core Insights:**
- Value and momentum are negatively correlated — when value underperforms, momentum tends to outperform, and vice versa
- Combining both factors produces higher risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratios) than either alone
- The diversification benefit between value and momentum is consistent across multiple markets and time periods

**Link:** [JPM 1997](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpm/23/2/29)

---

### 1.2 Value and Momentum Everywhere (2013)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, Tobias J. Moskowitz, Lasse Heje Pedersen
**Source:** *The Journal of Finance*, 68(3), 929-985, June 2013

**Core Insights:**
- Found consistent value and momentum return premia across **eight diverse markets and asset classes**: equity indices, currencies, commodities, and government bonds
- Value and momentum are positively correlated across asset classes — a "common factor structure"
- The value premium is higher among assets with larger dispersion in valuations
- The momentum premium is stronger among assets with higher recent return dispersion
- These findings suggest common global risk factors or behavioral biases driving returns

**Award:** Selected as a top finance paper by multiple academic journals

**Link:** [Journal of Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12021)

---

### 1.3 Fact, Fiction, and Factor Investing (2023)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, Andrea Frazzini, Ronen Israel, Tobias J. Moskowitz, Lasse H. Pedersen
**Source:** *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 2023

The most comprehensive review of factor investing myths and realities.

**Core Insights:**
- Factor investing is NOT dead — the long-term premiums remain intact despite periods of underperformance
- Many criticisms of factor investing are based on short-term data or survivorship bias
- Factor timing is extremely difficult — most timing strategies fail to outperform a simple static allocation
- The empirical evidence for value, momentum, quality, and carry factors spans decades and global markets
- Multi-factor portfolios provide superior risk-adjusted returns compared to single-factor approaches

**Award:** Winner of the 25th Annual Bernstein Fabozzi/Jacobs Levy Award for Best Article in *The Journal of Portfolio Management*

**Link:** [AQR Journal Article](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Fact-Fiction-and-Factor-Investing)

---

### 1.4 Fact, Fiction, and Value Investing (2025)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, Andrea Frazzini, Ronen Israel, Tobias J. Moskowitz
**Source:** *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 2025

A focused follow-up examining specifically the value factor.

**Core Insights:**
- "Value is not dead" — the value premium persists, though it goes through extended dry spells
- The 2020-2021 period was one of the worst for value, but the subsequent recovery confirmed its resilience
- The devil is in the details of HML (High Minus Low) construction — specification matters enormously
- Book-to-market is not the only value measure; earnings yield, cash flow yield, and sales-to-price all capture different aspects

**Link:** [AQR Journal Article](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Value-Investing.pdf)

---

### 1.5 Quality Minus Junk (2019)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, Andrea Frazzini, Lasse Heje Pedersen
**Source:** *Review of Accounting Studies*, 24(1), 34-112, 2019

**Core Insights:**
- Defined a "Quality Minus Junk" (QMJ) factor based on profitability, growth, safety, and payout
- High-quality stocks have higher average returns than "junk" stocks — contradicting traditional risk-return trade-off
- The quality premium is significant globally across 24 countries
- Quality is negatively correlated with value, making it an excellent diversifier
- Quality stocks tend to have lower beta, lower volatility, and higher Sharpe ratios

**Link:** [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-018-9470-6)

---

### 1.6 Leverage Aversion and Risk Parity (2012)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, Andrea Frazzini, Lasse Heje Pedersen
**Source:** *Financial Analysts Journal*, 68(1), 47-59, 2012

**Core Insights:**
- Risk parity works because investors are leverage-averse — they underweight low-risk assets and overweight high-risk assets
- Leverage aversion creates a "betting against beta" anomaly: low-beta stocks outperform high-beta stocks on a risk-adjusted basis
- Risk parity portfolios that equalize risk contributions across asset classes produce superior risk-adjusted returns
- The strategy benefits from diversification across equities, bonds, currencies, and commodities

**Link:** [FAJ](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v68.n1.1)

---

### 1.7 Fight the Fed Model (2003)

**Source:** *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 30(1), 11-24, 2003

**Core Insights:**
- The "Fed Model" (comparing stock market earnings yield to bond yields) is fundamentally flawed
- Higher bond yields do NOT justify lower stock valuations — this is a confusion of real versus nominal values
- Stocks are not bonds; comparing their yields directly ignores the equity risk premium
- The Fed Model gained popularity in the 1990s but was debunked by Asness's rigorous analysis
- Using the Fed Model for market timing would have produced poor results historically

**Link:** [JPM 2003](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpm/30/1/11)

---

### 1.8 Do Industries Explain Momentum? (1999)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, John Liew, Ross Stevens
**Source:** *The Journal of Finance*, 54(4), 1999

**Core Insights:**
- Industry momentum explains a significant portion of individual stock momentum
- Both within-industry and across-industry momentum effects exist
- Momentum is not purely an industry effect — stock-specific momentum remains after controlling for industry
- The industry component of momentum is strongest in certain sectors (technology, consumer goods)

**Link:** [Journal of Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00161)

---

### 1.9 The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis (2024)

**Authors:** Cliff Asness, John Liew
**Source:** SSRN Working Paper, September 2024

**Core Insights:**
- Markets are becoming **less efficient** over time, not more — contrary to the Efficient Market Hypothesis
- The rise of passive investing, ETFs, retail trading, and zero-day options has increased market inefficiencies
- More participants chasing short-term signals creates more mispricing opportunities for systematic investors
- Factor premiums have not disappeared; they remain intact but with higher volatility
- This paper argues for a "Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" as a new framework

**Link:** [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4942046)

---

### 1.10 The Devil in HML's Details (2013)

**Source:** *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 39, 2013

**Core Insights:**
- The construction of the value factor (HML) matters enormously for performance
- Different value metrics (book-to-price, earnings yield, sales-to-price) produce different results
- The value premium is sensitive to rebalancing frequency, weighting scheme, and universe definition
- Understanding these construction details is critical for both academic researchers and practitioners

**Link:** [JPM 2013](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpm/39/4/49)

## 5. Key Themes Across Asness's Writings

1. **Persistence of Factor Premiums:** Factor returns are long-term phenomena, not data-mined artifacts
2. **Diversification Across Factors:** Combining value, momentum, quality, carry, and defensive factors provides the best risk-adjusted returns
3. **Skepticism of Market Timing:** Factor timing is extremely difficult; static multi-factor allocation outperforms dynamic strategies
4. **Respect for Data:** Asness follows Fama's principle: "If it's in the data, write the paper" — even when findings challenge his priors
5. **Methodological Rigor:** Construction details matter enormously — small specification changes can destroy or create apparent premiums
6. **Critique of the Fed Model:** A recurring theme — comparing equity yields to bond yields is intellectually lazy and practically misleading
7. **Markets Are Less Efficient:** The rise of passive and retail investing creates more opportunities for systematic active managers

## 6. 2026 Publications

### 6.1 Interview with Cliff Asness: The Illusion of Safety in Private Assets

**Source:** *Journal of Private Markets Investing*, Spring 2026, 24(3), 136-139

**Core Insights:**
- Private market valuations are systematically misleading due to infrequent marking
- The low volatility of private equity NAV is an illusion, not a risk reduction
- Private assets are riskier than they appear — investors should demand higher premia
- Compares the smoothing of private asset returns to "the illusion of safety"
