# Joel Greenblatt — The Systematic Value Investor

> *"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."*
> *"The magic formula works because it's not magic at all — it's just common sense applied systematically."*

Joel Greenblatt is the founder of Gotham Capital (1985), managing principal and co-CIO of Gotham Asset Management, and an adjunct professor at Columbia Business School. From 1985 to 1994, his Gotham Capital generated ~50% annualized returns. His "Magic Formula" is one of the most widely implemented systematic value strategies.

This skill embodies Greenblatt's approach: **systematic, quality-focused, value-aware** — grounded in quantitative discipline but guided by fundamental judgment.

## I. The Magic Formula

### The Core Principle

Buy good companies at cheap prices. Systematically.

### The Two Metrics

| Pillar | Metric | Formula | What It Reveals |
|--------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **Quality** | Return on Capital (ROC) | EBIT / (Net Working Capital + Net Fixed Assets) | How efficiently the company uses its capital |
| **Value** | Earnings Yield (EY) | EBIT / Enterprise Value (EV) | How cheap the stock is relative to its earnings power |

### Why These Metrics?

**Return on Capital (ROC)** — measures business quality:
- A high ROC (30%+) indicates a company with a competitive advantage
- Uses EBIT (operating profit, not net income) to compare capital efficiency across firms
- Excludes cash and non-operating assets — focuses on the capital actually needed to run the business

**Earnings Yield (EY)** — measures cheapness:
- The inverse of P/E ratio, but more informative
- Uses Enterprise Value (EV) instead of market cap — accounts for debt and cash
- A high EY (15%+) suggests an undervalued stock

### The Ranking System

```
Step 1: Universe → 3,500+ stocks (exclude financials, utilities)
Step 2: Rank by ROC → #1 is the highest ROC
Step 3: Rank by EY  → #1 is the highest EY
Step 4: Composite Rank = ROC Rank + EY Rank
Step 5: Buy the 20-30 stocks with the LOWEST composite rank
Step 6: Hold for one year, then rebalance
```

### Why 20-30 Stocks?

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Diversification** | Reduces single-stock risk without diluting returns |
| **Statistical validity** | The formula works on a basket, not on individual picks |
| **Behavioral defense** | Some stocks will underperform; the basket protects you from panicking |

### Backtest Results

| Period | Magic Formula | Market |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| 17-year (book backtest) | ~30.8% CAGR | ~12.3% CAGR |
| Europe 1999-2011 | +182.8% total | +30.5% total |
| Europe 2016-2025 (w/ momentum) | +10.7% CAGR | +9.5% CAGR |

### The Discipline Requirement

> "The formula only works if you stick with it when it stops working."

- The formula underperforms in roughly 2-3 out of every 5 years
- Underperformance is **necessary** — it creates the opportunity
- Most investors quit during underperformance, which is when the strategy is most attractive
- The alternative to the formula (emotional, unsystematic investing) is worse

## II. Special Situations ("The Easy Game")

### The Concept

Special situations are corporate events where the market systematically misprices securities. They are the "easy game" — less competitive, structurally inefficient.

**The Restaurant Analogy:**
> "If you go to the best restaurant, everything on the menu is good. You just need to pick from the good items. The same applies to investing — find the 'easy game' where the selection is already high quality."

### Types of Special Situations

| Type | Opportunity | Structural Edge |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Spinoffs** | Parent companies shed divisions; new shares are sold by institutional holders who don't want small, unfamiliar companies | Temporary supply/demand imbalance |
| **Merger Arbitrage** | Spread between deal price and current price during an acquisition | Predictable outcome with defined timeline |
| **Corporate Restructurings** | Value unlocked through asset sales, debt restructuring | Complex analysis deters competition |
| **Liquidations** | Net asset value exceeds market price | When the "cigar butt" has one last puff |
| **Stub Stocks** | Remaining equity after a major stake is taken private | Complex to analyze; ignored by most |

### Key Principle

> "Don't compete with Goldman Sachs at their own game. Play a different game."

The individual investor's advantage:
- No institutional constraints (mandates, benchmarks, liquidity requirements)
- Can invest in small companies that institutions ignore
- Can be patient — no quarterly performance pressure
- Can invest in complex situations requiring deep analysis

## III. Dynamic Margin of Safety

### Graham's Original Concept

Benjamin Graham: Buy something worth $10 for $6. The $4 discount is the margin of safety.

### Greenblatt's Extension

The margin of safety changes over time depending on business quality:

```
Static (Cigar Butt):
  Pay $6 for $10 that stays $10 → margin of safety stays at $4
  Risk: $10 might become $8 → margin of safety shrinks to $2

Dynamic (Quality Business):
  Pay $8 for $10 that grows to $14 → margin of safety grows
  The "extra" you paid is more than compensated by growth
```

### The Key Insight

> "Which one had the bigger margin of safety? It was most likely the one you paid more for, but the value was growing over time."

This insight bridges the gap between:
- **Pure value** (Graham: buy anything cheap)
- **Quality value** (Buffett: buy wonderful companies at fair prices)

### Framework: Trade the Cheap, Hold the Great

| Type | Action | Criteria |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Cigar Butts** | Trade them | Cheap but not great businesses; sell when price normalizes |
| **Quality Compounders** | Hold them | Good businesses bought at reasonable prices; let value grow |

## IV. Quality-First Investing

### What Makes a "Good Business"

Greenblatt's criteria evolved significantly over his career:
- High return on capital (30%+) — the company earns more than its cost of capital
- Competitive advantage — the returns are sustainable (moat)
- Management that allocates capital well
- Business model you can understand

### The Evolution

```
1985: Graham cigar butts → anything cheap
1997: Special situations → when others aren't looking
2005: Magic Formula → systematic quality + value
2011: Index for most → honest about limits of active management
2020: Universal capital ownership → economic equality view
2024: Quality compounders → margin of safety grows
```

### Quality vs. Growth

> "Value and growth are one. Growth is part of value. It's an artificial division."

- The Magic Formula inherently captures growth through the ROC metric
- A company that grows its intrinsic value expands your margin of safety
- The Magic Formula ranks high-ROC companies highly — it's "growth at a reasonable price"

## V. Practical Application

### Implementing the Magic Formula

**Method 1: DIY Screener**
1. Set minimum market cap ($100M-$1B+ depending on your risk tolerance)
2. Screen for EBIT / (NWC + NFA) → rank high to low
3. Screen for EBIT / EV → rank high to low
4. Add ranks, buy the 20-30 with lowest combined rank
5. Hold 1 year, then repeat

**Method 2: Online Magic Formula Screener**
- https://www.magicformulainvesting.com
- Various third-party screeners with built-in Magic Formula ranking

**Method 3: Gotham Asset Management Mutual Funds**
- For those who prefer professional management
- Low-fee alternatives available

### For Different Investor Types

**For Individual Investors with Time:**
- Screen and implement the Magic Formula yourself
- Requires discipline to stick with it through underperformance
- Consider adding a momentum overlay to reduce drawdowns

**For Individual Investors without Time:**
- "For most people, index investing is better than trying to beat the market"
- A combination of low-cost index funds + small value tilt is optimal

**For Active Managers:**
- Use the Magic Formula as an initial screen, then apply fundamental overlay
- Look for special situations where the formula doesn't capture the full story
- "Trade the cheap, hold the great"

### Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Abandoning after 1 bad year | The formula needs 3-5 years to work | Commit to a multi-year time horizon |
| Tinkering with the formula | Changes break the system | Follow the process exactly |
| Buying only 1-2 top-ranked stocks | The formula is designed for baskets | Buy 20-30 stocks |
| Not excluding financials/utilities | Their financial ratios are distorted | Apply proper universe filters |
| Rebalancing too frequently | The formula's edge compounds over 1-year periods | Stick to annual rebalancing |

## VI. Greenblatt Beyond Investing: Common Sense

### The Capitalist's Birthday Gift

In *Common Sense* (2020), Greenblatt proposed a market-based solution to economic inequality:

**The Proposal:**
- Every American child receives a ~$20,000 trust fund at birth
- Funded by a moderate consumption tax (not income or wealth tax)
- Invested in a diversified market portfolio until retirement age
- Creates universal capital ownership without redistributing existing wealth

**Key Quote:**
> "The best way to make capitalism work for everyone is to make everyone a capitalist."

### The Evolution of Greenblatt's Thinking on Society

| Year | Focus | Expression |
|------|-------|------------|
| 2002 | Education reform | $2.5M donation to P.S. 65Q, Queens |
| 2006 | Charter schools | Co-founded Success Academy |
| 2020 | Economic equality | *Common Sense* book and universal capital ownership proposal |

### Why This Matters for Investors

Greenblatt's broader work shows that his systematic thinking extends beyond stock picking:
- The same analytical framework applied to social problems
- Data-driven, market-based solutions
- Focus on measurable outcomes
- "Easy game" concept applied to philanthropy — find where you can make a difference

## X. Limitations

This skill:
- **Does not** provide real-time stock screens or recommendations
- **Does not** guarantee specific returns — past performance does not predict future results
- **Does not** replace your own due diligence on individual companies
- **Excels at** providing a systematic framework for stock selection and portfolio construction
- **Works best** when combined with the discipline to stick with the process through underperformance
- **Is designed for** equity investors — the framework does not directly apply to fixed income or other asset classes
- **Assumes** the Magic Formula's historical edge persists — future market conditions may differ
