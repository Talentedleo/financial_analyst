# 04 — External Views

Critical and external perspectives on Joel Greenblatt's philosophy, methods, and track record.

---

## Criticisms

### 1. Magic Formula Performance in Recent Years
**Criticism:** The Magic Formula has underperformed for extended periods, particularly during the growth-dominated 2010s and the post-COVID speculative boom.

**Context:** The backtest shows the formula underperforms in 2-3 out of every 5 years. From 2018-2024, value strategies broadly struggled against growth. The European backtest showed underperformance in 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023, and 2024 — five out of nine years.

**Counterpoint:** Underperformance is built into the strategy's design. As Greenblatt says, "the formula only works if you stick with it when it stops working." The 2025 rebound confirmed this pattern.

### 2. Survivorship Bias in the Backtest
**Criticism:** The 30.8% annual return over 17 years reported in the book may overstate results due to survivorship bias — companies that went bankrupt would have been removed from the backtest data.

**Context:** Greenblatt has acknowledged this concern. More recent independent backtests (e.g., Quant Investing 2016-2025, Europe) show more modest outperformance: 10.7% CAGR vs. 9.5% for the benchmark — still positive but far less dramatic.

### 3. Less Relevant in a Low-Rate World (2010-2021)
**Criticism:** The Magic Formula screens for relatively cheap, high-ROIC companies. During the zero-interest-rate era, growth companies with negative earnings dramatically outperformed value stocks. The formula looked broken.

**Counterpoint:** Greenblatt acknowledged this environment was hostile to value, which is precisely why he described the value opportunity set as the most attractive in decades (2021 interview).

### 4. Too Simple / Accessible
**Criticism:** If anyone can run the Magic Formula, why would it continue to work? As more people use it, the edge may diminish.

**Counterpoint:** Despite the formula's popularity, behavioral factors keep it working:
- Most investors can't tolerate the drawdowns
- Most investors abandon it during underperformance
- The formula requires holding 20-30 stocks for one year — most can't resist tinkering

### 5. Limited to Equities
**Criticism:** Unlike Buffett or Marks, Greenblatt's framework is almost exclusively equity-focused. It doesn't address fixed income, real estate, or other asset classes.

### 6. Gotham's Recent AUM Decline
**Criticism:** Gotham Asset Management's AUM peaked at $5.73B (2018) and declined to $3.74B (2021). This may reflect performance difficulties or redemptions.

## Peer Perspectives

### Howard Marks
Interviewed Greenblatt (2017), calling him a "fellow value investor" and exploring his approach to special situations and margin of safety.

### Benjamin Graham (Indirect)
Greenblatt extends Graham's margin of safety concept — from static to dynamic (value that grows over time).

### Warren Buffett (Indirect)
Greenblatt's evolution from cigar-butt to wonderful businesses mirrors Buffett's own evolution, which Greenblatt explicitly acknowledges.

### Michael Burry
Greenblatt backed Burry's Scion Capital in 2000, investing $1M for 25% of the fund. This was a vote of confidence in a then-unknown manager.

---

## Key External Analyses

### Academic Studies
Multiple academic studies have tested the Magic Formula:
- World-wide studies find the formula tends to generate long-term outperformance
- Higher volatility and sharper drawdowns than the market
- Best results in smaller-cap stocks
- Works across geographies (US, Europe, Asia)

### Media Coverage
Greenblatt is respected but less covered than Buffett or Marks. He is occasionally featured in financial press (CNBC, Bloomberg) for market commentary, but his media presence is intentionally limited.
