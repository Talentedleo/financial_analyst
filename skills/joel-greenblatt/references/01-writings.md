# 01 — Writings

Joel Greenblatt's written work spans four books spanning over two decades. His writing is known for making complex value investing concepts accessible through clear, step-by-step reasoning and wit. He is also the author of over 30 years of investment memos to Gotham Capital partners.

---

## Books

### You Can Be a Stock Market Genius: Uncover the Secret Hiding Places of Stock Market Profits (1997)

**Publisher:** Simon & Schuster  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/You-Can-Stock-Market-Genius/dp/0684832135

Greenblatt's first book, focused on special situations investing — spinoffs, restructurings, mergers, and other corporate events where the market systematically misprices securities.

**Core Ideas:**
- Special situations are the most fertile ground for individual investors — they are underfollowed, misunderstood, and offer asymmetric returns
- Spinoffs create value because parent companies shed divisions that don't fit, and the new standalone entity is often ignored by institutional investors
- Risk arbitrage (merger arbitrage) can generate consistent returns with proper execution
- Corporate restructurings, liquidations, and stub stocks offer hidden opportunities
- The key is finding situations where the market's attention is elsewhere

**Key Quotes:**
> "The best place to look for bargains is where others aren't looking."

> "Special situations are the 'easy game' for the individual investor — you don't need to beat the professionals at their own game if you play a different game."

> "When a company spins off a division, the new shares are often sold by institutional holders who don't want to own a small, unfamiliar company — creating a temporary supply-demand imbalance that the individual investor can exploit."

**Notable Case Studies:**
- Spinoff of Seagate Technology (detailed walkthrough of how the spinoff was analyzed and traded)
- Various merger arbitrage examples from the 1980s-1990s

### The Little Book That Beats the Market (2005)

**Publisher:** John Wiley & Sons  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Little-Book-Still-Beats-Market/dp/0470624159

Greenblatt's most famous book, a New York Times bestseller with over 300,000 copies sold. Introduces the "Magic Formula" — a systematic approach to identifying good companies at bargain prices.

**Core Ideas:**
- The Magic Formula combines two metrics: high Return on Capital (quality) and high Earnings Yield (cheapness)
- By ranking stocks on these two factors, the formula systematically identifies mispriced quality businesses
- Over a 17-year backtest, the strategy returned 30.8% per year vs. 12.3% for the market
- The strategy works because it exploits the market's tendency to overreact in the short term
- Patience is essential — the formula underperforms in 2-3 out of every 5 years

**The Magic Formula:**

| Pillar | Metric | Formula | What It Measures |
|--------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| Quality | Return on Capital (ROC) | EBIT / (Net Working Capital + Net Fixed Assets) | How efficiently a company uses its capital to generate profits |
| Value | Earnings Yield (EY) | EBIT / Enterprise Value (EV) | How cheap the stock is relative to its operating earnings |

**Implementation:**
1. Start with a universe of 3,500+ stocks (excluding financials and utilities)
2. Rank all companies by ROC (highest = #1)
3. Rank all companies by EY (highest = #1)
4. Add the two ranks together for a composite score
5. Buy the 20-30 stocks with the lowest combined rank
6. Hold for one year, then rebalance

**Key Quotes:**
> "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."

> "The magic formula works because it's not magic at all — it's just common sense applied systematically."

> "Underperformance is normal, expected, and actually necessary for the formula to work over the long term."

> "The formula only works if you stick with it when it stops working."

**Updated Edition (2010):** *The Little Book That Still Beats the Market* — includes new introduction and afterword addressing the 2008 financial crisis, with updated backtest data.

### The Big Secret for the Small Investor: A New Route to Long-Term Investment Success (2011)

**Publisher:** Crown Business  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Big-Secret-Small-Investor-Long-Term/dp/0385527100

Aimed at individual investors who don't have the time or expertise for stock-picking. Expands on the Magic Formula concept and introduces a "big secret" approach using low-cost index funds combined with value-oriented strategies.

**Core Ideas:**
- For most people, a combination of low-cost index funds and a small allocation to value-oriented strategies is the optimal approach
- The "big secret" is that you don't need to pick individual stocks to succeed
- Behavioral discipline matters more than stock-picking skill
- Dollar-cost averaging into diversified holdings is the most reliable path
- Acknowledges that active management has a poor track record for most investors

**Key Quotes:**
> "The big secret is that there is no big secret. Successful investing is about discipline, patience, and keeping costs low."

> "Most people should not try to beat the market. They should join it."

### Common Sense: The Investor's Guide to Equality, Opportunity, and Growth (2020)

**Publisher:** Columbia University Press  
**Link:** https://www.amazon.com/Common-Sense-Investors-Equality-Opportunity/dp/023119786X

Greenblatt's most recent and most unusual book — rather than a pure investing guide, it proposes a market-based solution to economic inequality by giving every American a "capital stake" at birth, funded by a consumption tax.

**Core Ideas:**
- Every child born in America would receive a $20,000+ trust fund (the "Capitalist's Birthday Gift")
- Funded by a moderate consumption tax (not income or wealth tax)
- The trust would be invested in a diversified market portfolio
- This creates universal capital ownership without redistribution of existing wealth
- Aims to address inequality by making everyone a capitalist, not by punishing capital owners

**Key Quotes:**
> "The best way to make capitalism work for everyone is to make everyone a capitalist."

> "We don't need to punish success to spread opportunity."

### Notable 2021 Commentary on Market Froth

**Source:** Acquirer's Multiple — https://acquirersmultiple.com/2021/04/joel-greenblatt-im-excited-about-the-value-opportunity-set-going-forward/

**Key Points:**
- 359 companies that lost money in 2019 had market caps over $1 billion
- Their median return in 2020 was 65%, average was 120%
- "Just buy everything that loses money" was the best single investment strategy in 2020
- This kind of market behavior historically signals a major opportunity for value investors
