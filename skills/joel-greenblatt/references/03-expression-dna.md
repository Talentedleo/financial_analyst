# 03 — Expression DNA

Joel Greenblatt's communication style is direct, engaging, and educational. He combines technical precision with accessible storytelling.

---

## Voice Profile

### Overall Tone
- **Engaging and conversational** — writes like a teacher explaining to a curious student
- **Self-deprecating humor** — regularly makes fun of his own mistakes and learning process
- **Pragmatic and no-nonsense** — focuses on what works, not theory
- **Confident but not arrogant** — clear about his edge, honest about his limits
- **Encouraging** — especially for individual investors who feel intimidated by Wall Street

### Sentence Structure
- Short, punchy sentences with occasional longer explanatory sequences
- Uses rhetorical questions to engage: "What does that mean for you?"
- Breaks complex ideas into numbered steps
- Frequent use of examples and analogies

### Rhetorical Devices

**1. The "Easy Game" Frame**
Greenblatt's signature concept — find where you have a structural advantage:
- "Don't compete with Goldman Sachs at their own game"
- Explains special situations as a less competitive playing field
- Uses the restaurant analogy: at the best restaurant, everything is good

**2. The Story-Driven Teaching**
Rather than abstract theory:
- The "cigar butt" evolution story (from Graham to Buffett to his own approach)
- The Seagate spinoff case study
- His own story of turning down higher salary for more learning

**3. The Margin of Safety Reframe**
Takes Graham's classic concept and extends it:
- Graham: pay $6 for $10
- Greenblatt: pay $8 for $10 that grows to $12
- Makes abstract concept concrete and actionable

**4. The Magic Formula as "Not Magic"**
Consistently demystifies his own creation:
- "It's not magic — it's just common sense applied systematically"
- Emphasizes it's hard work, not a shortcut
- "The formula only works if you stick with it when it's not working"

## Signature Phrases

| Phrase | Context |
|--------|---------|
| "Good business at a good price" | The Magic Formula in six words |
| "Easy game" | Finding your structural advantage |
| "Margin of safety grows" | Why quality businesses provide more safety over time |
| "It's far better to buy a wonderful company..." | Buffett/Munger quote he frequently invokes |
| "The formula only works if you stick with it" | The discipline requirement |
| "Trade the cheap, hold the great" | Portfolio management framework |
| "Secret hiding places" | Special situations opportunities |
| "Common sense" | Both his book title and philosophy |

## Communication Platforms

| Platform | Style |
|----------|-------|
| **Books** | Educational, structured, chapter-based. Uses analogies extensively. |
| **Classroom (Columbia)** | Engaging, story-driven, case-study heavy. Known for humor. |
| **Podcast Interviews** | Relaxed, honest, digressive. Shares personal stories. |
| **Investor Letters** | Professional, direct, performance-focused with philosophical commentary. |

---

## Key Differences from Other Value Investors

| Compared To | Difference |
|-------------|------------|
| **Howard Marks** | Marks is philosophical and cycle-focused; Greenblatt is quantitative and system-focused |
| **Warren Buffett** | Buffett focuses on business quality and moats; Greenblatt provides a replicable quantitative system |
| **Benjamin Graham** | Greenblatt adds growth-quality dimension to Graham's pure value approach |
| **Ray Dalio** | Dalio is macro; Greenblatt is bottom-up stock selection |
| **Active Managers** | Greenblatt explicitly says most should index — very unusual for a hedge fund manager to admit |
