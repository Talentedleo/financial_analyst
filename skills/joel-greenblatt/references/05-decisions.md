# 05 — Key Decisions & Investment Cases

Joel Greenblatt's most significant investment decisions and cases demonstrate how his philosophy translates into action.

---

## 1. Founding Gotham Capital (1985)

**Context:** Greenblatt graduated from Wharton (BS + MBA) and briefly studied law at Stanford before dropping out. He worked at Halcyon Investment (a risk arbitrage firm) as the sole analyst for three partners. In 1985, he started Gotham Capital with $7 million, most provided by Michael Milken.

**Decision:** Start a hedge fund focused on special situations at age 28 with capital from the "junk bond king."

**Result:** 50% annualized returns (before fees) over the next 9 years.

**Framework:**
- Special situations were the "easy game" — underfollowed, misunderstood, institutional constraints
- Small fund size enabled nimble positioning
- Deep knowledge of corporate events (spinoffs, mergers, restructurings)

---

## 2. Returning All Outside Capital (1995)

**Context:** By 1995, Gotham Capital had grown from $7M to approximately $500M. The special situations strategy was becoming harder to execute at scale.

**Decision:** Return all outside partner capital (~$500M). Operate as a family office.

**Why:**
- The strategy's capacity was reached — too much capital chasing too few special situations
- Integrity — wouldn't manage money he couldn't deploy effectively
- Focus on his own capital and developing the Magic Formula

**Result:** Greenblatt spent the next 13 years developing, testing, and refining the Magic Formula system.

---

## 3. Backing Michael Burry / Scion Capital (2000)

**Context:** In 2000, a young, unknown Michael Burry was starting his hedge fund. Greenblatt recognized his talent.

**Decision:** Invested $1M for 25% of Scion Capital's capital. By October 2006, Gotham's investment had grown to $100 million.

**Framework Applied:**
- Recognized a kindred spirit — value-oriented, analytical, contrarian
- Special situation investing applied to people: found an undervalued manager
- The "easy game" concept extended to manager selection

**Result:** One of the most successful early-stage hedge fund investments in history.

---

## 4. The Apple Investment

**Context:** Greenblatt bought Apple at approximately 7x earnings when it was widely viewed as a hardware manufacturer.

**Decision:** Recognize Apple as more than a hardware company — "a gateway to the internet" with a valuable ecosystem.

**Framework Applied:**
- Second-level thinking: "Maybe this isn't quite only a hardware manufacturer"
- Margin of safety through growth: buying cheap + business value growing
- "Trade the cheap, hold the great" — Apple was a holder

**Key Quote:**
> "The real analysis was, boy, what a gateway to the internet, was really where the value."

---

## 5. Reopening to Outside Investors (2008-2010)

**Context:** After 13 years of private capital management, the Magic Formula was fully developed. The 2008 crisis created attractive conditions for value investing.

**Decision:** Created Gotham Asset Management (2008), launched mutual funds (2010), raised $360M.

**Framework Applied:**
- The Magic Formula is scalable (quantitative, process-driven)
- Market conditions were favorable for value
- Institutional demand existed for systematic value strategies

**Result:** Gotham Asset Management grew to $5.73B AUM (peak, 2018), managing long/short and long-only strategies.

---

## 6. Founding Success Academy Charter Schools (2006)

**Context:** Greenblatt had donated $2.5M to a public elementary school in Queens. He saw the need for systemic educational change.

**Decision:** Co-founded Success Academy Charter Schools (then Harlem Success Academy) in 2006. Started with one elementary school.

**Framework Applied:**
- Same "easy game" approach — find where you can make a difference
- Systematic methodology — applied data-driven management to education
- Focus on measurable outcomes

**Result:** Success Academy grew to become New York City's largest charter school network, serving over 20,000 students.

---

## 7. Publishing the Magic Formula (2005)

**Context:** Greenblatt had developed a quantitative value strategy that produced exceptional backtested returns. He chose to publish it publicly.

**Decision:** Write *The Little Book That Beats the Market* revealing the exact formula.

**Why:**
- Believed the strategy would still work despite being public
- Behavioral factors prevent most investors from following it
- Wanted to help individual investors

**Result:** New York Times bestseller, 300,000+ copies sold. The formula continues to be widely used.

## Investment Style Summary

| Dimension | Greenblatt's Approach |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Primary Asset Class** | US equities (long/short and long-only) |
| **Position Sizing** | 20-30 equally-weighted positions |
| **Holding Period** | ~1 year (Magic Formula); longer for high-quality holdings |
| **Decision Frequency** | Annual rebalance (Magic Formula); opportunistic for special sits |
| **Risk Management** | Diversification across 20-30 stocks; systematic ranking removes emotion |
| **Edge Source** | Quantitative screening + qualitative overlay |
| **Evolution** | 1985-1995: Special situations → 2005+: Systematic Magic Formula |
