### 1. Money Maze Podcast (2024)
**Date:** 2024  
**Link:** https://acquirersmultiple.com/2024/10/joel-greenblatt-great-businesses-expand-their-margin-of-safety/

**Conversation Overview:**
A comprehensive interview covering Greenblatt's evolution from special situations investor to systematic value investor. Key focus on how his thinking has evolved over 40 years.

**Key Excerpts:**
> "If you could pay $8 for something worth $10, but that $10 was growing at 12 or 14%, your margin of safety is actually growing over time. So which one had the bigger margin of safety? It was most likely the one you paid more for, but the value was growing over time."

> "I think you want to stick in general to good businesses that invest their money well. More and more, I've gravitated towards that over the last 30, 40 years."

**Apple Case Study:**
- Gotham owned Apple at 7x earnings
- Greenblatt recognized Apple as "a gateway to the internet" with an ecosystem beyond hardware
- This hybrid approach: buy cheap for trading, hold great businesses long-term
- Framework: trade the cheap, hold the great

### 2. Howard Marks Interviews Joel Greenblatt (2017)
**Date:** 2017  
**Source:** Chinese financial media (translated) — https://www.sohu.com/a/135845011_747646

**Conversation Overview:**
Howard Marks interviewed Greenblatt in a rare conversation between two investing legends. Greenblatt shared his investment philosophy in depth.

**Key Excerpts (translated):**

*On "Easy Game":*
> "If you go to the best restaurant, everything on the menu is good. You just need to pick from the good items. The same applies to investing — find the 'easy game' where the selection is already high quality."

*On Margin of Safety:*
> "Graham would find a cigar butt — pay $6 for something worth $10. But it wasn't a great business, so maybe the $10 becomes only $8, and your margin of safety shrinks. I prefer to find $10 that can grow to $12, so the margin of safety grows."

*On Value vs. Growth:*
> "Value and growth are one. Growth is part of value. It's an artificial division. The value of growth increases your margin of safety."

*On Market Madness:*
> "The market is crazy, giving big opportunities to value investors. People's likes and dislikes cause price swings far beyond what fundamentals justify."

### 3. Rich Pzena Masterclass on Value Investing (2021)
**Host:** Rich Pzena  
**Source:** https://acquirersmultiple.com/2021/04/joel-greenblatt-im-excited-about-the-value-opportunity-set-going-forward/

**Conversation Overview:**
Greenblatt discussed why he was excited about the value opportunity set going forward, providing statistical evidence of market froth.

**Key Statistics Shared:**
- 359 companies that lost money in 2019 had market caps over $1B
- These companies' median return in 2020: +65%, average return: +120%
- "Just buy everything that loses money" was the best strategy for 2020 (normally the worst)
- This divergence signaled a massive opportunity for value investors

**Key Quote:**
> "That makes me very hopeful, and it's cold and calculated."

## Key Themes Across Interviews

### 1. Evolution from Special Situations to Systematic Value
Greenblatt's career shows a clear evolution:
- **1985-1995 (Gotham Capital I):** Special situations focus — spinoffs, restructurings, merger arbitrage. 50% annualized returns.
- **1995-2008 (Private Capital):** Returned outside capital, traded his own money. Developed the Magic Formula framework.
- **2008-Present (Gotham Asset Management):** Systematic value investing using quantitative screens combined with fundamental analysis.

### 2. Quality Over Cigar Butts
A consistent evolution in his thinking:
- Started as a pure Graham "cigar butt" investor
- Shifted toward buying wonderful companies at fair prices (Buffett-Munger influence)
- Recognized that a good business's growth expands your margin of safety
- "Pay $8 for $10 growing at 12% vs. $6 for $10 shrinking"

### 3. The Easy Game Concept
Recurs across all interviews:
- Find the market where you have a structural advantage
- Individual investors should focus on spinoffs, small caps, and special situations
- Institutional constraints (mandates, benchmarks, liquidity needs) create opportunities
- "Don't compete with Goldman Sachs at their own game"

### 4. Emotional Detachment
- The Magic Formula's automation removes emotion
- "The formula only works if you stick with it when it stops working"
- Underperformance is expected — it's the price of admission
- Most investors abandon strategies during drawdowns, which is exactly when they should be doubling down

### 5. Value vs. Growth is a False Dichotomy
- Growth is a component of value
- A business that grows its intrinsic value over time provides an expanding margin of safety
- The Magic Formula captures both (ROC measures quality, EY measures cheapness)

### 6. Common Sense and Equality
- Later in his career, Greenblatt's focus expanded beyond investing
- *Common Sense* (2020) proposes universal capital ownership as a solution to inequality
- His charter school work (Success Academy) reflects similar commitment to opportunity

## Gotham Capital Track Record

**1985-1994 (Gotham Capital I):**
- Annualized return: 50% (before GP incentive fees), ~30% net of all fees
- Capital: Started with $7M, grew to ~$500M
- Strategy: Special situations (spinoffs, restructurings, merger arbitrage)
- Returned all outside capital in 1995

**1995-2008 (Private Capital):**
- Gotham closed to outside investors
- Greenblatt traded his own capital
- Developed Magic Formula framework
- Supported Michael Burry's Scion Capital (2000-2006)

**2008-Present (Gotham Asset Management):**
- Reopened to outside investors
- Launched mutual funds in 2010 ($360M raised)
- Systematic value approach combining quantitative screens with fundamental research
- $3.74B AUM (as of Dec 2021)
- Long/short and long-only institutional strategies
