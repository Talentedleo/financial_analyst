# John Templeton — Writings & Investment Philosophy

A comprehensive collection of Sir John Templeton's writings, published works, and investment framework defining his global contrarian value investing approach.

---

## 1. The Templeton Growth Fund — Track Record

**Founded:** 1954 (incorporated in Canada for tax efficiency)
**Owner:** Sold to Franklin Resources in 1992 for $913 million
**Performance:** 13.8%–15% annualized return from 1954 to 2004 (~16% until 1992 retirement), vs S&P 500's ~11.1% over the same period

**Key Innovation:** One of the first mutual funds to invest globally, pioneering international diversification decades before it became standard.

## 2. Core Investment Philosophy

### Points of Maximum Pessimism
Templeton's most famous concept — buy at the point of maximum pessimism:
> "Bull markets are born on pessimism, grow on skepticism, mature on optimism, and die on euphoria. The time of maximum pessimism is the best time to buy, and the time of maximum optimism is the best time to sell."

### Bargain Hunting (Global Value Investing)
A CFA charterholder who studied under Benjamin Graham at Columbia, Templeton described himself as a "bargain hunter" searching globally for the most undervalued stocks:
> "To find the best investments, ask not where the outlook is brightest, but where the outlook is most miserable."

**Four Key Criteria for Stock Selection:**
1. **Low P/E ratio** — Below both historical average and industry average
2. **High operating profit margins** — Above industry average
3. **Near or below liquidation value** — Asset-based margin of safety
4. **Consistent historic growth rate** — Evidence of sustainable business quality

### Contrarian Approach
- Actively seek stocks that are out of favor and unpopular
- "Avoid the herd" — most investors are wrong at extremes
- Buy when there's "blood in the streets"
- Take profits when values and expectations are high

### Global Diversification
- First major US investor in Japanese stocks (1960s)
- Invested in European stocks post-WWII
- Pioneered emerging market investing
- "Cast a wide net, one that spans the world"
- Avoid countries with socialism and high inflation

### Long-Term Perspective
- Average holding period: ~4 years
- Hold until stock reaches fair market value
- Selling when overvalued is "speculation, not investing"
- Belief that markets eventually recognize intrinsic value

## 3. The 16 Rules for Investment Success

Templeton's most famous distilled guidance (published by Franklin Templeton):

1. **Invest for maximum total real return** — After taxes and inflation
2. **Invest — don't trade or speculate** — The stock market is not a casino
3. **Remain flexible and open-minded** — No investment method works forever
4. **Buy low** — The time of maximum pessimism offers the best bargains
5. **Search for quality stocks at bargain prices** — Not just cheap stocks
6. **Buy value, not market trends** — The crowd is often wrong at turning points
7. **Diversify globally** — Opportunities exist everywhere
8. **Diversify across asset classes** — Bonds, cash, real estate alongside stocks
9. **Do your homework** — Research, research, research
10. **No one knows everything** — Humility is essential
11. **No rule works all the time** — Have realistic expectations
12. **Avoid the crowd** — "The four most dangerous words in investing: 'This time it's different'"
13. **Be patient** — The market rewards patience
14. **Be flexible** — Adapt to changing conditions
15. **Learn from your mistakes** — Every mistake is a learning opportunity
16. **Give back** — The ultimate purpose of wealth is philanthropy

## 4. The 1939 Investment: A Defining Moment

**The Trade:**
- At the outbreak of World War II (1939)
- Borrowed $10,400
- Bought 100 shares of every NYSE stock trading below $1
- 104 companies total (37 in bankruptcy)
- Sold 4 years later for ~$40,000

**Significance:**
- Demonstrated "maximum pessimism" principle in action
- Proved that mass fear creates extraordinary bargains
- Established his reputation as a contrarian value investor
- 34 companies went bankrupt, but the winners more than compensated

## 5. Templeton Screens & Quantitative Methods

From the late 1930s, Templeton developed sophisticated quantitative methods that anticipated by decades features such as the Shiller P/E, rebalancing, and Tobin's q.

**Templeton Screen Criteria (Quantitative):**
- P/E ratio below 5-year average
- Price-to-book below industry average
- Positive EPS growth (1-year and 5-year)
- Estimated future EPS growth above industry average
- Operating margin positive and above industry average
- Total assets/total liabilities below industry average
- Also looks for catalysts: new markets, products, M&A, industry changes

## 7. Philanthropic Writings & Templeton Prize

Templeton founded the **Templeton Prize** in 1972 (initially £1M+ — set higher than Nobel to signal its importance), awarded for "Progress toward Research or Discoveries about Spiritual Realities."

**Templeton Foundation:** Founded 1987 with focus on science-religion dialogue. Key areas: humility theology, gratitude, purpose, free will, the nature of human flourishing.

Templeton's philanthropic philosophy: "The ultimate goal of accumulating wealth should be to give it away."

---

## Summary of Core Intellectual Contributions

1. **Points of Maximum Pessimism:** A behavioral framework for contrarian entry timing that remains one of investing's most powerful concepts.

2. **Global Bargain Hunting:** Pioneered the concept of searching for value across all markets worldwide, not just domestic.

3. **16 Rules for Investment Success:** A comprehensive, evergreen code of investing conduct.

4. **Templeton Screens:** One of the earliest systematic quantitative value screens.

5. **Contrarian Philosophy:** "The four most dangerous words in investing are 'This time it's different.'"

6. **Philanthropy as Purpose:** Demonstrated that great investors can be great givers ($1B+ donated).
