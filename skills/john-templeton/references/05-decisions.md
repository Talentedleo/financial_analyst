# John Templeton — Key Investment Decisions

---

## 1. The 1939 World War II Trade (Life-Defining)

**Context:** Day World War II began
**Action:** Called his broker; bought 100 shares of every NYSE stock under $1
**Investment:** $10,400 borrowed
**Companies:** 104 total (37 in bankruptcy)
**Result:** Sold 4 years later for ~$40,000

**Significance:**
- Embodied "maximum pessimism" — war-driven fear created extraordinary bargains
- 34 went completely bankrupt, yet winners more than compensated
- Established his reputation and philosophy for life

---

## 2. Japanese Stock Investing (1950s–1960s)

**Context:** Japan was a war-torn, overlooked market
**Action:** Templeton Growth Fund became one of the first American funds to invest heavily in Japan
**Timing:** Entered early (1950s-60s), exited as Japan became fashionable (1970s)
**Result:** Massive outperformance as Japan's economy rebuilt

**Significance:**
- Demonstrated his willingness to go where others wouldn't
- Proved global diversification works
- Rotated out at "maximum optimism" — sold when everyone wanted Japanese stocks

---

## 3. European Stocks Post-WWII

**Context:** Europe was devastated, prices were low
**Action:** Invested in undervalued European companies in rebuilding economies
**Criteria:** Low inflation, fewer regulatory obstacles, pro-business environments
**Result:** Significant returns as Europe rebuilt

---

## 4. Emerging Markets Funds

**Action:** Created the first emerging markets fund available to US investors
**Focus:** Latin America, Asia, Eastern Europe
**Approach:** Sought countries with free enterprise, low inflation, minimal government interference

---

## 5. Selling the Firm to Franklin (1992)

**Context:** Templeton was 80 years old
**Action:** Sold Templeton, Galbraith & Hansberger to Franklin Resources for $913 million
**Significance:** One of the largest mutual fund acquisitions at the time; ensured his philosophy would live on through Franklin Templeton

---

## 6. 2005 Memo — Financial Chaos Prediction

**Context:** Privately circulated to family and Franklin management
**Prediction:** Within 5 years, housing market collapse, near-zero government bond yields
**Published:** Made public in 2010 (posthumously)
**Accuracy:** Correctly anticipated the 2008 Global Financial Crisis

---

## 7. The Templeton Prize (1972)

**Context:** Templeton wanted to elevate spiritual research
**Action:** Created prize with initial value exceeding Nobel Prize (£1M+)
**Purpose:** "Progress toward Research or Discoveries about Spiritual Realities"
**Significance:** Reflected his belief that science and spirituality are complementary; one of the most valuable annual prizes in the world
