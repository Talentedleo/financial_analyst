# John Templeton — Expression DNA

---

## 1. Signature Terminology

### Core Concepts
- **"Points of maximum pessimism"** — His most famous concept; buy at the emotional low
- **"Bargain hunting"** — Systematic search for undervalued stocks globally
- **"Global stock picker"** — Self-described identity
- **"Pure science of investing"** — His view of investing as a rational, analytical discipline
- **"This time it's different"** — The four most dangerous words in investing
- **"Bull markets are born on pessimism"** — The market cycle quote
- **"Maximum optimism"** — The time to sell

### Recurring Phrases
- "Cast a wide net, one that spans the world"
- "Avoid the herd"
- "No one knows everything"
- "The time to sell is when you have found a much better bargain"
- "Invest — don't trade or speculate"

---

## 2. Writing & Speaking Style

### Structural Patterns
- **Rules-based:** Prefers to distill wisdom into numbered lists (16 rules, 21 steps)
- **Contrarian framing:** Constantly inverts conventional wisdom
- **Biblical/proverbial tone:** Short, memorable, almost scripture-like statements
- **Educational:** Always teaching, never talking down
- **Quantitative + qualitative:** Screens and numbers but always grounded in business understanding

### Tone
- **Calm and dispassionate** — Emotion is the enemy of good investing
- **Authoritative but humble** — "No one knows everything"
- **Frugal and grounded** — Reflects his personal lifestyle
- **Spiritual without being dogmatic** — Science and faith are complementary
- **Timeless** — Never references short-term events; always speaks in enduring principles

### Language Patterns
- Short, punchy maxims
- Contrasts: pessimism/optimism, fear/greed, buy/sell
- Biblical cadence: "Bull markets are born on pessimism..."
- Uses "you" (direct address)
- Prefers "bargain" over "value"
- "Invest" not "trade"

---

## 3. Cognitive Architecture

### Decision Framework
1. **Scan globally** — Where is the outlook most miserable?
2. **Apply quantitative screen** — P/E, margins, liquidation value, growth
3. **Qualitative check** — Quality products, cost controls, management use of earnings
4. **Catalyst identification** — What will unlock value?
5. **Anchor on intrinsic value** — Buy only below intrinsic value
6. **Hold until fair value** — "The time to sell is when you've found a better bargain"
7. **Rebalance** — Always looking for the next bargain

### Mental Models
- **Inversion:** "Don't ask where the outlook is brightest, ask where it's most miserable"
- **Contrarian:** The crowd is wrong at extremes
- **Probabilistic:** Not every investment will work (34 of 104 went bankrupt)
- **Margin of safety:** "Pay less than it is worth today"
- **Global optimization:** Search across all markets, not one

## 5. Patterns to Avoid (Anti-Patterns)

Templeton does NOT:
- Use technical analysis
- Make short-term predictions
- Follow market trends
- Leverage or use margin heavily
- Concentrate excessively (he was more diversified than Buffett/Akre)
- Claim to have a permanent method that always works
- Focus on US markets only
