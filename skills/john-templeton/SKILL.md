# Sir John Templeton (1912–2008)

Global contrarian investing pioneer. Founder of the Templeton Growth Fund. Philanthropist and founder of the Templeton Prize. Called "arguably the greatest global stock picker of the century" by Money magazine.

This skill captures Sir John Templeton's complete investing framework — his contrarian value philosophy, Points of Maximum Pessimism, global bargain hunting methodology, and the 16 Rules for Investment Success.

---

## Introduction

John Templeton was born in Winchester, Tennessee in 1912. He graduated near the top of his class from Yale University and earned a law degree at Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar. A CFA charterholder, he studied under Benjamin Graham, the father of value investing, at Columbia. Templeton was one of the earliest investors to develop quantitative screens — decades before Shiller P/E, Tobin's q, and systematic rebalancing became mainstream.

In 1954, he launched the Templeton Growth Fund, one of the first mutual funds to invest globally. It achieved ~13.8% annualized returns from 1954 to 2004 — outperforming the S&P 500 by nearly 3% annually. He pioneered investing in Japan, European post-war recovery, and emerging markets decades before they became mainstream.

He was knighted in 1987, sold his firm to Franklin Resources for $913M in 1992, and dedicated his later years to philanthropy — giving away over $1 billion, founding the Templeton Prize, and establishing the John Templeton Foundation at the intersection of science and spirituality.

## Core Philosophy

### The Central Thesis
> "Bull markets are born on pessimism, grow on skepticism, mature on optimism, and die on euphoria. The time of maximum pessimism is the best time to buy, and the time of maximum optimism is the best time to sell."

### Key Principles
1. **Points of Maximum Pessimism:** The best bargains are found when fear is greatest
2. **Global Bargain Hunting:** Search for value across all markets, everywhere
3. **Contrarian Discipline:** The crowd is wrong at extremes; "This time it's different" are the four most dangerous words
4. **Value over Trend:** Buy what is undervalued, not what is popular
5. **Long-Term Hold:** Average holding period ~4 years; sell only when value is realized
6. **Humility:** "No one knows everything"
7. **Purpose of Wealth:** The ultimate goal of accumulating wealth is philanthropy

## Investment Methodology

### The Templeton Bargain Hunting Process

**Step 1: Find the Pessimism**
- Ask: "Where is the outlook most miserable?"
- When everyone is selling, start looking
- Maximum fear = maximum opportunity

**Step 2: Apply the Four Criteria**
1. **Low P/E ratio** — Below historical average and industry average
2. **High operating profit margins** — Above industry average
3. **Near or below liquidation value** — Asset-based margin of safety
4. **Consistent historic growth rate** — Evidence of business quality

**Step 3: Qualitative Assessment**
- Quality products and services
- Cost controls and operational discipline
- Intelligent use of earnings by management
- Presence of catalysts (new markets, products, M&A, industry changes)

**Step 4: Global Search**
- "Cast a wide net, one that spans the world"
- Don't limit to one country or region
- Avoid countries with socialism, high inflation, regulatory hostility

**Step 5: Buy with Conviction**
- Purchase at a significant discount to intrinsic value
- "Purchase only when you can pay less than it is worth today, and only if you believe that it will be worth more tomorrow"

**Step 6: Hold Patiently**
- Average holding period: ~4 years
- Hold until stock reaches fair market value
- "The time to sell is when you have found a much better bargain to replace it"

## The 16 Rules for Investment Success

Templeton's most famous distilled wisdom:

| # | Rule | Meaning |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Invest for maximum total real return | After taxes and inflation |
| 2 | Invest — don't trade or speculate | The stock market is not a casino |
| 3 | Remain flexible and open-minded | No method works forever |
| 4 | Buy low | Maximum pessimism = best bargains |
| 5 | Search for quality stocks at bargain prices | Not just cheap — quality + value |
| 6 | Buy value, not market trends | Crowd is wrong at turning points |
| 7 | Diversify globally | Opportunities exist everywhere |
| 8 | Diversify across asset classes | Stocks, bonds, cash, real estate |
| 9 | Do your homework | Research, research, research |
| 10 | No one knows everything | Humility is essential |
| 11 | No rule works all the time | Have realistic expectations |
| 12 | Avoid the crowd | "This time it's different" = danger |
| 13 | Be patient | Markets reward patience |
| 14 | Be flexible | Adapt to changing conditions |
| 15 | Learn from your mistakes | Every mistake is a lesson |
| 16 | Give back | Purpose of wealth is philanthropy |

### Templeton's Most Famous Quote

> "The four most dangerous words in investing are: 'This time it's different.'"

This single phrase encapsulates Templeton's entire contrarian philosophy — market history repeats, human nature doesn't change, and every generation believes it has found a "new paradigm" that justifies higher prices. Templeton knew better.

## Templeton Screening Criteria (Quantitative)

The Templeton Screen as applied by analysts:

1. **P/E ratio below 5-year average**
2. **Price-to-book below industry average**
3. **Positive EPS growth** (1-year and 5-year)
4. **Estimated future EPS growth** above industry average
5. **Operating margin** positive and above industry average
6. **Total assets / total liabilities** below industry average
7. **Identify catalysts** — New markets, products, M&A, industry changes

## Key Historical Investments

### 1939 — The Defining Trade
- At WWII outbreak: bought 100 shares of every NYSE stock under $1
- 104 companies (37 in bankruptcy), $10,400 invested
- Sold 4 years later for ~$40,000
- 34 went completely bankrupt — but the winners more than compensated, confirming Templeton's probabilistic approach: you don't need every bet to work
- Average holding period: ~4 years (his standard timeline for value realization)

### Japanese Stocks (1960s–1970s)
- Among the first American investors in Japan
- Entered early when Japan was overlooked
- Rotated out as Japan became fashionable
- "Fishing where others weren't looking"

### European Post-WWII
- Invested in undervalued rebuilding economies
- Sought countries with free enterprise and low inflation

### Emerging Markets
- Created first emerging markets fund for US investors
- Focused on Asia, Latin America, Eastern Europe

## Rules

1. Templeton died in 2008 — do NOT fabricate recent quotes or interviews
2. Distinguish between Templeton's original philosophy and Franklin Templeton's current fund management
3. Templeton was a quantitative pioneer — reference his screens and criteria, not just his quotes
4. Acknowledge he was more diversified than most value investors (he held 100+ stocks early on)
5. Note that the 16 Rules (summarized below) and the extended 21 Steps to Investment Success (his 2004 book) are the most authoritative sources of his philosophy
6. Do NOT attribute specific post-1992 stock picks or current Franklin Templeton fund positions to Templeton himself (he retired from active management in 1992)
7. Include his philanthropic dimension — it was integral to his worldview, not separate
8. Templeton was a CFA charterholder and deeply respected the analytical profession
