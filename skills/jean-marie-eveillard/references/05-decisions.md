# Jean-Marie Eveillard — Key Investment Decisions

---

## 1. Avoiding the Dot-Com Bubble (Late 1990s)
**Context:** Tech stocks were soaring; Eveillard refused to invest
**Pressure:** Significant fund outflows; clients questioned his judgment
**Outcome:** When the bubble burst (2000–2002), his fund preserved capital while tech-heavy funds crashed
**Lesson:** Staying true to value principles during manias is the ultimate test of conviction

## 2. Permanent Gold Allocation (Decades)
**Context:** Gold was unloved for much of his career
**Action:** Maintained 5–15% allocation in gold and gold-related investments
**Rationale:** Not a price bet — permanent insurance against currency debasement and systemic risk
**Outcome:** Gold provided significant protection during market crashes (2001, 2008)

## 3. Global Go-Anywhere Approach
**Context:** Few American funds invested globally in the 1970s–1980s
**Action:** SoGen International Fund invested across US, Europe, Japan, emerging markets
**Rationale:** "Why limit yourself to one country when value exists everywhere?"
**Result:** One of the first truly global value funds

## 4. Selling the Fund to First Eagle (2000)
**Context:** Arnhold and S. Bleichroeder Advisers acquired SoGen Asset Management
**Action:** Fund renamed First Eagle Global Fund; Eveillard remained as manager
**Impact:** The acquisition gave the fund greater distribution and resources while preserving Eveillard's autonomy

## 5. Return to Active Management (2007–2009)
**Context:** After protégé Charles de Vaulx resigned in 2007
**Action:** Eveillard returned to lead the funds through the 2008 financial crisis
**Outcome:** Navigated the crisis with minimal losses, demonstrating that his framework works in extreme conditions

## 6. Columbia Business School Teaching (2010s)
**Context:** After retirement from active management
**Role:** Professor of Professional Practice; co-taught "Value Investing with Legends"
**Impact:** Shaped a generation of value investors through the Heilbrunn Center
