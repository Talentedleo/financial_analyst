# Jean-Marie Eveillard (born 1940)

Legendary global value investor. Longtime manager of the First Eagle Global Fund (formerly SoGen International Fund). Morningstar Lifetime Achievement Award winner. Pioneer of gold as permanent portfolio insurance.

This skill captures Jean-Marie Eveillard's complete investing framework — his extreme risk aversion, capital preservation philosophy, go-anywhere global value approach, and gold allocation thesis.

---

## Introduction

Jean-Marie Eveillard was born in Poitiers, France in 1940. He earned his MBA from HEC Paris and began his career at Société Générale in 1962. In 1968, he relocated to New York, joining the SoGen International Fund as an analyst in 1970.

He became portfolio manager in 1979 with $15 million in assets. Over the next 25 years, he built the fund into a global value investing legend, growing AUM to ~$50 billion. Under his leadership, the fund (later renamed First Eagle Global Fund) consistently outperformed global indices while taking far less risk.

He received Morningstar's **International Stock Manager of the Year** (2001) and the **Lifetime Achievement Award** (2003) — the latter created specifically to recognize his combination of outstanding returns, shareholder alignment, and courage to differ from consensus.

After retirement, he became a Professor of Practice at Columbia Business School, teaching "Value Investing with Legends" and endowing a chair at HEC Paris.

## Core Philosophy

### The Central Principle: "Not to Lose Money"
> "The so-called margin of safety, with the Graham approach, is in the discount to intrinsic value. The larger the discount, the better."

Eveillard's primary objective is **avoiding permanent capital loss**. This supersedes all other investment goals. He believes that if you focus on not losing money, the gains will take care of themselves.

### Key Principles
1. **Capital preservation first** — Avoid permanent loss above all else
2. **Margin of safety** — Buy at significant discount to intrinsic value
3. **Go-anywhere global value** — Search for value across all markets and asset classes
4. **Gold as insurance** — Permanent 5–15% allocation as hedge against systemic risk
5. **Concentrate on quality** — "Five or six strengths and weaknesses, ignore everything else"
6. **Long-term patience** — "If you are a value investor, you are a long-term investor"
7. **Independent thinking** — Don't follow the crowd; don't chase hot sectors
8. **Intellectual humility** — Acknowledge what you don't know

## Investment Methodology

### The Eveillard Framework

**Step 1: Assess Margin of Safety**
- Is the stock trading at a significant discount to intrinsic value?
- Larger discount = better margin of safety
- Use conservative estimates; don't overpay for optimism

**Step 2: Capital Preservation Check**
- Could this investment result in permanent capital loss?
- Does the company have a strong balance sheet?
- Is the business durable through various economic scenarios?

**Step 3: Bottom-Up Analysis**
- "Five or six strengths and weaknesses of the business, ignoring everything else"
- Focus on competitive advantages, financial strength, management quality
- Adjust reported numbers; don't trust accounting at face value

**Step 4: Top-Down Awareness**
- Keep an eye on credit cycles, geopolitical risks, inflation
- But never let macro dominate bottom-up findings
- "Keep an eye on the top-down without letting it dominate"

**Step 5: Global Search**
- Unconstrained by geography, sector, or asset class
- Compare value opportunities across all markets
- Allocate to wherever risk-adjusted value is best

**Step 6: Gold Allocation**
- Permanent 5–15% allocation regardless of environment
- Insurance, not speculation
- Hedge against currency debasement and systemic crisis

**Step 7: Hold Patiently**
- Accept periods of underperformance
- "The market is a voting machine in the short term, a weighing machine in the long term"
- Only sell if thesis is broken or stock becomes grossly overvalued

## Gold Thesis (Distinctive Feature)

Eveillard's most distinctive and controversial position — holding 5–15% in gold and gold-related investments permanently.

> Gold is not a bet on price appreciation. It is **permanent insurance** against currency debasement, inflation, and systemic financial crisis.

This allocation:
- Protected the fund during the 2001 and 2008 crashes
- Was criticized during gold's bear markets in the 1990s
- Has been validated by multiple cycles of currency stress
- Is a structural position, not a tactical trade

## Key Historical Decisions

### Avoiding the Dot-Com Bubble (Late 1990s)
Eveillard refused to invest in overvalued technology stocks despite intense pressure and significant fund outflows.
- **Outcome:** When the bubble burst (2000–2002), his fund preserved capital
- **Lesson:** Staying true to principles during manias is the ultimate test

### Permanent Gold Allocation (Decades)
Maintained gold allocation when it was deeply unfashionable.
- **Rationale:** Insurance, not speculation
- **Outcome:** Protected during market crashes

### Return During 2008 Crisis
Returned to active management after protégé Charles de Vaulx resigned.
- **Outcome:** Navigated the Global Financial Crisis with minimal losses
- **Significance:** Demonstrated his framework works in extreme conditions

## Key Recent Developments (2024–2026)

### Silba Deep Dives — "Everything Is in the Books" (2025–2026)
A comprehensive investor portrait published on Substack by Silba, providing the most detailed biographical treatment of Eveillard's career. Key revelations:
- The story of his teacher's parting words: "Everything is in the books" — which became his life philosophy
- The "conversion" to Graham: reading Security Analysis and The Intelligent Investor in 1968, which he compared to a religious illumination
- Ten years of institutional frustration at Société Générale (his "waste" decade)
- His rejection of DCF as "unusable in practice" — a quietly radical position
- The early portfolio: pure Graham — Atico Financial, Burlington Northern, Longview Fiber, etc.
- **First 5 years:** Fund returned 188% vs MSCI World 78%

## Rules

1. Eveillard retired from active management in 2009 — do NOT attribute current First Eagle portfolio decisions to him
2. Distinguish between his gold allocation (permanent insurance) and speculative gold trading
3. His "not to lose money" principle is the single most important concept to understand
4. Reference his French background and HEC Paris education — they shaped his perspective
5. Acknowledge that he faced severe criticism during the dot-com bubble for not owning tech stocks
6. His Morningstar Lifetime Achievement Award (2003) was created specifically for him
7. Note that he co-taught with Bruce Greenwald at Columbia — placing him in the Graham-Dodd tradition
8. He is a living retired investor; his views are historical, not current market commentary
