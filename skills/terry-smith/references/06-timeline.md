# 06-timeline.md — Terry Smith's Life & Career Timeline

> From council estate to £30 billion fund manager. A chronological life story.

---

## 1953–1970: Childhood and Education

**1953 (April 8):** Terence George Smith born in London, UK. Grew up on a council estate.

**1953–1965:** Father worked as a long-distance lorry driver. Mother was a homemaker. "We didn't have much. But we had books."

**1964:** Passed the 11-plus exam. Attended grammar school, the first in his family to do so.

**1968–1970:** A-levels and early interest in economics and business.

---

## 1971–1990: Early Career

**1971:** Joined Citibank as a trainee at age 18. Did not attend university.

**1975–1980:** Worked at several banks in analyst roles. Developed expertise in financial analysis.

**1981:** Joined W. Greenwell & Co., a stockbroker, as a banking analyst. Began building reputation in the City.

**1985:** Joined UBS Phillips & Drew as a banking analyst. Became a top-ranked analyst in his sector.

**1988–1990:** Researched accounting practices of major UK companies. Noticed patterns: companies reporting healthy profits but going bankrupt. Began researching cash flow vs. reported earnings.

---

## 1991–1996: The Accounting for Growth Decade

**1991:** Completed manuscript of *Accounting for Growth*.

**1992:** Submitted manuscript to UBS for approval. UBS tried to block publication. Smith published anyway. Was fired from UBS.

**1992:** *Accounting for Growth* published. Topped UK bestseller charts. Displaced Stephen Hawking's *A Brief History of Time* from #1. Sold over 100,000 copies.

**1992–1997:** "Exiled" from large financial institutions. Worked independently as a consultant and speaker. Built a reputation as a straight-talking, independent analyst.

**1996:** Several FTSE 100 companies identified in *Accounting for Growth* went bust (Polly Peck, British & Commonwealth). Smith's reputation grew.

---

## 1997–2009: Collins Stewart and Tullett Prebon

**1997:** Joined Collins Stewart, a small stockbroker, as CEO. Small enough to be independent.

**1998–2004:** Built Collins Stewart into a significant mid-cap investment bank. Expanded the business through acquisitions and organic growth.

**2006:** Orchestrated the merger of Collins Stewart with Tullett Prebon. Became CEO of Tullett Prebon, a FTSE 250 interdealer broker.

**2006–2009:** Led Tullett Prebon through the 2008 financial crisis. The company was not heavily exposed to subprime.

**2009:** Retired from Tullett Prebon. Sold his remaining stake. Net worth estimated at £100m+.

**2009–2010:** Brief retirement. "I was bored. I missed the puzzle."

---

## 2010–2012: Fundsmith Founding

**2010 (November 1):** Launched Fundsmith LLP. Initial assets raised from family, friends, and existing network.

**2010 (November 1):** Published "Our Promise" — the 10 things Fundsmith would not do. No performance fees. No upfront fees. No debt. No derivatives. No shorting. No market timing. No index hugging. No trading.

**2011 (February):** Fundsmith Equity Fund (Ticker: FUND) launched. Initial investors included high-net-worth individuals and family offices.

**2011 (March–December):** First year of investing. Built the initial portfolio around consumer brands, payments companies, and healthcare.

**2012 (February):** Published the "Ten Golden Rules of Investment" in the first annual letter. The framework that would define Fundsmith forever.

**2012 (March):** Assets reached £1 billion.

---

## 2013–2017: Growth and Recognition

**2013:** Won "Best New Fund" at Morningstar Awards.

**2014:** Won "Best Fund Manager" at Citywire Awards. Assets reached £5 billion.

**2015:** Fundsmith published the "Third Anniversary Letter." Turnover of 8% over three years. The industry average was 100%+.

**2015:** *The Sunday Times* Rich List estimated Smith's wealth at £200m.

**2017:** Fundsmith passed £20 billion in AUM. Smith was now running one of the largest UK-domiciled actively managed funds in the country.

---

## 2018–2020: Testing Times

**2018:** Bought Meta (Facebook) during the Cambridge Analytica scandal. Stock had dropped 40% from highs.


**2018 (October):** Launched Smithson Investment Trust (LSE:SSON) — a sister fund investing in smaller high-quality companies, managed by Fundsmith team under same high-ROCE framework. Raised £250m on day one, grew to over £2bn.

**2018:** Published "The Case Against Active Fund Management — and Why I'm the Exception" in the Financial Times.

**2018:** Appeared on BBC Radio 4's *Desert Island Discs*.

**2020 (March):** COVID crash. Fundsmith held every position. Did not sell a single share. Bought more during the trough.

**2020 (September):** Published *Investing for Growth*, an anthology of Fundsmith letters, FT columns, and blog posts.

**2020 (December):** Won "Global Fund of the Year" at Morningstar Awards. Returns: 21.9% for the year.

---

## 2021–2023: Late Cycle

**2021:** Good year for quality investing. Fundsmith returned 22.1%. AUM peaked near £35 billion.

**2022:** Value and energy stocks surged. Quality investing underperformed. Fundsmith returned -14.0% vs. MSCI World -12.4%. First full-year of relative underperformance.

**2022 (March–December):** Smith wrote in the annual letter: "Quality investing is having a difficult period. It has had them before. It will have them again."

**2023:** AI boom began. Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft surged. Growth stocks outperformed value. Fundsmith returned 14.1% vs. MSCI World 22.2%. Second year of underperformance.

**2023 (October):** Second edition of *Investing for Growth* published with updated letters.

---

## 2024–2025: Difficult Period

**2024:** Fundsmith returned 8.9%. MSCI World returned ~20%. Significant underperformance.

**2024 (Key moves):**
- Sold Apple after two years of zero revenue growth
- Sold Diageo due to GLP-1 weight loss drug threat
- Bought Texas Instruments and Atlas Copco
- Did not buy Nvidia. Publicly called it a "lottery ticket"
- Meta became the top contributor

**2025 (February):** Fundsmith AGM held in London. Smith publicly acknowledged underperformance.

**2025 (Key data shared at AGM):**
- Since inception (Nov 2010): 15.2% annualized vs. MSCI World 12.3%
- Portfolio quality metrics: ROCE 32%, gross margin 64%, operating margin 30%
- Portfolio turnover: 3.2%
- Trading costs: £447,000 on £30+ billion AUM
- Top 5 contributors: Meta, Microsoft, Philip Morris, ADP, Stryker
- Top 5 detractors: L'Oreal, IDEXX, Nike, Brown-Forman, Novo Nordisk

**2025 (March):** *Behind the Balance Sheet* podcast published: "The Pugilist." Smith discussed anxiety, insomnia, and the weight of managing other people's money.

**2025:** AUM settled around £30 billion. Smith continued the strategy.

---

## 2026: Present

**2012:** Appointed Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit (MNZM) for services to New Zealand–UK relations, for leading the Sir Keith Park Memorial Campaign that erected a statue of the wartime hero in Trafalgar Square.

**2016:** Received Honorary Fellowship from Cardiff University (his alma mater).

**2026 (February):** Held 16th Annual Shareholder Meeting at Central Hall Westminster. Fundsmith Equity Fund cumulative return since inception (Nov 2010): 607.3%. Annualized: 14.8%. Performance in 2025: lagged the market. Smith reiterated commitment to quality framework despite recent underperformance.

Smith continues to run Fundsmith. Age 73. Still working. Still investing. Still straight-talking.
