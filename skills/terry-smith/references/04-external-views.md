## 1. "The British Warren Buffett"

This is the most common label applied to Smith by the financial media.

**First used:** Around 2010–2012, as Fundsmith gained traction.

**Does Smith like it?** Not really.

> "People call me the British Warren Buffett. I'm not. I'm Terry Smith. I have my own approach." — *Behind the Balance Sheet*, 2025

**Why the comparison is made:**
- Both focus on quality companies with durable competitive advantages
- Both advocate low turnover and long holding periods
- Both are skeptical of technology hype and speculative investing
- Both write accessible, quotable shareholder letters
- Both are outspoken critics of financial industry practices

**Why it's imperfect:**
- Smith is more systematic and metric-driven (strict ROCE thresholds)
- Smith does not run a conglomerate (Berkshire Hathaway model)
- Smith is purely a public equity investor, not a business acquirer
- Buffett uses float from insurance; Smith does not use leverage at all

**Source:** Various financial media comparisons.

## 3. Morningstar Rating

Fundsmith Equity Fund has been rated by Morningstar for many years.

**Rating:** Silver (as of latest assessment)

**Context:**
- Morningstar's analyst rating system: Gold, Silver, Bronze, Neutral, Negative
- Silver indicates "the fund has advantages that clearly differentiate it from competitors"
- Fundsmith previously held a Gold rating but was downgraded after key person changes (lead manager changes count against ratings)

**Morningstar's assessment:**
> "Terry Smith is a proven steward of investor capital with a clear, repeatable investment process. The fund benefits from a disciplined approach and a stable team. However, key-person risk remains a concern given Smith's central role."

**Source:** [Morningstar — Fundsmith Equity Fund](https://www.morningstar.co.uk/)

## 9. Critical Views

### What Critics Say

1. **Key-person risk:** The entire fund depends on Terry Smith. If he retires or becomes incapacitated, what happens?

2. **Style drift concern:** Some argue that recent buys (Texas Instruments, Atlas Copco) suggest a shift away from consumer brands toward industrials.

3. **Tech underweight:** Critics argue Smith missed the largest wealth creation event in history (FAANG stocks) because his process excludes high-growth tech.

4. **Size is a headwind:** With £30+ billion, Fundsmith can only invest in large-cap stocks, limiting opportunity set.

5. **Underperformance persistence:** By 2025, Fundsmith had underperformed for several consecutive years. Critics argue the strategy may have stopped working.

### How Supporters Respond

1. **Process is documented:** The process survives Smith via the documented framework and trained team.
2. **New buys are consistent:** Texas Instruments has ROCE > 30%. Atlas Copco has ROE > 25%. Both fit the process.
3. **Quality over growth:** Microsoft and Meta are in the portfolio. Smith owns quality tech, not speculative tech.
4. **Size is a quality signal:** Large companies with high ROCE are harder to disrupt.
5. **Underperformance is cyclical:** Quality investing has cycles. The long-term record speaks for itself.
