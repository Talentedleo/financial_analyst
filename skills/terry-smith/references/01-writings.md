## 1. Accounting for Growth (1992)

**Link:** [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Accounting-Growth-Terry-Smith/dp/0712656601)

Smith's first book, written while working as an analyst at UBS Phillips & Drew. A bestseller that exposed 12 accounting techniques companies used to misrepresent their financial health.

**Background:** A series of FTSE 100 companies (Polly Peck, British & Commonwealth) went bust despite reporting healthy profits. Smith's analysis showed they had cash flow problems hidden by aggressive accounting.

**Impact:**
- Topped UK bestseller charts, displacing Stephen Hawking's *A Brief History of Time* from #1
- Sold over 100,000 copies
- Got Smith fired from UBS (they tried to block publication)
- Established his reputation as an independent, rigorous analyst

**Core Argument:** Reported profits are not the same as real profits. Cash flow, not earnings, reveals the true health of a business.

## 2. Investing for Growth: How to Make Money by Only Buying the Best Companies in the World (2020, Second Edition 2023)

**Link:** [Amazon UK](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Investing-Growth-Second-buying-companies/dp/1804093734) | [Fundsmith](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/)

An anthology of Smith's investment writing from 2010–2020, covering his Fundsmith letters, Financial Times columns, and blog posts.

**Core philosophy — The Three-Step Framework:**
1. **Buy good companies** — defined by high returns on capital employed (ROCE), strong cash generation, pricing power, and durable competitive advantages
2. **Don't overpay** — valuation matters but quality trumps valuation over long periods
3. **Do nothing** — low turnover, long holding periods, let compounding work

**Key Metrics Smith Uses to Define "Good Companies":**
- **ROCE > 20%** — target portfolio average ~30%
- **Gross margin > 60%** — pricing power and inflation protection
- **Operating margin > 20%**
- **Cash conversion ~100%** — profits are real cash, not accounting fiction
- **Low leverage** — debt/equity < 40% typical
- **Interest cover > 10x** — strong financial cushion

**Performance Context:** From Fundsmith's launch in November 2010 to end of 2024: cumulative return of 607.3% (14.8% annualized) vs. MSCI World's 403.4% (12.1% annualized).

> "His essays are readable, easy to digest and refreshingly light on techno-babble. They will help you understand why some companies have durability while others, ostensibly doing well, suddenly disintegrate." — **Jeff Randall, Sky News presenter**

## 4. Financial Times Columns (Ongoing)

Smith has been a regular FT contributor for years, writing articles such as:

- **"Why I Never Invest in Bank Shares"** (2023) — Banks require excessive leverage; Smith avoids them entirely
- **"Lessons from the Tour de France"** (2012) — Investment is a test of endurance, not speed; the winner sticks with a good strategy

## 6. Key Intellectual Contributions Summarized

1. **Accounting for Growth (1992):** Exposed financial reporting gimmicks; became a landmark text in forensic accounting.

2. **The Three-Step Framework:** "Buy good companies, don't overpay, do nothing" — radically simple and brutally difficult to execute.

3. **High-ROCE Focus:** Smith argued that the single most important metric for identifying quality companies is return on capital employed (ROCE). A 30% ROCE company generating real cash is fundamentally superior to any high-growth, low-ROCE business.

4. **Extreme Low Turnover:** Fundsmith's turnover is typically 5–10% annually. In an industry where 100%+ turnover is standard, Smith proved that "doing nothing" is a competitive advantage.

5. **Fee Revolution:** Fundsmith charges no performance fee, no upfront fee. Smith has been an outspoken critic of the fund industry's fee structure.

6. **No Debt, No Derivatives, No Shorting, No Market Timing, No Index Hugging:** Fundsmith's "Our Promise" — 10 things they don't do, which define their approach as much as what they do.
