## 1. Fundsmith Annual General Meeting — 2025 (Keynote Q&A)

**Context:** Terry Smith and Julian Robbins, the AGM was held in London in early 2025. The full transcript was published by steadycompounding.com. Smith acknowledged a difficult year — 2024 returns of 8.9% vs. MSCI World's ~20%, a significant relative underperformance.

### Key Takeaways

#### On Performance
> "We had a disappointing year in 2024. The portfolio returned 8.9% while the MSCI World returned about 20%. That isn't something we're happy about, but we understand why it happened."

#### On Long-Term Track Record
> "Since inception in November 2010, the annualized return is 15.2% versus 12.3% for the MSCI World. That's a 2.9% annual outperformance. But you have to live through the bad years to get the good ones."

#### Portfolio Quality Metrics (Current)
- ROCE: 32%
- Gross margin: 64%
- Operating margin: 30%
- Cash conversion ratio: 88%
- Interest cover: 27x
- Portfolio turnover: 3.2%

#### On Trading Costs
> "Our total trading costs last year were £447,000. That's 0.007% of assets. Most active funds have costs that are 50 to 100 times higher."

#### On the "Fab Five" Tech Concentration
> "Nvidia alone contributed 21% of the S&P 500's total return last year. That's not investing — that's a lottery ticket. We don't own Nvidia. We probably never will. At 53x earnings, the valuation is absurd."

#### On Selling Apple
> "We sold Apple. The company had two years of zero revenue growth and the share price doubled. That's multiple expansion, not business performance. We don't want to hold stocks where the valuation has run ahead of the business."

#### On Selling Diageo
> "We sold Diageo. The emergence of GLP-1 weight loss drugs changes alcohol consumption patterns. This is a structural change, not a cyclical one. We don't want to fight that trend."

#### On New Buys
> "We bought Texas Instruments and Atlas Copco. Texas Instruments has been around for 90 years, has pricing power in analog chips, and returns on capital above 30%. Atlas Copco has been around since 1873, makes industrial compressors — boring but essential."

#### Top 5 Contributors (2024)
1. Meta Platforms
2. Microsoft
3. Philip Morris International
4. Automatic Data Processing (ADP)
5. Stryker

#### Top 5 Detractors (2024)
1. L'Oreal
2. IDEXX Laboratories
3. Nike
4. Brown-Forman (Jack Daniels)
5. Novo Nordisk

#### On Passive Investing Distortions
> "53% of global assets are now in passive funds. That's unprecedented. When everyone owns the same stocks regardless of price, the market stops being a weighing machine and becomes a popularity contest. That creates opportunities for active managers who can think independently."

#### On Management Incentives (Nike Case)
> "Nike's management compensation peer group didn't include Adidas. How is that possible? If you're allowed to exclude your main competitor, you've designed a compensation system that rewards mediocrity. That's not capitalism — that's rent seeking."

#### On Unilever's CEO Change
> "Hein Schumacher was the right CEO. He understood that Unilever needed to focus on its best brands and stop chasing social purpose. The board sacked him after 18 months. That's not long enough to turn around a company of that size. I'm disappointed."

## 2. Behind the Balance Sheet Podcast — 2025 (Subtitled "The Pugilist")

**Context:** In-depth one-hour interview covering Smith's background, Fundsmith's recent underperformance, and his personal struggles.

### On His Background
> "I grew up in a council estate. My father was a long-distance lorry driver. Nobody in my family had been to university. I passed the 11-plus and went to grammar school. If you'd told me then I'd end up running £30 billion of other people's money, I'd have thought you were insane."

### On Underperformance and Insomnia
> "Of course I lose sleep. Anyone who tells you they don't care about underperformance is lying. But the question is: do you change what you're doing because you had a bad year?"

### On Anxiety
> "I'm stressed most of the time. Not because I'm worried about losing money — I know the portfolio is high quality. I'm stressed because I care. I care about the trust people have placed in us. That's a heavy weight."

### On His Reputation
> "People call me the British Warren Buffett. I'm not. I'm Terry Smith. I have my own approach. I don't try to copy anyone."

### On Investing Philosophy
> "The three-step process is simple: buy good companies, don't overpay, do nothing. It's not complicated. What's hard is having the conviction to stick with it when your style is out of fashion. And right now, quality investing is out of fashion."

**Source:** [Behind the Balance Sheet — The Pugilist (2025)](https://behindthebalancesheet.substack.com/)

## 3. Financial Times Columns — Selected Quotes

### "Why I Never Invest in Bank Shares" (2023)
> "Banks are leveraged 20:1. That means a 5% loss on their assets wipes out all equity. How can you invest in a business where the downside is total loss and the upside is a 12% ROE? The math doesn't work."

### "Lessons from the Tour de France" (2012)
> "Investment is a test of endurance, not speed. The Tour de France is won by the rider who can sustain a high average speed over three weeks, not the one who wins the fastest sprint. Fundsmith's approach is the same: consistent compounding over decades, not dramatic outperformance over quarters."

### "The Case Against Active Fund Management — and Why I'm the Exception" (2018)
> "Most active managers fail for one simple reason: they don't have a repeatable process. They chase performance. They trade too much. They try to time the market. Fundsmith has one process that we've followed since day one. It has never changed."

### "Share Buybacks Are Not Evil" (2019)
> "When a great company buys back its own shares at a reasonable price, it creates enormous value for remaining shareholders. The demonization of buybacks is economically illiterate. It's the most tax-efficient way to return capital to shareholders."

**Source:** [Financial Times — Terry Smith columns](https://www.ft.com/)

## 4. Bloomberg Television Interviews

### On Inflation (2022)
> "The best protection against inflation is pricing power. If you own companies that can raise prices without losing customers, inflation is actually good for you. That's why we focus on high gross margins — they're direct evidence of pricing power."

### On Fundsmith's Fee Structure (2018)
> "We charge 0.9% and no performance fee. That's it. No entry fee, no exit fee, no hidden charges. The industry average is 1.5% plus 20% of everything above a benchmark. That's theft dressed up as performance."

### On Active vs. Passive Debate (2023)
> "I've been saying for years that most active managers add no value. The data is clear. But at extremes — like 53% of assets in passive — the system breaks. Markets need price discovery. If nobody is doing fundamental analysis, prices become disconnected from reality."

**Sources:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/), YouTube clips.

## 5. Fundsmith Annual Shareholder Letters (2011–2025)

### Selected Quotes by Year

**2012 — Introducing the Ten Golden Rules:**
> "We have ten rules that govern everything we do. They are non-negotiable. If a potential investment violates even one of them, we walk away."

**2015 — On Patience:**
> "The single greatest advantage any investor can have is patience. Not intelligence. Not information. Patience."

**2018 — On High ROCE:**
> "A business with a 30% ROCE that cannot easily be replicated is worth more than a business with a 15% ROCE growing at twice the speed. Compounding 30% over 20 years produces a result so large that most people cannot visualize it."

**2020 — On COVID Volatility:**
> "We held every position through the crash. We did not sell a single share during the panic. We bought more of the companies we own at lower prices. This is not bravery — this is process. The process says: buy good companies, don't overpay, do nothing."

**2022 — On Value vs. Growth:**
> "Quality investing is having a difficult period. That's fine. It has had difficult periods before. It will have them again. The question is whether the underlying businesses have changed. They have not. The ROCEs are still above 25%. The margins are still high. The cash is still coming in."

**2024 — On Underperformance:**
> "We underperformed the market significantly in 2024. We know this. We accept it. We will not change our process because of one bad year. The businesses in this portfolio are, on average, the highest quality in the world. Over time, that quality wins."

**Source:** [Fundsmith.co.uk — Documents](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/)
