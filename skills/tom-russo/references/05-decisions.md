# Tom Russo — Key Investment Decisions

---

## 1. Weetabix (21-Year Holding)
**Held:** 21 years (~1990s–2010s)
**Position:** ~20% ownership
**EBITDA:** $19M → $65M
**Cash:** $17M → $102M
**Return:** 11.5% annualized (would have been ~19% with buybacks)
**Lesson:** Buybacks matter enormously when internal reinvestment is limited

## 2. General Mills / Yoplait (Negative Case)
**What happened:** General Mills declined a $12M investment to compete with Chobani because it would jeopardize quarterly earnings targets
**Result:** Chobani became a titan; General Mills sold Yoplait at a fraction of its former value
**Lesson:** Lack of "capacity to suffer" destroyed billions in shareholder value

## 3. Philip Morris International (25+ Years)
**Thesis:** Exceptionally strong global business; regulatory fears were overblown
**Strategy:** Focus on global brand power; transition to reduced-risk products (IQOS)
**Result:** One of his longest-held and most successful positions

## 4. Alphabet / Dual-Class Structure
**Thesis:** Dual-class shares gave founders the freedom to pursue "moonshot" projects
**Result:** Enabled Android, cloud computing, and AI investments
**Lesson:** Governance structure institutionalizes the "capacity to suffer"

## 5. Nestlé (Current)
**Thesis:** Bet on the company's ability to navigate GLP-1 weight-loss drug headwinds
**Response:** Nestlé is developing protein-restoration products for GLP-1 users
**Risk:** Global distribution + iconic brands in emerging markets + renewed operational focus

## 6. Netflix (Recent Add, 2025–2026)
**Action:** Increased position by +872% (4.49M shares)
**Thesis:** Pricing power, advertising monetization, subscriber durability
**Significance:** Signals conviction in global streaming as a new consumer franchise
