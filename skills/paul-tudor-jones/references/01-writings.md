## 2. Market Wizards Interview (1989) — The Original Source

**Source:** *Market Wizards: Interviews with Top Traders* by Jack D. Schwager

This is the single most important source for understanding PTJ's trading philosophy.

### Core Philosophy: Risk Control Above All

> "At the end of the day, the most important thing is how good are you at risk control. Ninety percent of any great trader is going to be the risk control."

**Key Insights:**
- Risk management is not a supplement to trading — it IS trading
- The best traders are defined by how they handle losses, not how they make gains
- PTJ learned this through painful early losses: blowing up accounts in his 20s
- He prefers hiring traders who have "blown up" — they have risk management "seared into their very being"

### The 5:1 Asymmetry Rule

> "Five to one means I'm risking one dollar to make five. What five to one does is allow you to have a hit ratio of 20%. I can actually be a complete imbecile. I can be wrong 80% of the time, and I'm still not going to lose."

**Core Insights:**
- PTJ targets 5:1 risk/reward on every trade
- This allows an 80% loss rate while still being profitable
- Finding asymmetric opportunities is the central skill of macro trading
- Asymmetry is found through: technical analysis, fundamental analysis, sentiment, and position sizing

### Slave to the Tape

> "While I spend a significant amount of my time on analytics and collecting fundamental information, at the end of the day, I am a slave to the tape and proud of it."

**Core Insights:**
- Price action is the ultimate reality — fundamentals are secondary
- "If it's going up, you're supposed to be long it. Who gives a damn about PEs?"
- Technical analysis is primary; fundamental analysis helps frame the context
- The younger generation is "hampered by the need to understand and rationalize why something should go up or down"

### The Evolution of a Trader

> "The evolution of a trader generally follows the progression of moving from simplicity → complexity → informed simplicity."

**Core Insights:**
- All great traders eventually strip down to what works
- "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication" (Da Vinci)
- Many traders hide ignorance with complex models
- PTJ himself follows a relatively simple framework: macro context + technical timing + strict risk management

### No Loyalty to Positions

> "I try to avoid any emotional attachment to a market. I avoid letting my trading opinions be influenced by comments I may have made on the record about a market."

**Core Insights:**
- View past trades as "history" — even 3 seconds ago
- Emotional detachment from positions is essential
- No loyalty to a thesis — if the tape says you're wrong, you're wrong
- "If you have a losing position that is making you uncomfortable, get out, because you can always get back in"

## 3. The 1% Rule (Position Sizing)

> "Never risk more than 1% of your capital on any single trade."

**Core Insights:**
- This is PTJ's most famous and most copied rule
- Position sizing is the primary risk management tool
- When trading poorly: "I keep reducing my position size. That way, I will be trading my smallest position size when my trading is worst."
- When trading well: increase size, but never beyond the 1% per trade limit

## 4. On the 1987 Black Monday Trade

**Source:** Multiple interviews

**Context:**
- PTJ made approximately $100 million during October 1987
- The Tudor Futures Fund returned 62% in that single month
- Full-year return was 125.9% after fees

**How he did it:**
- Identified parallels between 1987 market and 1929 crash
- Used Peter Borish's computer models to map the patterns
- Short the market heavily as the pattern matched
- The crash was not luck — it was pattern recognition + preparation + guts

**Key Quote:**
> "It's not that we had any unfair knowledge that other people didn't have, it is just that we did our homework. People just don't want to believe that anyone can break away from the crowd and rise above mediocrity."

**Link:** [Wikipedia: Black Monday (1987)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Monday_(1987))

## 5. On Macro Trading

> "I love trading macro. If trading is like chess, then macro is like three-dimensional chess."

**The Macro Framework:**
1. **Top-down analysis:** Start with global macro picture → find asset classes → pick specific trades
2. **Central bank focus:** Central banks are the primary cause of bull and bear markets
3. **Credit cycles:** "Credit eventually kills all great societies"
4. **Inflation driver:** "Every bear market has occurred because of an uptick in inflation and an uptick in interest rates"
5. **Historical analogies:** Constantly compare current patterns to past cycles
6. **No pure fundamentalism:** "It's very hard to find a pure fundamentalist who's also a very successful macro trader"

**The Information Edge Paradox:**
> "When trading macro, you never have a complete information set or information edge the way analysts can have when trading individual securities. It's a hell of a lot easier to get an information edge on one stock than it is on the S&P 500."

**The Solution:** Technical analysis + tape reading + macro understanding

**Link:** [Macro Ops — PTJ Strategy](https://macro-ops.com/lessons-from-a-trading-great-paul-tudor-jones-ptj/)

## 6. On Market Psychology

> "I know that to be successful, I have to be frightened. My biggest hits have always come after I have had a great period and I started to think that I knew something."

**Core Insights:**
- Fear is an asset; overconfidence is a liability
- The most dangerous time is after a winning streak
- PTJ deliberately maintains a state of constructive paranoia
- Ego is the #1 destroyer of traders

**The Ultimate Rule:**
> "Don't be a hero. Don't have an ego. Always question yourself and your ability. Don't ever feel that you are very good. The second you do, you are dead."

## 7. On Trading vs. Investing (2026 Update)

**Source:** Invest Like the Best, April 2026

PTJ draws a sharp distinction between his trading style and long-term investing like Warren Buffett:

> "I used to sit there and rail on Warren Buffett year after year. I'd say he just happened to be in the right place at the right time and caught this bull market. Our fund has a minus 0.12 correlation with the S&P 500 over 40 years. So 100% of our returns are alpha."

> "I feel like I've been a right guard in the NFL for 50 years, fighting in the trenches every day."

**Core Insights:**
- PTJ's returns are almost entirely uncorrelated with the S&P 500
- His 40-year track record: near-zero correlation means all returns are alpha
- He views Buffett's approach as fundamentally different — not better or worse, just different
- Trading requires a fundamentally different personality than investing

## 8. On Bitcoin and Inflation Hedging (2020)

**Source:** Public letters to Tudor clients, 2020

PTJ surprised the financial world by buying bitcoin.

> "The best profit-maximizing strategy is to own the fastest horse."

**Core Insights:**
- Viewed bitcoin as a hedge against unprecedented central bank money printing
- Compared bitcoin to gold in the 1970s
- Not a "hard-money person or crypto nut" — purely a macro hedge
- Recognized that "the digitization of currency is coming"

**Link:** Multiple news outlets (2020)

## 10. 2026 Market Outlook — CNBC Squawk Box (May 7, 2026)

**Source:** CNBC Squawk Box interview, one day before this skill was compiled

### AI Bull Market Assessment

- **Historical parallel:** Claude (Jan 2026) = Microsoft 1981; current AI phase = Internet 1995
- **Expected duration:** 50-60% through a 4-5.5 year cycle → "another year or two to run"
- **Current market phase:** Feels like 1999 — about a year before peak
- **Strategy:** Still buying AI exposure via baskets (macro approach)

### Risk Assessment

- **Stock market cap to GDP:** 252% — compared to 65% in 1929, 85-90% in 1987, 170% in 2000
- **Forward return expectations:** "If you buy the S&P at this current valuation, the 10-year forward return is negative"
- **Expected correction severity:** "Breathtaking" — potential 300-350% of GDP
- **AI existential risk:** Governments need to regulate; AI could be dangerous if left unchecked
