# 05-decisions.md — Paul Tudor Jones's Key Decisions

---

## 1. Predicting Black Monday 1987

**Context:** The stock market was rising. Most analysts were bullish. PTJ saw a pattern.

**The Decision:** Heavy short positions in the months before October 1987.

**The Logic:**
- Peter Borish's computer models mapped 1987 market against 1929 crash
- The pattern was eerily similar
- PTJ trusted the data, even though it contradicted the consensus
- "It's not that we had any unfair knowledge — it's just that we did our homework"

**The Result:**
- Tudor Futures Fund: +62% in October alone
- Full-year return: 125.9% after fees
- Estimated $100 million profit
- Made PTJ a legend at age 33

**The Lesson:** Pattern recognition + preparation + conviction = extraordinary results.

---

## 2. Founding Tudor Investment Corporation (1980)

**Context:** PTJ was 26 years old, a floor trader at the New York Cotton Exchange.

**The Decision:** Left floor trading to start his own firm.

**The Logic:**
- Had proven himself as a floor trader (only one losing month in 3.5 years)
- Wanted to trade larger size across more markets
- Had backing from his mentor Dunavant and Tullis as first clients
- Commodities Corporation gave him $30,000 to manage

**The Result:**
- Tudor Investment Corporation founded in 1980
- Grew to manage $12 billion (as of 2022)
- PTJ remains CEO and CIO

---

## 3. Co-Founding the Robin Hood Foundation (1988)

**Context:** After the 1987 crash, PTJ was wealthy but wanted to do more.

**The Decision:** Co-founded Robin Hood Foundation to fight poverty in New York City.

**The Logic:**
- Inspired by a 60 Minutes episode about Eugene Lang's "I Have a Dream" program
- Initially adopted a sixth-grade class in Bedford-Stuyvesant — only 33% graduated
- Realized he "vastly underestimated both the academic and social challenges"
- Decided to build a professional organization, not just write checks

**The Result:**
- One of the most respected anti-poverty organizations in the US
- Hundreds of millions raised annually
- Became a model for hedge fund philanthropy

---

## 4. Betting on Bitcoin (2020)

**Context:** The Fed was printing unprecedented amounts of money during COVID-19.

**The Decision:** Bought bitcoin as an inflation hedge.

**The Logic:**
- "The best profit-maximizing strategy is to own the fastest horse"
- Viewed bitcoin as gold in the 1970s
- "The digitization of currency is coming and COVID-19 is speeding this up"
- Not ideological — purely a macro hedge

**The Result:**
- Significant profits as bitcoin surged in 2020-2021
- Influenced other major investors to consider crypto
- But warned it was a trade, not a permanent allocation

---

## 5. The $800,000 SEC Settlement (1994)

**Context:** Tudor Investment Corporation was accused of violating the uptick rule.

**The Decision:** Settled without admitting or denying wrongdoing.

**The Result:**
- Paid $800,000 fine (second highest at the time)
- Avoided prolonged legal battle
- Continued business without interruption

---

## 6. Co-Founding JUST Capital (2013)

**Context:** PTJ wanted to use market forces to improve corporate behavior.

**The Decision:** Founded JUST Capital, which ranks public companies on social and environmental metrics.

**The Logic:**
- Natural extension of his philanthropy work
- "What gets measured gets managed"
- Market-based solution to social problems

**The Result:**
- JUST Capital became a respected voice in ESG investing
- Publishes annual rankings of America's most JUST companies
- Influenced corporate disclosure practices

---

## 7. Moving to More Conservative Trading (2000s)

**Context:** Returns "dimmed" after the early 2000s.

**The Decision:** Deliberately traded more conservatively.

**The Logic:**
- Fewer big interest-rate and currency moves as central banks kept rates near zero
- More competition as hedge fund industry mushroomed
- PTJ prioritized capital preservation over maximum returns

**The Result:**
- Lower returns but also lower volatility
- The fund survived the 2008 crisis
- Continued to generate alpha with near-zero correlation to S&P 500

---

## 8. 2026 AI Bull Market Call

**Context:** AI stocks surging; many saying it's a bubble.

**The Decision:** Bought more AI exposure — but with a clear view that a "breathtaking correction" will come.

**The Logic:**
- AI = Microsoft in 1981 + Internet in 1995
- "Another year or two to run" in the bull market
- Feels like 1999 — still room but the end will be sharp
- "I'm a macro trader, so I just buy baskets"

**The Result:**
- TBD — but shows PTJ's adaptability after 45+ years

## Key Decision Patterns

1. **Pattern recognition:** Every major decision is based on historical analogies
2. **Conviction with flexibility:** Strong views, loosely held. Can change instantly.
3. **Risk first:** Every decision considers worst-case scenario before best-case
4. **Asymmetric bets:** Small risk with potential for large reward
5. **Adapt or die:** PTJ has evolved from cotton futures to macro to AI in 45 years
