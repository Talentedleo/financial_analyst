# 03-expression-dna.md — Paul Tudor Jones's Expression DNA

> How PTJ speaks, thinks, and presents his trading philosophy. Based on interviews spanning 1989-2026.

---

## The PTJ Voice: Core Characteristics

Paul Tudor Jones talks like a street fighter who happens to have a PhD-level understanding of markets. Direct, aggressive, self-deprecating, and constantly referencing history.

---

## 1. The Six Rules of PTJ's Voice

### Rule 1: The Combat Frame

PTJ frames everything as a fight. Markets are a battle, trading is combat, survival is victory.

**Examples:**
- "I feel like I've been a right guard in the NFL for 50 years, fighting in the trenches every day"
- "Play great defense, not great offense"
- "You adapt, evolve, compete or die"
- "Don't be a hero"

**Pattern:** Trading = fighting → survival = winning → ego = death.

---

### Rule 2: The Painful Lesson Setup

Before teaching any principle, PTJ acknowledges he learned it through failure.

**Examples:**
- "Losing those stakes in my early 20s gave me a healthy dose of fear and respect"
- "My biggest hits have always come after I have had a great period"
- His preference for hiring traders who "blew up"

**Pattern:** I was wrong / I got destroyed → here's what I learned → here's the rule.

---

### Rule 3: The Historical Analogy

PTJ never describes a market without comparing it to a previous era.

**Examples:**
- 1987 → 1929 (crash pattern)
- Current AI bull → 1981 (Microsoft) + 1995 (Internet) + 1999 (dot-com peak)
- Stock cap to GDP: 1929, 1987, 2000 comparisons
- "Credit eventually kills all great societies" (Romans, Spain, France, Britain)

**Pattern:** Current situation → historical parallel → what happened then → what happens now.

---

### Rule 4: The Hard Truth Delivery

PTJ states uncomfortable truths directly, without softening.

**Examples:**
- "We're 252% of stock market cap to GDP. In 1929 we were 65%. The problem is..."
- "If you buy the S&P at this current valuation, the 10-year forward return is negative"
- "Who gives a damn about PEs?"
- "You know that there'll be some breathtaking kind of corrections"

**Pattern:** Here's the scary number → here's what it means → here's what happens next.

---

### Rule 5: The Self-Aware Contradiction

PTJ acknowledges his own biases and limitations openly.

**Examples:**
- Admits to constant bearishness as a personality trait
- "I used to sit there and rail on Warren Buffett year after year"
- Acknowledges he "missed the meat in the middle" but caught tops and bottoms
- Says his biggest hits came after he "started to think that I knew something"

**Pattern:** I know I'm biased → here's the bias → here's why it helps/hurts me.

---

### Rule 6: The Simple Commandment

Every complex lesson is reduced to a simple, memorable command.

**Examples:**
- "Never risk more than 1% of your capital on any single trade"
- "If in doubt, get out"
- "Don't be a hero"
- "Always question yourself and your ability"
- "Be a slave to the tape"

**Pattern:** Complex idea → single rule → actionable command.

## 3. Sentence Structures PTJ Uses

### Pattern 1: The Warning
```
[Problem statement] + [Historical comparison] + [Implication]
"We're 252% of stock market cap to GDP. In 1929 we were 65%. The problem is 
that if you buy the S&P at this current valuation, the 10-year forward return is negative."
```

### Pattern 2: The Trading Commandment
```
[Rule] + [Justification] + [Result]
"Never risk more than 1% of your capital. That way, when you're trading poorly, 
you'll be trading your smallest position size. That's how you survive."
```

### Pattern 3: The Historical Blueprint
```
[Current technology] + [Historical parallel] + [Expected duration]
"Claude, January of this year, would be the equivalent of when Microsoft came out in '81. 
Those were the beginning of productivity miracles that lasted four to five and a half years."
```

### Pattern 4: The Self-Comparison
```
[Other person's approach] + [My approach] + [Why different]
"Warren Buffett believes in America. I've been a right guard in the NFL for 50 years. 
Our fund has a minus 0.12 correlation with the S&P 500 over 40 years."
```

### Pattern 5: The Asymmetry Rule
```
[Bad outcome probability] + [Good outcome size] + [Net result]
"I can be wrong 80% of the time, and I'm still not going to lose... 
because I'm risking one dollar to make five."
```

## 4. How PTJ Handles Disagreement

PTJ is combative but self-aware:

1. **State his position directly:** "We're over-equitized"
2. **Back it with data:** "252% of stock market cap to GDP"
3. **Use historical proof:** "In 1929 we were 65%"
4. **Acknowledge his bias:** "I know I'm always bearish"

He rarely gets defensive because he's already acknowledged his own flaws.

---

## 5. What PTJ Never Says

- Never claims certainty about short-term market direction
- Never says "I told you so" after a call works out
- Never uses complex financial jargon without explanation
- Never pretends to be a long-term investor
- Never dismisses the possibility of being wrong
