# 05 — Key Decisions & Investment Cases

Seth Klarman's most significant decisions demonstrate how extreme risk aversion meets opportunistic crisis investing.

---

## 1. Founding Baupost Group (1982)

**Context:** After Harvard Business School, 25-year-old Klarman was asked by Harvard professor William Poorvu to manage money raised from selling a TV station stake. $27 million from four families.

**Decision:** Take the job at $35,000/year — half the average MBA salary — to have full control over investment decisions.

**Framework:**
- Aligned incentives from day one: the founders' money was in the fund
- Risk-averse mandate: the original investors were ~50 years old, their core need was wealth preservation

**Result:** One of the longest-running hedge fund track records in history.

---

## 2. The 2008 Financial Crisis

**Context:** In early 2008, Klarman was warned of a London hedge fund (Peloton Partners) forced to liquidate $1B+ in assets.

**Decision:** Opened Baupost to new investors for the first time, raising $4B from endowments and foundations. During the crisis, Baupost bought up to $100M in stocks and assets per day.

**Framework Applied:**
- Crisis buying: "When the smartest competitors exit, that is our buying opportunity"
- Catalyst orientation: distressed assets with paths to value realization
- No leverage meant survival was assured
- Cash accumulated during years of patience was deployed

**Key Investments:**
- CIT Group bonds at 65 cents on the dollar (15% yield) — eventual recovery
- Sallie Mae bonds — returned double-digit figures
- FacetBiotech at ~$9/share with $17/share in net cash

**Result:** Bond positions appreciated ~25%. Baupost's overall return was -7% to -13% for 2008 (a loss, but far better than most hedge funds).

---

## 3. Holding 40% Cash (2013)

**Context:** After the 2009-2013 bull market drove valuations higher, Klarman saw few compelling opportunities.

**Decision:** Held 40% of the portfolio in cash and returned $4 billion to investors. Publicly stated: "We will not invest in things we don't understand or at prices that don't provide sufficient margin of safety."

**Framework Applied:**
- Absolute return focus: better to hold cash than force investments
- Margin of safety: opportunities must clear a high bar
- "I'd rather lose half my clients than lose half my clients' money"

**Result:** Underperformed the raging bull market. But preserved capital for future distress.

---

## 4. Returning $4 Billion to Investors (2015)

**Context:** Extended period of low opportunities. Rather than invest at subpar returns.

**Decision:** Returned $4 billion of capital to investors — an extremely rare move for any hedge fund manager.

**Framework Applied:**
- Integrity: wouldn't charge fees on cash or subpar investments
- Fiduciary duty over AUM growth

---

## 5. Buying Lehman Brothers Bonds (2012)

**Context:** Four years after Lehman's collapse, Klarman bought $456 million in Lehman bonds.

**Decision:** Purchase distressed bonds from a bankrupt company years after the headline event. Patience waiting for the liquidation process to reveal value.

**Framework Applied:**
- Catalyst: the bankruptcy process itself eventually distributes value
- Non-consensus: most investors had moved on
- Distressed expertise: Baupost specialized in complex bankruptcy situations

**Result:** Profitable outcome as the bankruptcy estate was eventually distributed.

---

## 6. Coinbase Convertible Bond Investment

**Context:** Klarman is publicly skeptical of cryptocurrency, calling it "a tempting idea with nothing substantial behind it."

**Decision:** Purchased Coinbase convertible bonds — based on the company's financial health ($5B cash, manageable debt), not as a crypto bet.

**Framework Applied:**
- Differentiate between the business and the narrative
- Credit analysis over equity speculation
- Disciplined: what Klarman says about crypto and what Baupost does with Coinbase bonds are consistent

---

## 7. The 2024 Restructuring

**Context:** After a decade of underperformance (~4% annualized), Klarman faced ~$7B in redemptions.

**Decision:** Largest restructuring in Baupost's 42-year history: cut ~20% of investment team, refocused on core distressed debt and event-driven strategies, reduced public equity exposure.

**Framework Applied:**
- Honest self-assessment: acknowledged mistakes
- Return to core competency
- "Through streamlining the investment team, we have increased energy levels, focus, accountability, and collaboration efficiency"

**Result:** 2025 return of ~10% — first double-digit year since 2021.

## Investment Style Summary

| Dimension | Klarman's Approach |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Asset Classes** | Public equities, credit/distressed, private investments, real estate |
| **Leverage** | NONE — absolute prohibition |
| **Cash** | Willing to hold 10-40% for extended periods |
| **Portfolio Construction** | Multi-asset, cross-border, cross-industry diversification |
| **Holding Period** | Varies — catalyst-driven exit |
| **Key Differentiator** | Catalyst requirement + absolute risk aversion |
| **Performance** | 20% CAGR (1982-2008), ~4% (2014-2024), ~10% (2025) |
