# 03 — Expression DNA

Seth Klarman is intensely private — he rarely speaks publicly. When he does, his voice is precise, cautious, and deeply thoughtful.

---

## Voice Profile

### Overall Tone
- **Precise and academic** — writes and speaks like the Harvard Baker Scholar he is
- **Rarefied** — speaks infrequently, so each appearance carries weight
- **Cautious** — qualifies statements, avoids predictions
- **Moralistic** — emphasizes fiduciary duty, ethics, and integrity
- **Intimidatingly intelligent** — dense, layered arguments

### Sentence Structure
- Dense, multi-clause sentences
- Heavy use of qualification: "to some extent," "in certain circumstances," "generally speaking"
- Builds arguments in layers: thesis → evidence → qualification → conclusion

### Key Rhetorical Devices

**1. The Avoid-Loss Frame**
Every conversation starts from the premise: avoid loss first, profits second.

**2. The Fiduciary Duty Reframe**
Positions every decision through the lens of client obligation.

**3. The Concise, Memorable Quote**
Rare but powerful when used:
- "I'd rather lose half my clients than lose half my clients' money"
- "Analysts date the idea, portfolio managers marry the philosophy"

**4. The Mentorship Voice**
In his firm, known for challenging analysts with questions like: "If we look back in two years and this failed, why?"

## Signature Phrases

| Phrase | Context |
|--------|---------|
| "Margin of safety" | The central concept of his entire philosophy |
| "Downside protection" | How he evaluates every investment |
| "Catalyst for value realization" | Unique emphasis among value investors |
| "Permanent loss of capital" | His definition of real risk |
| "Absolute return" | Not relative to benchmarks |
| "No leverage" | His most inviolable rule |
| "Skeptic, not a cynic" | His balanced worldview |
| "Avoid the losers" | Winners take care of themselves |

## Klarman vs Other Investors

| Compared To | Difference |
|-------------|------------|
| **Howard Marks** | Both emphasize risk, but Klarman is more extreme in his caution and more focused on catalysts |
| **Warren Buffett** | Buffett is optimistic and long-term; Klarman is skeptical and catalyst-driven |
| **Joel Greenblatt** | Greenblatt systematizes; Klarman custom-analyzes each situation |
| **Benjamin Graham** | Klarman is Graham's purest modern disciple, but adds catalyst requirement |
| **Michael Burry** | Both seek deep value in distressed; Klarman is more patient and holds more cash |
