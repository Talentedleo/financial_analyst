# 01 — Writings

Seth Klarman is famously reclusive — he has written only one book, does not publish public memos or letters, and rarely gives interviews. However, that single book — *Margin of Safety* — is considered one of the most important texts in value investing. It is out of print and sells for thousands of dollars on the secondary market.

---

## Books

### Margin of Safety: Risk-Averse Value Investing Strategies for the Thoughtful Investor (1991)

**Publisher:** HarperBusiness (1991) — now out of print  
**Amazon (collectible):** Sells for $1,000-$2,500+  
**PDF scans:** Widely circulated among value investors

Klarman's only book. Written after the 1987 crash and during the S&L crisis, it distills the investment philosophy that has guided Baupost Group for over 40 years.

**Core Framework:**

The book is organized around three pillars:

**Part I: Most Investors Would Be Better Off in Index Funds**
- Most active managers underperform
- The stock market is highly efficient for large, well-followed stocks
- Investors are their own worst enemy — emotional decision-making destroys returns
- The first step: admit you probably can't beat the market

**Part II: The Philosophy of Value Investing**
- The defining characteristic of value investing: buying assets at a discount from their underlying value
- Margin of safety is the central concept — a buffer against uncertainty and error
- Risk is not volatility — risk is the probability of permanent loss
- The relationship between price and value is the only thing that matters
- A focus on absolute returns, not relative returns (don't benchmark-hug)

**Part III: The Practice of Value Investing**
- Areas of opportunity: spinoffs, restructurings, distressed securities, liquidations
- The importance of catalysts — events that close the gap between price and value
- How institutional constraints create opportunities for patient capital
- Portfolio management and risk control

**Key Concepts:**

**1. Margin of Safety (The Core Idea)**
> "Margin of safety is the central concept of value investing — the buffer that allows you to be wrong and still survive."

Klarman extends Graham's concept:
- It's not a formula — it's a state of mind
- The margin must be large enough to absorb errors in valuation, adverse business developments, and market volatility
- "We build our portfolio always focused on downside protection"

**2. Risk = Permanent Loss, Not Volatility**
> "Risk is not a number. Risk is the probability of permanent loss."

- Academics define risk as beta (volatility) — Klarman calls this "intellectual fraud"
- Real risk comes from: overpaying, using leverage, lack of diversification, investing in things you don't understand
- "The greatest risk is the risk of being wrong and being wiped out"

**3. The Catalyst Principle**
Klarman is unique among value investors in emphasizing catalysts:
- A catalyst is an event that causes the gap between price and value to close
- Examples: spinoffs, liquidations, share buybacks, takeovers, activist pressure
- Without a catalyst, the discount could persist or widen indefinitely
- Catalysts reduce risk by creating a mechanism for value realization independent of market forces

**4. Absolute vs. Relative Performance**
- Baupost focuses on absolute returns, not relative to benchmarks
- This means holding cash when no opportunities exist, even if that means underperforming in a bull market
- "I'd rather lose half my clients than half my clients' money"

**5. The "Easy Game" (Similar to Greenblatt)**
- Institutional constraints (mandates, benchmarks, liquidity requirements) create predictable mispricings
- Forced selling due to redemptions creates opportunities
- Small, complex, unpopular securities are the most fertile ground

**6. No Leverage**
Klarman's most distinctive rule: Baupost never uses leverage.
- Leverage amplifies losses and forces liquidation at the worst possible time
- No leverage = you survive any market meltdown
- "If you avoid the losers, the winners take care of themselves"

**Key Quotes:**
> "A value investor's primary goal is to avoid loss."

> "The value investor is a skeptic, not a cynic."

> "The stock market is the story of cycles and of human behavior. Good investment decisions generate good investment outcomes only if all other things are equal. They often are not."

> "Investment success requires a temperament that is able to both ignore the crowd's behavior and think independently."

> "If you buy a dollar's worth of assets for 50 cents, you're going to make money — it just may take some time."

## Investment Letters

Klarman writes detailed quarterly letters to Baupost investors, but these are private and not publicly available. Summaries occasionally appear in financial media.

**Known Letter Themes (various years):**

| Year | Theme |
|------|-------|
| 2007 | Warning about excessive risk-taking, holding large cash position |
| 2008 | "A fortuitous time" — buying aggressively during the crisis |
| 2013 | Caution on frothy markets, held 40% cash |
| 2015 | Returned $4 billion to investors — "few opportunities worth pursuing" |
| 2017 | Warning about cryptocurrency speculation ("I beg to differ" similar to Marks) |
| 2019-2020 | COVID opportunity — deployed capital into distressed credit |
| 2023 | Raised distressed exposure to ~24% of AUM |
| 2023 | Commercial real estate: "The deadlock is beginning to crack. There's a wall of debt maturities coming due." — Increased distressed exposure to ~24% |
| 2024 | Baupost reduced investment team by ~20% in largest-ever restructuring; refocused on core strategies: distressed debt, event-driven equities, private investments |
| 2025 (Jan) | Annualized return 2014-2024: ~4% — far below historical 20%. Clients redeemed ~$7B over 3 years |
| 2025 (Jul) | Goldman Sachs interview — 10% cash, higher margin of safety required, portfolio breakdown shared publicly |
| 2025 (Dec) | Baupost achieved ~10% return for 2025 — first double-digit year since 2021 |

Notable Performance History:
- **1982-2008 (26 years):** ~20% annualized return
- **2014-2024 (10 years):** ~4% annualized return — a significant underperformance period
- **2025:** ~10% return (recovery year)
- Only 5 losing years in 42 years; worst loss was -12%
- AUM peaked at ~$30B, later declined to ~$22B by 2025 (estimated)

**2025 Restructuring:**
In mid-2024, Klarman conducted the largest restructuring in Baupost's 42-year history:
- Cut ~20% of investment team
- Refocused on: distressed debt, event-driven equities, private investments, corporate financing
- Reduced public equity exposure (this area had been consistently unprofitable)
- Increased credit exposure from 5% (2021) to ~25% (2025)
- Cash reduced from 30%+ to ~10%

### Key Letter Excerpts (from public sources)

**2007 (On Risk):**
Klarman warned extensively about the housing bubble and excessive leverage. Baupost held significant cash and avoided subprime exposure entirely.

**2013 (On Cash):**
> "With 40% of the portfolio in cash and having returned $4 billion to investors, we are patient. We will not invest in things we don't understand or at prices that don't provide sufficient margin of safety."

**2015 (On Returning Capital):**
Returned $4 billion to investors, saying the fund couldn't find enough compelling opportunities. Rare move for a hedge fund manager.

**2023 (On Distressed):**
> "Commercial real estate is beginning to crack. There's a wall of debt maturities coming due. We see more opportunity there than in other sectors."
