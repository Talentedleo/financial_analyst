## The Sleep OS: Core Principles

### 1. Scale Economies Shared (SES) — The Master Concept

Sleep's single most important intellectual contribution. SES is the idea that the best businesses do not capture their scale economies as profit — they share them with customers through lower prices. This creates a flywheel:

```
Lower prices → More customers → More volume → More scale → Lower costs → Lower prices
```

**Why SES is a competitive moat:**
- Competitors who match prices destroy their own margins
- Competitors who maintain margins lose customers
- The SES company gets stronger as it gets bigger (increasing returns to scale)
- The SES company has a structural cost advantage that cannot be replicated by anyone without equivalent scale

**Businesses Sleep identified as SES:**
- **Costco:** Archetypal SES. 14% gross margin cap (later 11%). Membership model ensures customer loyalty.
- **Amazon:** SES across multiple markets simultaneously. AWS is pure SES.
- **CarMax:** Potential SES in used car market through scale in inspection/reconditioning.
- **InterContinental Hotels:** SES through hotel franchising — scale shared with franchisees.

### 2. Gazelles and Elephants

Sleep does not size positions equally. He categorizes businesses by growth stage:

| Type | Description | Position Size | Time Horizon |
|------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Gazelle** | Early-stage compounder | Small (learning position) | Long (5–10+ years) |
| **Elephant** | Proven compounder at scale | Large (maximum conviction) | Very long (10–20+ years) |
| **Turnaround** | Distressed but fixable | Small, event-driven | Short (1–3 years) |

> "A gazelle that becomes an elephant is the best possible investment outcome. But you must be patient enough to let it grow, and disciplined enough to size it properly at each stage."

### 3. Portfolio Curvature

Sleep thinks about portfolios not as a list of stocks, but as a system with emergent properties.

> "The curvature of a portfolio matters more than the curvature of any individual position. Two positions that look fine independently can create dangerous convexity when combined. The portfolio has its own character."

**Application:**
- Avoid correlated positions that magnify downside
- Seek positions with asymmetric payoff profiles
- Consider how positions interact under different market scenarios
- Size positions to create a portfolio with the right "shape"

### 4. Geographic Franchise Thinking

Sleep evaluates competitive advantages not just on whether they exist, but on their scope.

> "A franchise that operates in one country is riskier than one that operates globally. A franchise with pricing power in one category is less valuable than one whose power extends across multiple categories."

**Franchise Dimensions:**
- **Geographic scope:** Single country vs. global
- **Category scope:** Single product vs. multi-product
- **Customer captivity:** Low switching costs vs. high switching costs
- **Regulatory protection:** None vs. significant
- **Brand power:** Weak vs. dominant

### 5. Partnership Alignment Over Fee Maximization

Sleep views the fund structure itself as an investment decision.

> "If you want different outcomes, you need different structures. The standard 2-and-20 hedge fund structure produces standard outcomes. We wanted something different, so we built something different."

**Nomad's structure:**
- No management fee
- No performance fee
- No lock-up period
- No marketing or sales
- No institutional capital
- Founder invested his entire net worth alongside partners

### 6. Knowing When to Stop

Sleep's most distinctive trait is his ability to recognize "enough."

> "The marginal utility of additional wealth is zero. The marginal utility of time is not."

**This manifests in:**
- Closing the fund at its peak
- Not starting a new fund
- Never leveraging the Nomad brand
- Disappearing completely after 2014
- Focusing on philanthropy (IGY Foundation)

### 7. Socratic Questioning

Sleep's letters are structured around questions rather than declarations. He poses a problem, explores alternative answers, and arrives at his conclusion through dialogue with the reader.

> "Why would a business deliberately leave money on the table? The answer tells you everything about its competitive position."

> "If the best businesses share their scale economies, why don't all businesses do it?"

**Application:** This style forces the reader (and the writer) to think from first principles rather than accepting received wisdom.

## Sleep's Decision-Making Heuristics

| Heuristic | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **SES Test** | Does the business share scale with customers? | Costco caps gross margins at 14% |
| **Owner-Operator Test** | Does management think like owners? | Invests with founder-led companies |
| **Ten-Year Test** | Can you see the business in 10 years? | Avoids fads and short-lived trends |
| **Correlation Check** | Does this position change portfolio curvature? | Avoids correlated sector bets |
| **Enough Check** | Is the marginal utility of more capital worth it? | Closed the fund at the peak |
| **Incentive Check** | Are incentives aligned with long-term outcomes? | Zero fees, zero agency conflicts |
