## 1. From Industry Peers

### William Green (Author, *Richer, Wiser, Happier*)

Green's book is the only long-form profile of Sleep. He describes Sleep as one of the most original and disciplined investors he has ever encountered.

> "Nick Sleep is the most intellectually honest investor I have ever met. He did not just talk about aligned incentives — he built a fund structure that made it impossible to act against his partners' interests."

> "The Nomad letters are, in my opinion, the finest investment writing of their generation. They deserve to be read alongside Buffett's letters."

### Founders Podcast (David Senra)

> "The best investors are not investors at all. They're entrepreneurs who have never sold. Nick Sleep's letters are a masterclass on the importance of understanding the underlying business."

### Anonymous Fund Manager (Quoted in Green's book)

> "Nick Sleep is the only fund manager I know who closed his fund because he had *enough*. Not because he was forced to. Not because returns were declining. Because he had enough. That takes a kind of self-awareness that is almost unheard of in this industry."

## 2. Performance Record

### Nomad Investment Partnership vs. MSCI World (2001–2014)

| Metric | Nomad | MSCI World |
|--------|-------|------------|
| **Cumulative return** | 921% | 118% |
| **Annualized return** | ~18.4% | ~6.4% |
| **Highest one-year return** | 48.2% (2002) | — |
| **Lowest one-year return** | Single-digit loss (2008) | -40%+ |
| **Final AUM at close** | ~£65m (returned to partners) | — |
| **Management fee** | 0% | Industry avg ~1.5% |
| **Performance fee** | 20% over high-water mark (only) | Industry avg 20%+ |
| **Total lifetime revenue to managers** | ~£3m (Sleep's estimate) | Would have been £50m+ at standard fees |

### Key Observation

Sleep could have charged standard hedge fund fees and made tens of millions. Instead, he charged almost nothing and returned all capital when he felt the opportunity set had diminished. This is the defining characteristic of his approach.

## 4. Enduring Legacy (Post-2014)

| Domain | What Sleep Changed | Before | After |
|--------|-------------------|--------|-------|
| **Investment letters** | Set new standard for candor | Fund letters were marketing documents | Nomad letters are studied as literature |
| **Fund structure** | Zero-fee partnership model | 2-and-20 was standard | Proved an alternative was possible |
| **Scale Economies Shared** | Original investment concept | Moats were about keeping value | Moats can be about giving value away |
| **Knowing when to stop** | Closing at the peak | Managers never close voluntarily | Set an example of discipline |
| **Partnership alignment** | Founder coinvests everything | Managers had "other people's money" | Skin in the game at maximum level |

## 5. Critical Views

### What Critics Say

- Nomad was small (£65m at peak) — the SES model may not work at large scale
- Sleep's returns, while extraordinary, came from a concentrated portfolio that could have gone wrong
- The decision to close the fund deprived partners of future returns that may have been substantial

### How Defenders Respond

- Small size was a feature, not a bug — Sleep deliberately capped AUM to maintain flexibility
- Concentration was not randomness — it was deep research and conviction
- Sleep offered partners full transparency about his intentions. If they wanted to keep their capital in similar strategies, they could allocate elsewhere. He did not "trap" them
