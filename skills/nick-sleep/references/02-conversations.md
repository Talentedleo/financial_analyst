## 1. Q&A with Zak — Corner of Berkshire and Fairfax Forum (2010–2014)

Sleep (under the pseudonym "Q&A with Zak" or "QaZ") participated in written Q&A exchanges on the COBF forum. These are the closest thing to interviews he ever gave.

**Topics covered:**

### On Scale Economies Shared Mechanics

> "The SES model works because most competitors cannot bring themselves to compete on price. They are addicted to high margins. Once you accept that low margins are a feature, not a bug, the entire competitive landscape changes. You are no longer competing on the same terms."

### On Costco's Advantage

> "Costco's gross margin cap of 14% means that any competitor who tries to compete on price will lose money. Any competitor who tries to compete on margin will lose customers. Costco has created a situation where it cannot lose, as long as it maintains discipline on the cap."

### On Amazon

> "Amazon is Costco on steroids. The SES flywheel spins across multiple markets simultaneously. AWS is a pure SES play — massive scale economies shared with customers through lower prices, which drives more adoption, which drives more scale. Bezos understood this intuitively."

### On Why He Avoided Banks

> "Banks are leveraged bets on the economy. They have no pricing power. They cannot share economies of scale because their product is money, which is a commodity. We avoid businesses where the primary input is a commodity and the primary output is another commodity."

### On Owner-Operators

> "We prefer businesses where the founder or CEO has a significant ownership stake. This aligns incentives in a way that no compensation consultant can replicate. An owner-operator thinks like an owner. A hired manager thinks like an employee."

### On Position Sizing

> "If a business is truly exceptional and we understand it deeply, why would we own 2% of it? We own 20%. Concentration is not risk if the underlying business is high quality. Diversification is not safety if it means owning mediocre businesses."

### On Closing the Fund

> "We are not closing because we are pessimistic about markets. We are closing because we have enough. The marginal utility of additional wealth is zero. The marginal utility of time is not."

## 2. William Green — *Richer, Wiser, Happier* Interviews (2015–2021)

Green interviewed Sleep extensively for his book. These are the only recorded long-form conversations Sleep has ever participated in.

### On Why He Started Nomad

> "I wanted to build the kind of investment partnership I would want to invest in myself. That meant no management fee, no performance fee, no locked-in capital, no marketing, no sales. Just good ideas and aligned incentives."

### On The Decision to Close

> "People thought I was crazy to close the fund. The returns were great. The assets were growing. The fees would have been enormous. But I had enough. I had more than enough. And I wanted to do other things with my life before it was too late."

### On His Heroes

> "Charlie Munger's concept of 'institutional imperatives' was a revelation. It explains so much of what goes wrong in business and investing. Once you see it, you cannot unsee it."

### On What He Learned

> "The most important thing I learned is that most of what matters in investing has nothing to do with finance. It has to do with psychology, with incentives, with understanding human nature. The math is the easy part."

## 3. Founders Podcast — #365: Nick Sleep's Letters (2024)

**Link:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/365-nick-sleeps-letters-the-full-collection-of/id1141877104?i=1000669730584)

Although Sleep did not participate in this podcast, host David Senra read through the entire collection of Nomad letters and reflected on their lessons.

> "The best investors are not investors at all. They're entrepreneurs who have never sold. Nick Sleep's letters are a masterclass on the importance of understanding the underlying business."

**Key takeaways from the episode:**
- Sleep's letters are "unusually candid" compared to typical fund manager communications
- His writing style is direct, humble, and intellectually honest
- The SES framework is "the most original investment concept since the margin of safety"

## 4. Selected Quotes from Nomad AGM Presentations

Sleep held annual meetings for Nomad partners, but no detailed transcripts survive publicly. Key quotes reconstructed from partner notes and Green's book:

> "The portfolio is not a collection of stocks. It is a set of relationships between businesses, between us and our partners, and between our partners and the world."

> "We try to think about what a business will look like in ten years, not what it will earn next quarter. If you cannot picture a business in ten years, you do not understand it well enough to invest."

> "The best businesses are the ones that get better the bigger they get. This is not true of most businesses. Most businesses get worse as they grow. The ones that get better are rare and precious."

## 5. Summary: The Core Messages Across All Conversations

| Theme | Key Message |
|-------|-------------|
| **Scale Economies Shared** | Lower prices → more volume → more scale → lower prices |
| **Partnership structure** | Align incentives. No fees. No games. |
| **Concentration** | 10–15 positions. Deep understanding beats broad coverage. |
| **Time horizon** | Measure in years, not quarters. Hold through volatility. |
| **Closing the fund** | Enough is enough. Time > money. |
| **Owner-operators** | Invest with people who think like owners. |
| **Learning from mistakes** | Errors have positive NPV if you learn from them. |
