# 05-decisions.md — Nick Sleep's Key Decisions

> The defining moments where Sleep's framework led to breakthrough outcomes.

---

## Decision 1: Structuring Nomad as a True Partnership (2001)

**Context:** Sleep was leaving Baron's Asset Management. He could have started a standard hedge fund and made millions in fees.

**The Decision:** Create a fund with:
- Zero management fee
- Zero performance fee (only a share of profits above high-water mark)
- No lock-up period
- No marketing or sales budget
- Founder invested 100% of his net worth alongside partners

> "I wanted to build the kind of investment partnership I would want to invest in myself."

**Impact:** This structure meant Sleep could only make money if his partners made money. There was no "gravy" — no management fee revenue regardless of performance. This aligned incentives completely.

---

## Decision 2: The SES Thesis — Costco (2004)

**Context:** Sleep was analyzing retailers. Most analysts focused on margins — higher margins meant better business.

**The Insight:** The best retailers do not maximize margins. They minimize them.

**The Decision:** Invest heavily in Costco based on the SES thesis: Costco's 14% gross margin cap was not a weakness — it was an unbreachable competitive moat.

> "Costco's 14% gross margin cap means that any competitor trying to match Costco's prices would go bankrupt. Any competitor trying to match Costco's margins would lose customers to Costco."

**Result:** Costco became Nomad's largest and longest-held position. The SES thesis was proven correct over 13+ years.

---

## Decision 3: Investing in Amazon (c. 2008)

**Context:** Amazon was still viewed as a low-margin retailer by most of Wall Street.

**The Insight:** Amazon was the ultimate SES company. Its flywheel applied across multiple markets simultaneously — retail, marketplace, AWS.

**The Decision:** Invest heavily. Sleep recognized that Amazon's willingness to operate at thin margins while reinvesting everything into growth was not a bug — it was the SES flywheel in action.

> "Amazon is Costco on steroids. The SES flywheel spins across multiple markets simultaneously."

---

## Decision 4: Holding Through the Financial Crisis (2008)

**Context:** Markets collapsed. Nomad positions fell significantly.

**The Decision:** Hold every position. Add to positions where valuations were most attractive.

> "The best time to buy is when prices reflect maximum pessimism. But you cannot buy at the bottom if you sold at the top. You have to be there throughout."

**Result:** Nomad recovered strongly. The willingness to hold through volatility was decisive.

---

## Decision 5: Investing in CarMax (c. 2010)

**Context:** The used car market was enormous, fragmented, and undisrupted.

**The Insight:** CarMax was the only national player with true scale in inspection, reconditioning, and distribution. It could apply the SES model.

**The Decision:** Build a position. Sleep believed CarMax could dominate used cars for decades through its scale advantage.

---

## Decision 6: Concentrated Portfolio (Ongoing)

**Context:** Most fund managers diversify across 50–200 positions.

**The Decision:** Hold 10–15 positions. Sized based on conviction and business stage (gazelles vs. elephants).

> "Diversification is a protection against ignorance. If you know what you are doing, it makes very little sense to own 100 stocks."

**Result:** Concentration magnified returns but required incredible conviction and research depth.

---


## Decision 6b: The "Parking Lot" Thesis — B&B Electronics / Service Businesses

**Context:** Sleep identified small-cap service businesses that were effectively invisible to Wall Street but had powerful SES dynamics.

**The Insight:** The best small businesses are "boring" — they operate in unglamorous industries where large competitors do not bother to compete. These businesses can grow for decades by being better, cheaper, and more reliable than fragmented local competition.

**Examples from Nomad letters:** Small industrial distributors, niche manufacturers, and service companies where scale gave a genuine cost advantage that could be shared with customers.

> "The best businesses are often the most boring. Nobody studies them. Nobody competes with them. They just compound quietly for decades."

**Result:** These "parking lot" positions added diversification without sacrificing returns.

## Decision 7: Writing Letters Unmatched in Candor (Ongoing)

**Context:** Most fund letters are marketing documents. Sleep's were not.

**The Decision:** Write with complete intellectual honesty. Acknowledge mistakes. Explain failed ideas. Share uncertainty.

> "The best investors are the ones who learn from their mistakes. A mistake is only a loss if you do not learn from it."

**Result:** Nomad letters are studied alongside Buffett's as the finest investment writing ever produced.

---

## Decision 8: Closing the Fund at the Peak (2014)

**Context:** Nomad had returned 921% vs. 118% for the MSCI World. Assets were growing. Fees would have been enormous.

**The Decision:** Close the fund. Return all capital to partners. Retire from professional investing at age ~50.

> "The hardest decision in investing is not what to buy. It is what to do with your life. We have decided to do something else."

> "The marginal utility of additional wealth is zero. The marginal utility of time is not."

**Aftermath:** Sleep effectively disappeared from public life. He focused on philanthropy through the IGY Foundation. He never started another fund. He never wrote about investing again.

**Why this matters:** This decision is almost unheard of in finance. Fund managers do not close at the peak. They ride the wave, raise more capital, and collect more fees. Sleep's decision to walk away is the ultimate demonstration of his framework applied to his own life.

---

## Decision 9: Not Starting Again (2014–Present)

**Context:** After Nomad closed, Sleep could have raised a new fund immediately. His reputation was at its peak.

**The Decision:** Do not start again. Do not write another letter. Do not give interviews. Do not appear at conferences. Do not leverage the Nomad brand for anything.

> "We had enough. More than enough."

**Result:** Sleep became one of the very few investors who truly walked away. His silence has only enhanced his reputation.

## Decision Patterns Across All Decisions

| Pattern | Frequency | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Structure follows philosophy** | Always | Zero-fee fund; not a profit-maximizing institution |
| **Test thesis with concentration** | Often | Costco, Amazon were 15%+ positions |
| **Hold through volatility** | Always | 2008 crisis — held all positions, added |
| **Write with candor** | Always | Acknowledged mistakes openly |
| **Think in decades** | Always | "If you cannot see it in 10 years, you don't understand it" |
| **Walk away when "enough"** | Once (decisive) | Closed fund at peak, never returned |
