# Chuck Akre — Key Investment Decisions

An analysis of Chuck Akre's major investment decisions, demonstrating his Three-Legged Stool framework in practice.

---

## 1. MasterCard (MA) — Signature Position

**Held since:** ~2000s
**Portfolio weight:** ~18% (largest position)
**Moat source:** Two-sided network effect (payment network)

**Three-Legged Stool Analysis:**
- **Leg 1 (Business):** Extraordinary 40%+ after-tax margins; asset-light; dominant two-sided network
- **Leg 2 (Management):** Consistent track record of capital allocation; shareholder-friendly
- **Leg 3 (Reinvestment):** Enormous runway — global shift from cash to digital payments still in early stages

**Action:** Akre trims MasterCard when it exceeds portfolio weight targets but never sells the core position.

---

## 2. Markel (MKL) — Longest-Held Position

**Held since:** ~1990s
**Moat source:** Insurance float + underwriting discipline

Markel represents one of Akre's earliest and longest-held positions. He identified the insurance compounding model — where underwriting profits combine with investment float to generate compounding returns — before it was widely appreciated. This investment demonstrates his ability to identify compounding machines early.

---

## 3. Berkshire Hathaway — Early Turning Point

**Invested:** When market cap was ~$100 million

Akre's early investment in Berkshire was formative:
> "I became the best student of Buffett I could and first bought Berkshire Hathaway shares when it had a $100 million market cap. From that happy experience, it became clear to me that the best way to see if a business is adding shareholder value is by the growth in its book value per share."

This experience taught him that business quality and compounding power matter far more than entry price.

---

## 4. O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY) — Non-Discretionary Compound

**Portfolio weight:** ~9%
**Moat source:** Scale + brand in auto parts retail

O'Reilly exemplifies the kind of "boring" business Akre loves. Non-discretionary demand (people must maintain cars), scale advantages in distribution, and pricing power. The Three-Legged Stool: high ROIC, strong management, and long reinvestment runway.

---

## 5. Fair Isaac / FICO — Recent Add (2025)

**Portfolio weight:** ~4% (increased 2,751%)
**Moat source:** Data/IP monopoly (credit scoring is an industry standard)

Akre's largest percentage add signals conviction in FICO's:
- Mission-critical data that banks must use
- Pricing power (FICO scores are embedded in US lending)
- Asset-light recurring revenue model
- Long runway for analytics expansion

---

## 6. Copart (CPRT) — Expanded Position (2025)

**Portfolio weight:** ~2% (increased 253%)
**Moat source:** Network effects in salvage auction market

Another "boring" business with extraordinary moat:
- Virtual monopoly in US salvage vehicle auctions
- Network effects: more buyers → better prices → more sellers
- Asset-light, high ROIC
- Resilient cash flows

---

## 7. Trim Discipline — Not Full Exit

Akre's unique approach to selling:

| Action | Frequency | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Full Exit | Very rare | Only if thesis is structurally broken |
| Trim | Regular | Position grew too large from price appreciation |
| Add | Occasional | Increased conviction on existing positions; new compounder found |

**Key 2025 trims:** MasterCard (-4%), Visa (-6%), Moody's (-8%), O'Reilly (-8%), Roper (-20%)
**Key 2025 adds:** FICO (+2,751%), Copart (+253%), CCC (+10%)

None were full exits — consistent with "compounding forever" philosophy.

---

## 8. What Akre Avoids

- **Commodity businesses:** Airlines, steel, paper — low ROIC, no moat
- **Short product cycles:** Tech hardware, fashion
- **Regulated utilities:** ROIC capped by law
- **Single-person-dependent businesses:** Key man risk
- **High-leverage financials:** Risk of permanent capital loss
- **Fad-driven industries:** "What would you rather have — market share that disappears next year?"
