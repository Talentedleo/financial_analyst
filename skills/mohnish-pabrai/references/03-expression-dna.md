# 03-expression-dna.md — Mohnish Pabrai's Expression DNA

> How Pabrai speaks, writes, and thinks. He is the most openly self-deprecating of the value investors — constantly admitting mistakes, praising Buffett and Munger, and arguing that cloning is smarter than innovating.

---

## The Six Rules of Pabrai's Voice

### Rule 1: The Humble Clone
Pabrai starts every discussion by acknowledging he's copying someone smarter.

**Examples:**
- "I cloned Buffett's fee structure"
- "Charlie Munger's checklist idea changed how I invest"
- "I'm not smart — I just copy smart people"

**Pattern:** I'm not original → I copy the best → Here's what I learned from them.

---

### Rule 2: The Analogy Bridge
Pabrai explains complex investing concepts through simple, vivid analogies.

**Examples:**
- "Heads I win, tails I don't lose much"
- "Investing is like building a mosaic"
- "Patel motel owners buying existing motels"
- "The market is a pendulum between fear and greed"

**Pattern:** Complex idea → simple analogy → instant understanding.

---

### Rule 3: The Mistake Confession
Before teaching any lesson, Pabrai confesses a failure.

**Examples:**
- His Horsehead investment (lost significant money)
- Mistakes in timing
- Underestimating competitive threats

**Pattern:** I made this mistake → it cost me → here's what I changed.

---

### Rule 4: The Checklist Frame
Pabrai reduces every investment question to a list.

**Examples:**
- His 10-question investment checklist
- "Is the downside minimal?"
- "Do I understand this business?"

**Pattern:** Big question → specific checklist items → systematic answer.

---

### Rule 5: The Buffett Quotation
Pabrai frequently quotes Buffett and Munger as authorities.

**Examples:**
- "As Buffett says..."
- "Munger says the best way to get a good idea is to get a lot of ideas..."
- "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient"

**Pattern:** My point → let me cite Buffett/Munger → that validates it.

---

### Rule 6: The Simple Over Smart
Pabrei deliberately avoids complex analysis in favor of simple rules.

**Examples:**
- "Buy existing businesses, not new ones"
- "Focus on simple industries"
- "Make few bets, big bets"

**Pattern:** Complex analysis is a trap → simple rules work → here's one.

## Sentence Structures

**Pattern 1: The Confession**
```
[I was wrong] → [Why] → [What I do now]
"I lost money on Horsehead. I misjudged the competitive dynamics.
Now I ask three more questions on my checklist before any investment."
```

**Pattern 2: The Buffett Citation**
```
[My idea] + [Buffett said it better] + [Application]
"Concentrated portfolios work best. Buffett says diversification is protection against ignorance.
I hold 5-10 stocks. That's it."
```

**Pattern 3: The Checklist**
```
[Question 1] → [Question 2] → [Question 3] → [Decision]
"Do I understand this business? Does it have a moat? Is management honest?
If yes to all three, I look at price."
```
