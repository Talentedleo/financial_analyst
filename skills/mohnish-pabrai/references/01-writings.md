## 1. The Dhandho Investor: The Low-Risk Value Method to High Returns (2007)

**Link:** [Amazon](https://geni.us/The-Dhandho-Investor)

Pabrai's flagship book. The single most important source for his investment philosophy. Title comes from the Gujarati word "Dhandho" (धंधो) meaning "business" or "endeavor."

### The Inspiration: The Patel Motel Story
Gujarati Patel immigrants arrived in the US with little capital but dominated the motel industry within 35 years, controlling over 50% of the market. Their method:
- Buy existing motels (not build new ones)
- Focus on cash flow, not speculation
- Operate with extreme capital efficiency (family labor, low costs)
- Buy at significant discounts to replacement cost

### The Dhandho Framework: 9 Core Principles

1. **Asymmetric Risk-Reward (Heads I Win, Tails I Don't Lose Much)** — Seek investments where upside vastly exceeds downside
2. **Circle of Competence** — Only invest in businesses you understand deeply
3. **Buy Existing Businesses** — Proven track records over unproven ventures
4. **Simple Businesses** — Easy to calculate intrinsic value with conservative assumptions
5. **Distressed Businesses** — Great companies with temporary setbacks
6. **Durable Moats** — Sustainable competitive advantages
7. **Few Bets, Big Bets, Infrequent Bets** — Concentrated portfolio of high-conviction ideas
8. **Patience** — Wait for the right opportunity with significant margin of safety
9. **Clone Successful Investors** — Copy proven strategies rather than innovate

### Key Quotes
> "Heads I win, tails I don't lose much."

> "The market is a pendulum. It swings between fear and greed."

> "Most humans have a strong aversion to cloning... They almost think it's like copying."

## 2. Mosaic: Perspectives on Investing (2012)

**Link:** [Pabrai Investment Funds](https://www.pabraiinvestmentfunds.com)

A collection of Pabrai's writings and letters. The "mosaic" metaphor refers to assembling investment insights from diverse sources.

### Key Ideas
- **The Investment Mosaic:** Small pieces of information, individually insignificant, collectively form a complete picture
- **The Checklist Approach:** Inspired by Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto*, Pabrai created a detailed investment checklist
- **Cloning as a Strategy:** Explicitly argues that cloning proven investors is rational, not lazy
- **Dhandho Arbitrage:** Finding opportunities with minimal risk and maximum returns through temporary market inefficiencies

## 3. Pabrai Investment Funds (PIF) Letters

**Source:** [Pabrai Investment Funds](https://www.pabraiinvestmentfunds.com)

Since 1999, Pabrai has written annual letters to investors modeled closely on Buffett's letters (including the partnership fee structure).

### Key Themes in Letters
- **Fee structure cloned from Buffett:** 25% of profits above 6% hurdle, with no management fee (later modified)
- **Concentrated portfolio:** Usually 5-10 positions
- **Mistake post-mortems:** Detailed analysis of errors
- **Holding period:** Long-term orientation
- **Performance vs. S&P 500:** Fund returned 517% net vs. 43% for S&P over key period

## 4. Pabrai's Investment Checklist

Inspired by Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto* and Munger's mental models, Pabrai uses a formal investment checklist.

### Key Checklist Items
1. Is this a business I understand very well?
2. Does the business have a durable competitive advantage (moat)?
3. Is the management honest and competent?
4. Is the downside in this investment minimal?
5. Is the upside significantly greater than the downside?
6. Is there a significant margin of safety?
7. Is this a "few bets, big bets, infrequent bets" opportunity?
8. Would I be comfortable holding this for 5+ years?
9. Am I cloning a proven investor's thesis or my own?
10. What could go wrong that I'm not seeing?

## 6. TransTech Inc. — The Entrepreneurial Foundation (1991-2000)

Pabrai's experience building and selling a company taught him how businesses really work — a foundation that most pure investors lack.

**The Story:**
- Founded TransTech Inc. in 1991 with $30,000 from his 401(k) and $70,000 from credit card debt
- IT consulting and systems integration company
- Grew to over $20 million in revenue
- Sold to Kurt Salmon Associates in 2000 for ~$20 million

**Why it matters for his investing:**
- Gave him deep understanding of business operations, not just financial statements
- His experience as a founder informs his respect for owner-operated businesses
- The sale provided the capital to start Pabrai Investment Funds
- Teaches that building a business from nothing requires capital efficiency — a core Dhandho principle
