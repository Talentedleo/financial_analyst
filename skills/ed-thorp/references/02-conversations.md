## 1. NPR Planet Money — Episode 749: "Professor Blackjack" (2017)

**Link:** [NPR Transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/510810752)
**Interviewer:** Jacob Goldstein
**Context:** In-depth interview covering Thorp's entire life story — blackjack, Kelly criterion, Wall Street, and the Madoff discovery.

### On How He Discovered Card Counting

Thorp tells the story of standing in the UCLA library, reading a paper by army mathematicians about blackjack:

> "I'm standing in the stacks. I pull the article down. And I decide to start reading it while I'm standing up."

He had a key insight — blackjack is fundamentally different from other gambling games because it has *memory*:

> "Most of the games — whatever happens on one trial — doesn't have any influence on what's going to happen next. So if you roll a pair of dice and get a five on one roll, it has no influence on what totals are going to come up on the next roll."

> "As I'm going down the page, I realized in a minute or two that if cards were used up during the play of the game, the odds would shift back and forth, sometimes for the casino, sometimes for me."

### On Using the IBM 704 Supercomputer

Thorp programmed the MIT IBM 704 (the first supercomputer) in Fortran to compute blackjack probabilities:

> "As the weeks and months went by, I finally got to the point where I could ask the machine this question — what would happen if I took all four aces out of the deck?"

> "I did it with all the other cards, and I found out that if the fives go out of the deck, the player gets a huge edge — bigger than any other card."

### On Why He Published Instead of Getting Rich

When asked why he chose to publish his findings rather than make millions:

> "What I thought was that I was going to lead the life of an academic. I thought that teaching in the university would be a great life, and I would just enjoy doing what I did. And that's pretty much all I cared about."

### On the Manny Kimmel Partnership

The famous story of the blue Cadillac and two "nieces":

> "A pretty blond got out of the passenger side. Another pretty blonde got the driver's side. And then this white-haired, kind of short, elderly man in long cashmere coat got out. He had been snuggled up between the two blondes."

> "I was naive, and I believed it, but my wife angrily disagreed (laughter). These are not nieces."

### On His Secret Code to Save Money on Phone Calls

Thorp arranged a collect-call code with his wife during his first Vegas trip:

> "Here's what I'll do. Every night, I'll call you collect. I'll ask to speak to Edward Thorp, and I'll add a middle initial. That will be like our secret code. So my middle initial is A, that means we're up zero to a thousand dollars. If it's a B, we're up 1,000 to $2,000."

> "And when we hit our peak — I think we're up 13,000 at one point — I asked for Edward M. Thorp, and she sounded very happy."

### On Why Blackjack Is The Best Training for Investing

> "The blackjack tables are an amazingly good training ground for learning how to invest, how to think about investments, how to manage them. And the reason is that they teach you, on the one hand, to use probability and statistics to evaluate things. And on the other, they teach you discipline. When you find something, you stick to it."

### On Princeton Newport Partners' Record

> "In, I think, 230 months, we had three down months. All the others were up, and three down months were all less than 1 percent."

### On His Approach to Every Market

> "If you bring in more information, what seemed unbeatable may become beatable."

## 2. CNBC Interview — 55th Anniversary of *Beat the Dealer* (2017)

**Link:** [CNBC (archived)](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/02/03/quant-king-ed-thorp-on-his-autobiography-a-man-for-all-markets.html)
**Key moments:**

### On Blackjack vs. Wall Street

> "In blackjack, I knew exactly the odds. The challenge on Wall Street was: what are the probabilities? You don't know them exactly. So you have to estimate them, and you have to manage the uncertainty of your estimates."

### On the Kelly Criterion

Thorp emphasized that the Kelly criterion was his "secret weapon" in both gambling and investing:

> "The Kelly criterion tells you how much to bet when you have an edge. It's the optimal way to grow your wealth over time. Most people bet too much or too little. Getting the bet size right is as important as finding the edge."

### On Beating the Market

> "I had 20% annualized returns over 28.5 years on my personal account. The key was not being smart — it was being disciplined. I followed the math, not my emotions."

## 4. *Fortune's Formula* by William Poundstone (2005)

**Link:** [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Fortunes-Formula-Scientific-Betting-Casinos/dp/0809046377)

The definitive book-length account of the Kelly criterion and its application by Thorp.

### Key Story: The Shannon-Thorp Wearable Computer

Thorp collaborated with Claude Shannon (the father of information theory) to build the world's first wearable computer:

> "The wearable computer was co-developed with Claude Shannon between 1960 and 1961. It relied on a pair of operators, where one would watch the wheel and use his toe to input the cadence of the wheel, and the other would receive a message in the form of musical tones through a hidden earpiece."

**Result:** By betting on groups of neighboring numbers on the roulette wheel, they could gain a sufficient advantage to make a profit. Final testing in Shannon's basement lab, June 1961.

Now illegal in Nevada due to the 1985 "devices law."

### Key Story: Detecting Madoff's Fraud

Thorp was an early skeptic of Bernie Madoff's returns. In 1991, he analyzed Madoff's reported trading and realized the numbers were mathematically impossible:

> "The returns were too consistent. The options strategy Madoff claimed to use couldn't produce those numbers. I told people. Nobody listened for 17 years."

## 6. *A Man for All Markets* — Select Interview Quotes

### On Playing Bridge with Warren Buffett

Thorp recounts meeting Buffett in the 1970s when they played bridge together:

> "Warren was a delightful bridge partner. Sharp, always calculating. We talked about investing. He understood probability intuitively — as well as anyone I've ever met."

### On the Fundamental Question of Investing

> "The real question is not 'what is the stock worth?' The real question is: 'given what I know, what is the probability distribution of future outcomes, and what is the optimal bet size given that distribution?'"

### On His Philosophy

> "I'm always looking for situations where the odds are in my favor. If the odds are 60-40 in your favor, bet 20% of your bankroll. If they're 55-45, bet less. If they're 50-50, don't bet at all. It's that simple, yet almost nobody does it."

## Summary of Thorp's Speaking Style & Cognitive Patterns

From across all interviews, Thorp's communication patterns emerge clearly:

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Mathematical precision** | Always quantifies, rarely speaks in vague terms | "230 months, 3 down months" |
| **Probability framing** | Every decision is framed as probability distribution | "If the odds are 60-40..." |
| **Discipline emphasis** | Constantly returns to the importance of sticking to the system | "Discipline is everything" |
| **Self-deprecation** | Humble about his intelligence despite extraordinary achievements | "I was naive... my wife angrily disagreed" |
| **Edge-seeking** | Natural instinct to find exploitable opportunities in any system | "Bring in more information" |
| **First-principles thinking** | Goes back to fundamentals, doesn't accept conventional wisdom | "Probability itself got started because mathematicians were interested in trying to figure out the odds" |
