## 2. From Institutional Profiles

### Princeton Newport Partners (Wikipedia)

Stated by Thorp to be **the world's first market-neutral hedge fund**. Achieved:
- 20% annualized returns after fees for over two decades
- Zero down quarters
- Only three down months in 230 months, all under 1%

## 2b. From Influential Thinkers

### Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Author, *Fooled by Randomness*, *The Black Swan*)

Taleb frequently cites Thorp as one of the rare examples of someone who genuinely understands probability and risk:

> "Ed Thorp is the only person I know who consistently made money in both gambling and investing, and understood exactly why."

In *Fooled by Randomness*, Taleb contrasts Thorp with the many "lucky fools" in finance — people who mistook random good outcomes for skill. Thorp, by contrast, understood the probability distribution of his own results and never confused luck with edge.

### Claude Shannon (Co-inventor of Information Theory)

Shannon, who worked with Thorp on the wearable computer, held him in exceptionally high regard:

> Shannon was known to remark that Thorp thought about probability with a clarity that matched (and sometimes exceeded) his own.

## 5. Academic Citations

### Kelly Criterion Recognition

Thorp's 1962 book *Beat the Dealer* is credited in Kelly's original paper as the first practical application of the Kelly criterion:

> "My 1962 book Beat the Dealer explained the detailed theory and practice. The 'optimal' way to bet in favorable situations was an important feature. In Beat the Dealer I called this, naturally enough, 'The Kelly gambling system,' since I learned about it from the 1956 paper by John L. Kelly." — **Edward O. Thorp, "Understanding Fortune's Formula" (2007)**

### Black-Scholes Connection

Thorp independently developed techniques for pricing options years before the Black-Scholes model was published. The warrant hedging formula in *Beat the Market* (1967) anticipated key elements of what became the Nobel Prize-winning options pricing model.

### Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (1961)

His paper "A favorable strategy for twenty-one" was notable for bypassing peer review — the PNAS allowed members to submit papers directly. Thorp's blackjack research is one of the very few examples where results reached the public directly, completely bypassing the usual academic peer review process cycle.

## 6. Enduring Legacy

| Domain | What Thorp Changed | Before | After |
|--------|-------------------|--------|-------|
| **Blackjack** | Proved the house can be beaten | House always wins | Card counting accepted as mathematically valid |
| **Gambling math** | Applied Kelly criterion to gambling | Intuitive betting | Optimal bet sizing |
| **Options pricing** | Independent warrant hedging formula | Options seen as lottery tickets | Options priced mathematically |
| **Hedge funds** | First market-neutral quant fund | Gut-feel investing | Systematic quantitative strategies |
| **Quantitative finance** | Founded the quant revolution | Old-boy networks | PhDs and computers on Wall Street |
| **Wearable computing** | Co-invented first wearable computer | No precedent | First practical wearable device |
