## 1. BYD Company Limited — The Signature Investment

**Timeline:**
- **2002/2004:** First purchased BYD shares as a cornerstone investor
- **2008:** Recommended BYD to Charlie Munger and Warren Buffett; Berkshire Hathaway acquired 10% stake for $230 million
- **2022–2024:** Berkshire gradually reduced position
- **Total Return:** ~7,826% (~23% CAGR over 20+ years)

**Investment Thesis (Reconstructed from available sources):**
1. **Technological moat:** BYD possessed deep expertise in battery manufacturing, a critical technology for the coming EV revolution
2. **Vertical integration:** BYD controlled the entire value chain from battery chemistry to final vehicle assembly — an advantage competitors couldn't replicate
3. **Founder quality:** Wang Chuanfu had built a strong engineering culture and a cost advantage in battery production
4. **Cost advantage:** Chinese manufacturing efficiency + proprietary technology = unbeatable cost structure
5. **Secular tailwind:** Global transition toward electric vehicles and renewable energy was inevitable

**Why It Worked:**
- Invested *before* the thesis became obvious to mainstream investors
- Held through extreme volatility (multiple market cycles, regulatory changes, competitive threats)
- Munger and Buffett's endorsement provided additional capital and validation
- BYD evolved from obscure battery manufacturer to world's largest EV company

## 2. Kweichow Moutai — The Luxury Consumer Bet

**Performance:** ~287-bagger return

**Investment Thesis:**
1. **Extraordinary moat:** 92% gross margins reflecting unparalleled pricing power
2. **Cultural significance:** Moutai is deeply embedded in Chinese business culture (gift-giving, banquets, social status)
3. **Geographic scarcity:** Production limited to Maotai town in Guizhou province — can't be replicated, similar to French champagne or Scotch whisky
4. **Pricing power durability:** Prices have increased consistently for decades without demand destruction
5. **Chinese consumer spending thesis:** Captured China's economic transformation as rising incomes enabled premium consumption

**Why It Worked:**
- Deep understanding of Chinese culture that foreign investors often miss
- Recognition that Moutai's role in business entertainment and social status creates demand transcending economic cycles
- Long holding period allowed compounding to work its magic

**Source:** [Shipra: Li Lu Investment Strategy](https://www.shipra.ca/blog/li-lu-investment-strategy-chinese-warren-buffett-himalaya-capital)

## 4. Investment Decision Framework (Generalized)

Based on analysis of his known decisions, Li Lu's investment process follows this pattern:

### Screening Stage
- Identify businesses with durable competitive advantages (moats)
- Focus on industries undergoing structural change or secular growth
- Prefer businesses where competitive dynamics are predictable 10+ years out

### Research Stage
- **Intensive due diligence:** Financial statements, industry structure, competitive positioning, supply chain analysis
- **Management quality assessment:** Visit local community, speak to neighbors, interview former employees
- **Cross-cultural analysis:** Understand regulatory, political, and cultural factors (especially critical for Chinese investments)
- **Verify "accurate and complete information"** — his investing mantra

### Decision Stage
- Determine intrinsic value with a margin of safety
- Assess risk of permanent capital loss
- Compare to next best alternative (opportunity cost)
- Decide position size based on conviction level

### Post-Investment
- Hold for long periods (decades, not years)
- Ignore short-term volatility and market sentiment
- Only sell if thesis is broken or valuation becomes extreme
- Allow compounding to work

## 5. What Li Lu Does NOT Do

- Does not trade based on macroeconomic forecasts
- Does not attempt to time the market
- Does not diversify for the sake of diversification
- Does not invest outside his circle of competence
- Does not chase short-term momentum or themes
- Does not discuss current positions publicly (beyond regulatory requirements)
- Does not charge management fees (partnership model)

## 6. Partnership Model — Structural Innovation

Munger helped Li Lu restructure Himalaya Capital into a partnership model with:
- **Zero annual management fees** (unlike typical hedge fund 2% management fee)
- **Long-term committed capital** (investors commit for extended periods)
- **Concentrated positions** (high conviction, not diversification)
- **Performance-based compensation only** (aligns incentives)

This structure allowed Li Lu to invest with a genuinely long-term horizon, free from the quarterly performance pressure that constrains typical fund managers.
