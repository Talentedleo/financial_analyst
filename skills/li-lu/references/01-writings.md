# Li Lu — Writings

A comprehensive collection of Li Lu's published works, essays, and key writings that define his intellectual framework, covering his civilization theory, modernization thesis, and value investing philosophy.

---

## 1. Major Book: "Civilization, Modernization, Value Investing and China" (2020)

**Published:** April 2020, CITIC Publishing House (China)
**ISBN:** 9787521712599
**Language:** Chinese (no official English edition as of 2025)
**Copies sold in China:** 279,000+
**Foreword by:** Charlie Munger
**Rights sold to:** Traditional Chinese, Korean, Thai

**Structure:** The book is divided into four thematic parts mirroring the title.

### Part I: Civilization & Modernization
Contains Li Lu's widely acclaimed **"Sixteen Lectures on Modernization"** (现代化十六讲), where he explores the evolution of human society through a framework of three civilizational stages:

- **Civilization 1.0 (Out of Africa):** ~60,000 BCE — Humans migrate from Africa; survival-driven nomadic societies.
- **Civilization 2.0 (Agriculture):** ~9,600 BCE onward — Rise of agricultural societies; Malthusian trap dominates; East and West develop in parallel with different political systems (Western feudalism vs. Chinese imperial meritocracy via the Keju/exam system).
- **Civilization 3.0 (Modern Era):** Industrial Revolution onward — Exponential technological growth, globalization, free markets, scientific discovery. The defining characteristic is the **break from the Malthusian trap** — for the first time, human productivity can outpace population growth indefinitely.

**Key Thesis:** Li Lu argues that geography played the decisive role in why the West (specifically Britain/Western Europe) reached Civilization 3.0 first, while China, despite leading the world through much of Civilization 2.0, fell behind due to its political structure (absolute monarchy controlling all resources, suppression of mercantile class, Keju rigidity) and failure to embrace scientific and economic meritocracy. He contrasts the Western system's separation of political and economic power (Magna Carta, rise of business class, parliamentary democracy) with China's unified system under imperial rule.

**Influences cited:** Jared Diamond (Guns, Germs, and Steel), Ian Morris (Social Development Index), E.O. Wilson, Adam Smith

### Part II: Value Investing
A collection of Li Lu's speeches, lectures, and essays on value investing from 2000–2020, covering:
- The correct understanding of value investing (not just "buying cheap")
- Intrinsic value estimation methodologies
- The importance of "circle of competence"
- Why value investing works in China
- The "moat" concept in Chinese business contexts
- Role of intellectual honesty in investing

### Part III: Reflections on China
Analysis of China's modernization path, market reforms, and long-term prospects. Topics include:
- China's political meritocracy system
- The unique challenges of investing in China
- China's long-term economic outlook
- The relationship between rule of law and market development

### Part IV: Broader Reflections
Book reviews, essays on human nature, contemporary issues, and future predictions. Includes reflections on:
- Behavioral economics
- Technological change and its social impact
- The role of education in human progress

## 2. "Moving the Mountain: My Life in China" (1990)

**Published:** 1990 (G.P. Putnam's Sons, US)
**ISBN:** 9780399135453
**Language:** English

An autobiographical account of Li Lu's early life in China, from surviving the Cultural Revolution and the 1976 Tangshan earthquake to his role as a student leader and press spokesman for the Democracy Movement during the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests.

**Key themes:**
- Survival through the Cultural Revolution (parents sent to labor camps)
- The 1976 Tangshan earthquake (one of deadliest in recorded history)
- Student activism and leadership at Nanjing University
- The Tiananmen Square Spring of 1989
- Escape from China and journey to the United States

**Adaptation:** Adapted into a 1994 feature documentary *Moving the Mountain*, directed by Michael Apted.

## 3. Foreword to Chinese Edition of "Poor Charlie's Almanack" (2010)

Li Lu wrote the foreword to the Chinese edition of *Poor Charlie's Almanack: The Wit and Wisdom of Charles T. Munger*. He also helped translate and publish the Chinese version in China.

**Key content:** In this foreword, Li Lu recounts how attending Warren Buffett's lecture at Columbia in 1993 transformed his view of investing — that one could succeed in the stock market purely by studying company value, without connections or special resources. He describes meeting Munger on Thanksgiving 2003 as one of the most important events of his life, and how Munger's mentorship fundamentally reshaped his investment approach from a hedge fund mentality to a true value investing partnership model.

## Summary of Core Intellectual Contributions

1. **Civilization Cycle Theory (文明周期论):** Three-stage framework of human progress (1.0 Hunter-gatherer → 2.0 Agricultural/Malthusian → 3.0 Modern/Compound Growth) with geography as the primary driver of differential development between East and West.

2. **Political vs. Economic Meritocracy:** Contrast between China's political meritocracy (Keju exam system) creating stability but suppressing economic dynamism, versus the West's economic meritocracy enabling capitalism and the Industrial Revolution.

3. **Eastern Long-termism (东方长期主义):** A synthesis of Western value investing principles with Eastern cultural and institutional understanding — patient capital applied to structurally growing markets, informed by deep institutional and cultural knowledge.

4. **Value Investing in Emerging Markets:** Adaptation of traditional Graham-and-Doddsville value investing to Chinese market conditions, emphasizing government policy understanding, cultural dynamics, and long-term structural trends alongside financial analysis.
