## 1. Li Lu on Charlie Munger's Wisdom and Legacy
### Munger College / 正和岛 Interview (November 28, 2024) — ⭐ MOST RECENT & IMPORTANT

**Context:** The first anniversary of Charlie Munger's passing. Li Lu gave an online interview hosted by 正和岛 (Zhenghedao), in collaboration with Munger College and CITIC Press Group. This was his first interview with domestic (Chinese) media in four years.

**Format:** Online interview, conducted in Mandarin
**Source:** [MOI Global PDF Transcript (English)](https://moiglobal.com/wp-content/uploads/li-lu-on-charlie-munger-202412.pdf)
**Source:** [Medium English Summary by Alex Carter](https://medium.com/@iamalexcarter/latest-interview-transcripts-li-lu-on-value-investing-charlie-munger-and-the-ai-revolution-34f3605a124f)
**Source:** [Substack Summary by EugeneNg_VCap](https://valueb9b.substack.com/p/lessons-from-li-lu-on-charlie-mungers)

**Key Quotes & Themes:**

#### On Losing Munger (Personal Reflection)
> "Today is a very special day — exactly one year since Charlie's passing, and it's also Thanksgiving in America. My first lengthy conversation with Charlie was on Thanksgiving Day in 2003. We talked for four or five hours, and afterward became partners until his death — 20 years have passed so quickly."

> "On that Thanksgiving weekend when Charlie passed away, he was with his family, jovially chatting during dinner as usual. However, when it came time for dessert, he felt unwell. He was taken to the hospital early the next morning and passed away about a day later. Charlie lived his entire life according to his own clock, never changing — working and spending time with family until his very last moment."

#### Munger's Investment Innovations (Two Pillars)
1. **Buy Great Companies at Reasonable Prices:** Munger shifted the focus from deeply undervalued stocks (Graham's approach) to great companies with long-term value creation potential — the strategy behind Berkshire Hathaway.
2. **Fishing Where There Are Fish:** Build expertise in areas rich with opportunities; operate within your circle of competence.

#### Munger's Four Levels of Rationality
1. **Universal Wisdom** — Ground decisions in first principles and facts; understand reality as it is, not as you wish it to be.
2. **Multidisciplinary Thinking** — Munger's "latticework of mental models" combining insights from various fields to solve complex problems.
3. **Avoiding Systematic Errors** — The 25 cognitive biases in *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment*.
4. **Respecting Common Sense** — "Copy what works, avoid what doesn't."

#### On AI and Global Collaboration
Li Lu extended Munger's lessons to AI — while AI holds transformative potential, it poses existential risks requiring unprecedented global collaboration. He echoed Munger's belief in cooperation between nations, particularly China and the US.

#### Summary of Life Lessons from the Interview
- Seek consistent role models whose actions align with their values
- Commit to lifelong learning — knowledge compounds like wealth
- Invest for the long term — focus on quality, stay within your circle of competence
- Embrace rational thinking — avoid cognitive biases, apply first principles
- The best investment is often in the talent of another person

## 2. Li Lu with Professor Bruce Greenwald — Columbia Business School (2021)

**Context:** Interview at Columbia Business School with Professor Bruce Greenwald, a renowned value investing expert and Ben Graham scholar.

**Key Content:**
Li Lu discussed his strategy of identifying **"long-term compounders"** — businesses where the nature of competition is such that one could predict the outcome in 10 years, even with all the ups and downs in the macro environment. He emphasized that this ability to project long-term competitive dynamics is the **most important skill** in investing.

## 3. Charlie Munger on Li Lu — Public Remarks

### Reuters Interview (2019)
Munger publicly stated: **"Li Lu is the only outside manager I have ever invested with."** He described Li Lu as the **"Chinese Warren Buffett."**

### Berkshire Hathaway Annual Meetings
- **2024 Meeting:** Warren Buffett recalled that Munger only "slammed the table" with him twice to insist on buying something. One of those was BYD — which Munger only learned about through Li Lu's recommendation.
- **Pre-2024 Meetings:** Munger hinted several times that Li Lu was "a foregone conclusion" to be one of Berkshire's top investment officials after Buffett's retirement (though Li Lu later withdrew himself from consideration in 2010).

### Business Insider (January 2025)
A friend of Charlie Munger's — Li Lu — revealed that Munger **doubled his money on a contrarian bet** shortly before his death at age 99, demonstrating that Munger's investment skills remained sharp until the very end.

## 5. The First Meeting: Thanksgiving 2003

One of the most frequently recounted stories in Li Lu's conversations:

> "I met Charlie Munger on Thanksgiving Day 2003. We talked for four or five hours. I walked through every single investment I'd researched. Munger, known for his skeptical nature and reluctance to delegate capital, decided to invest $88 million after that single conversation."

This meeting is considered a watershed moment. Munger told Li Lu:
> "The problem you have is the whole problem of Wall Street. Although Berkshire has achieved so much success, there is no company on Wall Street that can really imitate it. If you want to eliminate your troubles, we need to take a different path from Wall Street."

Munger then helped Li Lu restructure Himalaya Capital into the partnership model — no annual management fees, long-term committed capital, concentrated positions — exactly the model Munger and Buffett used in Berkshire's early days.

## 6. The Buffett Lecture — Columbia University (1993)

Li Lu has frequently told the story of how he stumbled upon Warren Buffett's lecture at Columbia University while looking for free food. This serendipitous event changed his life:

> "The biggest shock was: it turns out that a person can gain in the stock market just by studying the value of a company — without relying on connections or resources — and in turn change their destiny."

He immediately found all available books about Buffett and Munger, studying how they researched companies, how they decided to buy, and how they prevented losing money. Two years later, he bought his first stock.

## Summary of Conversational Themes

Across all conversations and interviews, certain themes consistently emerge:

1. **Life story as competitive advantage** — His unique background gives him insight that purely Western or Chinese investors lack
2. **Munger as transformational figure** — Every conversation references Munger's mentorship as the pivotal turning point
3. **Value investing as a philosophy** — Not just a strategy but a complete worldview
4. **Intellectual honesty** — "There is always a possibility that you don't know that you don't know"
5. **Long-term patient capital** — The core of Himalaya's model; compound returns require decades, not quarters
6. **Bridge between East and West** — Repeated emphasis on understanding both systems
7. **Deep research methodology** — Understanding CEO character through unconventional means (visiting local church, speaking to neighbors)
