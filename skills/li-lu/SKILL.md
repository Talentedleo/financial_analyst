His investment track record (~30% CAGR since 1998) places him among history's most successful investors.

His intellectual framework combines:
1. A **macro-historical theory of civilization cycles** (Civilization 1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0)

## Core Philosophy

### The Three Civilizations Framework

Li Lu divides human history into three stages, with geography as the primary driver of differential development:

**Civilization 1.0 — Hunter-Gatherer Era (~60,000 BCE)**
- Humans migrate out of Africa
- Survival-driven, nomadic
- Population capped at ~20,000 globally during Ice Age low point

**Civilization 2.0 — Agricultural Era (~9,600 BCE – 18th Century)**
- Rise of agriculture enables settled societies
- **Malthusian trap dominates**: growth limited by agricultural output; population booms inevitably lead to famine/disease/war that reset equilibrium
- East (China) and West (Europe) develop in parallel but with different political structures:
  - **West**: Fragmented feudalism → Magna Carta limits monarch power → rise of mercantile class → separation of political and economic power → economic meritocracy
  - **East**: Unified empire under absolute monarchy → Keju (exam) system creates political meritocracy but suppresses economic dynamism → merchants are at bottom of social hierarchy → no separation of economic from political power

**Civilization 3.0 — Modern Era (Industrial Revolution – Present)**
- **Break from the Malthusian trap**: For the first time, productivity can outpace population growth indefinitely
- Driven by: scientific method, free markets, technological innovation, globalization
- The defining characteristic is **compound growth** — wealth and knowledge accumulate exponentially
- The West (specifically Britain) reached this stage first due to its unique political evolution (economic meritocracy, parliamentary system, property rights)

**Key Thesis for Investing:** Understanding which stage a society/business is in and whether it is successfully making the transition to or operating within Civilization 3.0 is fundamental to identifying long-term investment opportunities.

### Investment Philosophy Principles

1. **Intrinsic Value Investing**: The true goal is long-term wealth compounding through ownership of productive businesses. Market prices fluctuate; intrinsic value reflects the business's sustainable cash flow generation over time.

2. **Risk First, Return Second**: Protect capital from permanent loss. Temporary volatility is unavoidable; permanent impairment occurs when you misjudge business economics or overpay for growth that never materializes.

3. **Concentrated Conviction**: Invest only in a small number of businesses you understand deeply. Diversification for its own sake dilutes returns. Position size should reflect conviction level.

4. **Long-Term Holding Period**: Hold for decades. Allow compounding to work. Short-term price movements reflect sentiment, not business value.

5. **Circle of Competence**: Know what you know and what you don't know. "There is always a possibility that you don't know that you don't know."

6. **Business Owner Mindset**: Evaluate investments as if buying the entire company. This forces deeper analysis of long-term economics, management quality, and competitive dynamics.

7. **Intellectual Honesty**: "Accurate and complete information" is the investing mantra. Investigate thoroughly — including unconventional channels (visiting local communities, understanding CEO character first-hand).

8. **Partnership Model**: Zero management fees. Long-term committed capital. Performance-based compensation only. This structure enables genuine long-term thinking.

## Key Investment Framework

### How Li Lu Analyzes a Business

1. **Assess the Moat**
   - Durable competitive advantage (technology, brand, scale, regulation, geography)
   - Can the advantage persist for 10+ years?
   - Are competitors able to replicate it?

2. **Evaluate Management Quality**
   - Character: Is the CEO honest and aligned with shareholders?
   - Competence: Can they execute the strategy?
   - Culture: Has the founder built an engineering/innovation culture?

3. **Estimate Intrinsic Value**
   - Sustainable cash flow generation
   - Growth potential within realistic assumptions
   - Terminal value based on competitive position

4. **Price vs. Value**
   - Is market price significantly below intrinsic value?
   - What is the margin of safety?
   - What would cause permanent capital loss?

5. **Holding Period**
   - Can this compound for 10–20 years?
   - Will the competitive advantage strengthen or weaken over time?
   - Am I willing to hold through severe volatility?

### Signature Case: BYD

Li Lu's most famous investment demonstrates all principles in action:
- **Identified early**: Invested when BYD was an obscure battery manufacturer
- **Deep research**: Understood Wang Chuanfu's engineering culture and vertical integration strategy
- **Technological moat**: Battery expertise and cost advantage
- **Secular trend**: Recognized inevitability of EV transition before it was obvious
- **Long holding**: Held through 20+ years of extreme volatility
- **Concentrated**: ~2.5% of BYD ownership in his fund
- **Result**: ~7,826% return (~23% CAGR)

### Signature Case: Kweichow Moutai
- **Cultural moat**: 92% gross margins from brand power and geographic scarcity
- **Chinese consumer thesis**: Captured rising disposable incomes and premium consumption
- **Result**: ~287-bagger return

## Civilization Cycle & Market Application

### How to Apply the Three Civilizations Framework to Investing

| Framework Element | Investment Implication |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Civilization 3.0 sectors | Technology, renewable energy, biotech — compound growth industries |
| Escaping the Malthusian trap | Seek businesses with productivity gains that outpace cost growth |
| Political vs. economic meritocracy | In markets with strong rule of law and property rights, economic meritocracy drives innovation; in markets with weaker institutions, government understanding is critical |
| Geography as destiny | Understand the geographic and institutional context of each market |
| Cultural DNA | Consumer behavior, savings rates, risk tolerance are shaped by centuries of cultural evolution |

### Why Value Investing Works in China (Li Lu's Thesis)

- China is undergoing the most rapid Civilization 2.0 → 3.0 transition in history
- Market inefficiencies create more mispricing opportunities than in developed markets
- Cultural understanding is a critical competitive advantage that outsiders lack
- Government policy direction is more predictable than short-term market movements
- Long-term structural trends (urbanization, consumption upgrade, technology adoption) are highly predictable
