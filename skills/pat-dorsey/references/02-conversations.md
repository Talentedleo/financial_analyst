# Pat Dorsey — Conversations & Interviews

A curated collection of Pat Dorsey's interviews, speaking engagements, and public discussions about his moat analysis framework and investment philosophy.

---

> **Note on sourcing:** Pat Dorsey is a notably private figure. He has not given frequent media interviews, particularly since leaving Morningstar and founding Dorsey Asset Management (2013). Most available recorded conversations date from his Morningstar era (2008–2010). His more recent contributions are primarily through his firm's portfolio actions (13F filings) rather than public commentary.

---

## 1. Talks at Google — "The Little Book That Builds Wealth" (2008)

**Format:** In-person talk at Google headquarters (Authors@Google series)
**Video:** [YouTube - Talks at Google](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFS5JBgz1Xc)
**Audio:** [Libsyn - Talks at Google Ep489](https://talksatgoogle.libsyn.com/ep489-pat-dorsey-the-little-book-that-builds-wealth)

**Key Themes Covered:**
- What it means for a company to have an economic moat
- The four (now five) sources of moats
- How to distinguish real moats from mistaken moats
- The key drivers of business valuation
- Practical examples using well-known companies

**Memorable Quotes from this Talk:**
- "Most investors waste time analyzing the wrong things — earnings reports, quarterly guidance, management's latest quote. The only thing that matters long-term is whether the business can defend its profits."
- "A great product is not a moat. A great product that becomes obsolete next year never made anyone rich."
- "The best returns come from holding high-quality companies for many years."

## 2. Book Signing & Lecture Circuit (2008–2010)

During the release of *The Little Book That Builds Wealth*, Dorsey participated in several speaking events and book signings. These talks focused on systematizing Buffett's moat concept into a teachable framework for retail investors.

**Key themes from these appearances:**
- "Better to have a good poker hand than a good poker player" — industry structure matters more than management
- The danger of mistaking temporary advantages for permanent moats
- How Morningstar developed its Moat Rating system
- The integration of qualitative and quantitative analysis for moat identification

## 3. Morningstar Investment Conference Appearances

As Morningstar's Director of Stock Analysis, Dorsey was a regular presenter at Morningstar Investment Conferences. These appearances were primarily internal/industry events rather than publicly distributed media.

**Topics covered:**
- Morningstar's equity research methodology
- The Moat Rating system development and implementation
- Industry-level comparative moat analysis
- Case studies of wide-moat companies

Since founding Dorsey Asset Management in 2013, Dorsey has maintained a **very low public profile**. He does not give regular media interviews, does not appear on financial television, and does not publish public letters.

## 6. Portfolio Activity as Stealth Commentary

Since Dorsey does not do interviews, his investment activity (13F filings) serves as his primary form of communication with the market. Notable observations from his holdings:

- **Concentrated portfolio** — Small number of high-conviction positions
- **Quality bias** — Companies with wide moats (Meta, Alphabet, Disney)
- **Long holding periods** — Positions held for multiple years with minimal trading
- **Selective new positions** — New additions are rare and seem to require exceptional moat characteristics

**Source:** [StockCircle - Pat Dorsey Portfolio Tracking](https://stockcircle.com/portfolio/pat-dorsey) (13F-based)

## Summary of Conversational Themes

Despite limited interview availability, consistent themes emerge across all available conversations:

1. **Systematic thinking** — Dorsey's greatest contribution is making moat analysis teachable and repeatable, not intuitive
2. **Structure over management** — "Bet on the horse, not the jockey"; industry structure determines outcomes more than CEO charisma
3. **Moat durability** — The key question is always: how long can this advantage persist?
4. **Integration of valuation** — Moat analysis alone is insufficient; you must also buy at the right price
5. **Skepticism of "moats"** — Dorsey warns extensively about mistaking temporary advantages for real moats
6. **Patience** — "There are no called strikes in investing"; wait for the right price
7. **Know when to sell** — The four reasons (mistake, change, alternatives, risk) are as important as the buy thesis
