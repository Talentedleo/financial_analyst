# Pat Dorsey — Writings

A comprehensive collection of Pat Dorsey's published works, Morningstar research, and key writings that define his systematic moat analysis framework.

---

## 1. Major Book: "The Little Book That Builds Wealth" (2008)

**Full Title:** *The Little Book That Builds Wealth: The Knockout Formula for Finding Great Investments*
**Published:** 2008 (Wiley)
**Series:** Little Book Big Profits series
**Author:** Pat Dorsey, CFA
**Role at time of writing:** Director of Equity Research, Morningstar, Inc.

**Core Thesis:** Long-term outperformance is achieved by owning businesses with durable **economic moats** — structural characteristics that allow companies to fend off competition and maintain superior profitability. Dorsey systematizes Warren Buffett's moat concept into a rigorous, repeatable analytical framework.

### Key Concepts

#### Mistaken Moats (What Moats Are NOT)
Dorsey identifies four common traps investors mistake for moats:
1. **Great products** — Can be copied or become obsolete
2. **Large market share** — Can be lost to competitors
3. **Great execution** — Operational excellence is not structural
4. **Great management** — "Bet on the horse, not the jockey"; 9 times out of 10, industry structure matters more than management

#### The Five Sources of Economic Moats (5 Sources Framework)

| Moat Source | Description | Key Question | Examples |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| **Intangible Assets** | Brands, patents, regulatory licenses | Does the customer pay a premium? | Coca-Cola (brand), 3M (patent portfolio), Moody's (regulatory license) |
| **Switching Costs** | High cost/time/risk to change providers | Is the customer locked in? | SAP, Oracle, Precision Castparts |
| **Network Effects** | Value increases with more users | Does the network get stronger? | Visa, Mastercard, Microsoft Office |
| **Cost Advantages** | Lower cost structure from scale, process, location | Can competitors replicate the cost advantage? | Walmart (scale), UPS (distribution), Dell (process — historically) |
| **Scale Advantages** | Economies of scale in a niche market | How big is the fish vs. the pond? | Waste Management, niche manufacturers |

> **Key insight:** "Better to have a good poker hand than a good poker player." Some businesses are simply better structurally than others — a poorly managed bank will still be more profitable than a stellar-managed supermarket.

#### Moat Erosion (When Moats Break)
Dorsey dedicates significant attention to how moats can erode:
- **Technological obsolescence** (Kodak film, landline telephones, newspapers)
- **Industry structure change** (big-box retailing, foreign labor outsourcing)
- **Corporate empire building** (Microsoft's Zune, MSNBC, MSN)
- **Competition** — it's only a matter of time

#### Valuation Framework
Dorsey advocates four drivers of business valuation:
1. **Risk** — How likely are future cash flows to materialize?
2. **Growth** — How large will future cash flows likely be?
3. **Capital requirements** — How much investment is needed to sustain the business?
4. **Moat** — How long can the business generate excess profits?

The **moat** determines the duration of profitability — the most important and most often overlooked driver.

#### Selling Discipline
Dorsey recommends selling only for these reasons:
1. **Mistake** — The investment thesis was wrong
2. **Change** — The facts have changed ("When the facts change, I change my mind")
3. **Better alternatives** — A much better opportunity exists
4. **Risk management** — Position has grown too large for the portfolio

### Book Format
- 15 chapters, ~200 pages
- Written in accessible, conversational style (part of the "Little Book" series)
- Uses recognizable company examples throughout
- Target audience: retail active stock investors

## 2. "The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing" (2004)

**Full Title:** *The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing: Morningstar's Guide to Building Wealth and Winning in the Market*
**Published:** November 2003/2004 (Wiley)
**Pages:** 384
**Author:** Pat Dorsey, CFA

A comprehensive guide to stock analysis covering the full investment process. Unlike *The Little Book That Builds Wealth* which focuses narrowly on moats, this book provides the complete analytical toolkit:

### The Five Rules
1. **Do Your Homework** — Thorough fundamental analysis before investing
2. **Find Economic Moats** — Identify sustainable competitive advantages
3. **Have a Margin of Safety** — Buy at a discount to intrinsic value
4. **Hold for the Long Term** — Let compounding work
5. **Know When to Sell** — Follow a disciplined exit framework

The book covers financial statement analysis, industry analysis, valuation methodologies (DCF, multiples), portfolio construction, and specific guidance for different sectors. It serves as the "how-to" manual for implementing the moat framework from his later book.

## 3. Morningstar Moat Rating System

Dorsey played an **integral role** in developing Morningstar's Economic Moat Rating system, which is the institutional embodiment of his framework.

### The Three-Tier Moat Rating
| Rating | Definition | Maintains Excess Returns For |
|--------|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Wide Moat** | Very high confidence | 20+ years |
| **Narrow Moat** | High confidence | 10+ years |
| **None** | No sustainable advantage | — |

### Moat Trend Assessment
Beyond the static rating, Morningstar also evaluates **Moat Trend**:
- **Positive** — Moat is strengthening
- **Stable** — Moat is stable
- **Negative** — Moat is eroding

### Morningstar Rating for Stocks
Dorsey also contributed to developing the **Morningstar Rating for Stocks** (the star rating system for equities), which combines moat analysis with valuation assessment.

## Summary of Core Intellectual Contributions

1. **Systematization of Moat Analysis:** Dorsey transformed Buffett's intuitive moat concept into a rigorous, teachable framework with clear categories and assessment criteria.

2. **The Five Moat Sources Framework:** A comprehensive taxonomy of competitive advantages — intangible assets, switching costs, network effects, cost advantages, and scale advantages — that can be applied systematically to any business.

3. **Moat vs. Management:** Dorsey's argument that industry structure (moat) matters far more than management quality was a contrarian position at the time, directly opposing the "star manager" narrative prevalent in business schools and media.

4. **Integration of Moat into Valuation:** Dorsey established moat as the fourth critical dimension of valuation (alongside risk, growth, and capital requirements), arguing that moat determines the **duration of profitability** — the most important variable for long-term compounding.

5. **Institutional Application:** Through Morningstar's Moat Rating system, Dorsey's framework became the industry standard for institutional competitive advantage analysis, applied to thousands of companies globally.
